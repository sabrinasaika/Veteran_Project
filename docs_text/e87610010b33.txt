A. Introduction  
 
In August 2024, the Rehabilitation Services Administration (RSA) conducted a targeted off-site review 
of the Idaho Division of Vocational Rehabilitation’s (IDVR) fiscal administration of the State 
Vocational Rehabilitation Services (VR) program. RSA’s continued concerns with IDVR’s fiscal 
administration of the VR program since the designation of the State as a high-risk grantee for purposes 
of the VR program and the imposition of specific conditions on May 3, 2024, necessitated the offsite 
review.  
 
During the offsite review, RSA assessed IDVR’s fiscal accountability of the VR program to ensure that: 
obligations are incurred, and expenditures are paid within the proper period of performance for each 
grant award; funds are used only for intended purposes; the State is satisfying its match and maintenance 
of effort (MOE) requirements under the VR program; and the agency has internal controls and reporting 
systems that satisfy Federal requirements. RSA conducted the offsite review through videoconference 
calls conducted between August 1 and August 8, 2024. The reviews were conducted in accordance with 
the Federal fiscal year (FFY) 2024 Monitoring and Technical Assistance Guide (MTAG) and with the 
requirements of section 107 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (Rehabilitation Act). IDVR was provided 
a copy of the draft report in accordance with the MTAG. IDVR accepted the draft report without 
comment.  
 
This report consists of the following sections: Part B: Background Facts Necessitating the Offsite 
Review; Part C: Overview of the Offsite Review; Part D: Analysis of Findings; Part E: Corrective 
Actions; and Part F: Conclusion.  
 
B. Background Facts Necessitating the Offsite Review  
 
On May 3, 2024, RSA issued a letter to IDVR designating the agency a high-risk grantee for purposes of 
the VR program, in accordance with 2 C.F.R. § 3474.10, and imposing specific conditions on the 
agency’s FFY 2024 VR award in accordance with 2 C.F.R. § 200.208. These actions were necessitated 
by RSA’s significant concerns regarding the current ability of IDVR to ensure appropriate financial 
accountability of the Idaho VR program in accordance with Federal requirements in title I of the 
Rehabilitation Act and 34 C.F.R. part 361, as well as those applicable to all Federal grantees in 2 C.F.R. 
part 200. RSA designated IDVR a high-risk grantee and imposed specific conditions because the agency 
had failed to implement a financial and data management system that meets Federal requirements at 34 
C.F.R. §§ 361.12 and 76.702 and at 2 C.F.R. §§ 200.302, 200.303, and 200.305. Specifically, IDVR was 
unable to track and report the expenditure of Federal award funds in a manner that permitted RSA to 
assess the agency’s compliance with Federal fiscal requirements and the allowability of costs charged to 
the Federal award and State funds used as non-Federal share, thereby, affecting RSA’s ability to protect 
the Federal interest. Without accurate Vocational Rehabilitation Financial Reports (RSA-17s) from 
IDVR, RSA cannot determine whether the State has satisfied the match and MOE requirements of 
Sections 19 and 111(a) of the Rehabilitation Act and 34 C.F.R. §§ 361.60, 361.62, and 361.64, because 
RSA relies on the financial data submitted on those reports to make these determinations. In addition, 
without accurate RSA-17s, RSA cannot be certain whether IDVR has satisfied other recordkeeping and

of the Cash Management Improvement Act of 1990 (CMIA).

C.  Overview of the Offsite Review 
 
Considering these facts, the purpose of RSA’s offsite review of IDVR was to: 
 
1. assess, to the extent possible, the amount of over-obligations incurred by IDVR and the period of 
performance during which those over-obligations were incurred; 
2. identify the root cause of the over-obligations; 
3. identify the significance of the risk of the over-obligations to Federal funds; 
4. assess IDVR’s ability to provide accurate financial data; 
5. determine the accuracy of Federal financial reports submitted to RSA; and  
6. identify any potential risks to the provision of ongoing services to VR participants.

D.  Analysis of Findings 
 
As a recipient of Federal VR funds, IDVR must implement procedures that ensure proper and efficient 
administration of its VR program and enable IDVR to carry out all required functions, including 
financial reporting. The methods of administration must ensure accurate data collection and financial 
accountability (34 C.F.R. § 361.12 and 2 C.F.R. § 200.302). Among these requirements, IDVR must be 
able to submit timely and accurate financial reports (i.e., RSA-17s), as required by 34 C.F.R. § 361.12, 2 
C.F.R. §§ 200.302, 200.303, and 200.328. 
 
1. Failure to Maintain Internal Controls 
 
Each non-Federal entity must establish and maintain effective internal control over the Federal 
award that provides reasonable assurance that the non-Federal entity is managing the Federal 
award in compliance with Federal statues, regulations, and the terms and conditions of the 
Federal award (2 C.F.R. § 200.303). Internal controls mean a process, implemented by the 
grantee, designed to provide reasonable assurance regarding the achievement of objectives in the 
effectiveness and efficiency of operations, reliability of reporting for internal and external use, 
and compliance with applicable laws and regulations (2 C.F.R. § 200.1). A grantee must take 
prompt action when instances of noncompliance are identified, including noncompliance 
identified in audit findings (2 C.F.R. § 200.303(d)). 
 
Internal controls serve to safeguard assets and prevent fraud, waste, abuse, improper payments, 
and mismanagement. They include methods and procedures the grantee uses to manage the day-
to-day operations of grant-supported activities to assure compliance with applicable Federal 
requirements and that performance goals are being achieved. The VR agency’s internal controls 
for ensuring compliance with fiscal requirements are a core component of the financial 
management system review, particularly as they relate to the VR agency’s use of Federal funds, 
and the requirements for non-Federal match.  
 
From the time RSA designated IDVR a high risk grantee on May 3, 2024, and began the offsite 
review on August 1, 2024, RSA met biweekly with the Interim IDVR director and fiscal staff to 
ascertain the full scope of the fiscal deficit facing the VR program and its root cause. With each 
meeting, it became clear to RSA that the issues IDVR faces are complex, but, at their core, stem 
from internal controls and financial management system processes that do not satisfy Federal 
requirements. 
 
Specifically, RSA learned that the Case Management System used by IDVR for program and 
service authorization data management purposes does not “talk with” the State’s accounting

8 
system. In other words, authorizations for services in one system were not reflected as 
obligations or expenditures in the other system. As a result, IDVR continued to authorize for the 
provision of services because the account seemed to suggest funds were available when, in fact 
those funds had previously been obligated with other authorizations. Although it was 
problematic that the two accounting systems did not “talk with” each other, the fundamental 
internal controls deficiency was that IDVR did not have a process in place to reconcile the 
authorizations and obligations occurring in both accounts on a regular basis (e.g., bi-weekly or 
monthly) so that it could ensure the accounts were accurate and thus the fiscal reports would be 
accurate, as required by 34 C.F.R. § 361.12 and 2 C.F.R. § 200.302. Had it engaged in regular 
reconciliations of the accounts, as it should have done pursuant to 2 C.F.R. § 200.303(d), IDVR 
would have been able to take prompt action to remedy the accounting errors and thus avoid the 
over-obligations of millions of dollars leading to the agency reaching its spending limit by 
September 2024 -- just a little over two months into the start of SFY 2025. Because the agency 
did not, IDVR faced a significant financial crisis jeopardizing services to thousands of Idahoans 
with disabilities.   
 
Once RSA identified the root cause of IDVR’s financial crisis (i.e., the lack of reconciliations 
between the IDVR and State accounting systems), RSA focused most of its efforts during the 
offsite review on evaluating IDVR’s fiscal management system. Specifically, RSA evaluated 
IDVR’s process for complying with Federal period of performance requirements to determine 
whether the agency assigns VR program obligations and expenditures to the correct VR grant 
award. Period of performance is the time during which the non-Federal entity (grantee) may 
incur new obligations to carry out the work authorized under the Federal award (i.e., the time 
interval between the start of the VR grant period (October 1) and the planned end date for that 
grant award (September 30)) (2 C.F.R. § 200.1). For expenditures to be allowable under a 
particular VR grant award, IDVR must demonstrate that the obligation occurred within the 
period of performance of that award (34 C.F.R. §§ 76.702 and 76.703). A “financial obligation,” 
as defined at 2 C.F.R. § 200.1, refers to orders placed for products and services, contracts made, 
and similar transactions that require payment. The Education Department General 
Administrative Regulations at 34 C.F.R. § 76.707 describe when an obligation is made. 
Therefore, to properly account for and liquidate expenditures, IDVR must be able to assign an 
obligation to a Federal VR grant award based on the date the obligation was made (34 C.F.R. §§ 
76.702, 76.703, 76.707, and 76.709).  
 
2.   Insufficient Financial Management System; Failure to Account for and Report Obligations and 
Expenditures Properly  
 
In this section of the report, RSA highlights specific deficiencies noted with IDVR’s financial 
management system, highlighting for the agency those aspects of the system that must be 
remedied to bring the financial management system into compliance with Federal requirements, 
particularly with respect to ensuring that the agency expends funds in the correct period of 
performance for each grant award. RSA’s review of IDVR’s CMS identified several areas where 
the processing of consumer service authorizations and invoices is inconsistent with Federal 
requirements related to period of performance and the accounting and reporting of obligations 
and expenditures:

9 
a. IDVR’s CMS permits the backdating of service authorizations without controls to ensure the 
proper assignment of those authorizations to the correct FFY. The report of open 
authorizations produced from the CMS found 323 authorizations where the service begin 
date was before the authorization date. This indicates an established practice of VR staff 
backdating authorizations in the CMS after services have begun. This practice is inconsistent 
with IDVR’s Vocational Rehabilitation Assistant (VRA) Desktop Manual which states “the 
begin date must not precede the start date of the service or current date.” 
 
Authorizations represent an agreement between the VR agency and the vendor, ensuring the 
correct service is provided to the correct consumer in accordance with Federal, State, and 
agency requirements. The authorization serves to protect the consumer, agency, and vendor 
by ensuring the terms of the transaction are clearly defined. It also protects Federal and State 
funds by preventing fraudulent transactions. The practice of back dating these authorizations 
not only could lead to fraud, but it also contributes to IDVR’s inability to track expenditures 
to the proper period of performance discussed previously. In the event an authorization must 
be backdated in accordance with the agency’s exception process for consumer emergencies, 
the agency must ensure that the start date of the services is within the period of performance 
for the Federal award to which the service is obligated or paid.  
 
b. IDVR’s CMS does not assign obligations or invoices for service authorizations to a FFY but 
is configured only to account for case service expenditures on a SFY basis. Federal 
regulations at 2 C.F.R. § 200.302 require that IDVR’s financial management system must be 
sufficient to permit the preparation of reports required by general and program-specific terms 
and conditions of the grant award. For purposes of the VR program, all fiscal data are 
reported on a FFY basis (see DCL-23-03 for RSA-17 fiscal reporting instructions). 
Therefore, to satisfy government-wide financial management requirements, IDVR must be 
able to adapt its SFY accounting system to account for obligations and invoices on a FFY 
basis as well so that these obligations and expenditures can be traced and reported to a level 
that ensures the funds were used in accordance with the requirements of the VR program (34 
C.F.R. § 361.12 and 2 C.F.R. § 200.302). In other words, the expenditures reported on the 
RSA-17 must be those that can be traced as having been incurred within that grant award’s 
period of performance.  
 
Without the proper assignment of obligations and invoices to a FFY, IDVR is unable to 
accurately report the total amount of State and Federal obligations (both liquidated and 
unliquidated) on the RSA-17 for each VR grant award. Rather, IDVR simply reports the 
liquidation of expenditures in a given grant year, regardless of when the obligation was 
actually incurred. As a result, IDVR is paying for costs that are outside of the period of 
performance for a particular VR grant award, which leads to questioned costs. When RSA 
pursued this further with IDVR during the offsite review and requested supporting 
documentation for sample obligations and expenditures to ensure they were incurred during 
the proper period of performance for a VR grant award, IDVR was unable to provide 
supporting documentation that reconciled to the amounts reported by IDVR. To the extent 
IDVR reported any unsupported non-Federal expenditures and obligations on its RSA-17 for 
match purposes, such amounts could lead to determinations that IDVR has not satisfied its

10 
match and MOE requirements for those FFYs. These determinations, in turn, could lead to 
further enforcement actions (i.e., recovery of funds or reduction of awards), as appropriate. 
 
c. IDVR’s CMS does not account properly for remaining balances on obligations and 
obligations cancelled after they have been reported on IDVR’s fourth quarter RSA-17 report 
as an unliquidated obligation. As stated in DCL-23-03, the instructions for the RSA-17:  
 
all non-Federal expenditures and obligations used for match purposes must be incurred 
during the FFY of appropriation. During the carryover year, the awardee must liquidate 
all unliquidated obligations reported on line 29 as having been incurred by the end of the 
FFY of appropriation but not liquidated by that time. All unliquidated obligations 
reported on line 29 for match purposes, incurred prior to the end of the FFY of 
appropriation, must be liquidated by the end of the liquidation period for that award (i.e., 
120 days after the end of the 8
th quarter (period of performance)). However, we recognize 
unliquidated obligations sometimes fall through during the carryover year and must be 
cancelled or deobligated. When this occurs, the awardee can no longer count those 
obligations toward satisfying its match requirement. 
  
When IDVR has a remaining balance on an authorization that is closed or when an 
authorization is cancelled, the CMS returns those funds to IDVR‘s budget. There is no 
accounting for whether the initial obligations were reported as unobligated match on IDVR’s 
fourth quarter RSA-17 report. Consequently, to the extent these cancelled non-Federal 
unliquidated obligations remained on the RSA-17 for purposes of satisfying the State’s match 
and MOE requirements, such amounts could lead to RSA determining that the State did not 
satisfy its match and MOE requirements for those FFYs.  Should RSA make such a 
determination, RSA may need to take further enforcement action.  
 
d. According to IDVR staff, the “end date of services” in the CMS can be either 1) the date 
through which the authorized services are to be provided or 2) the date the service invoice 
was received. However, the “service end date” should be only the date through which the 
authorized services are to be provided, thereby providing a mechanism in the CMS for 
ensuring that the services are provided during the period of performance for the grant award 
from which the invoices are paid. During the offsite review, IDVR stated it often receives 
delayed invoices from providers; therefore, to the extent invoices are delayed from a prior 
FFY and the “end date of services” is coded as the date the invoice is received, IDVR is 
likely paying for services that were not incurred during the period of performance of the 
particular VR grant award from which the payment is made. To the extent the “service end 
date” represents the invoice receipt date, IDVR has no mechanism for determining when the 
services were provided in the CMS. This makes it impossible for IDVR to account for and 
report these expenditures and obligations, with accuracy, for the proper period of 
performance for each VR grant award, as it is required to do pursuant to 34 C.F.R. § 361.12 
and 2 C.F.R. § 200.302.

references to proper assignment of obligations and expenditures to the correct period of

The VRA Desktop Manual indicates that contracts should not stop and start to accommodate a

constitutes a valid obligation, as established by 34 C.F.R. § 76.707. Consequently, IDVR 
must ensure that all services provided under a service authorization that crosses Federal fiscal 
years are assigned to and paid from the appropriate Federal award.  
 
As the above demonstrates, RSA has found that IDVR’s fiscal management system failed to 
satisfy Federal fiscal management requirements for ensuring the proper expenditure of VR 
program funds within the periods of performance for at least FFYs 2023 and 2024. 
 
3. Inaccurate and Unsupported Federal Financial Reporting 
 
In addition to the deficiencies noted above with the agency’s financial management system, RSA 
noted deficiencies with IDVR’s financial reporting – all of which should have been caught and 
remedied if IDVR had proper internal controls in place in accordance with 2 C.F.R. § 200.303. 
In this section, RSA highlights the financial reporting deficiencies noted during the offsite 
review.   
 
As a recipient of Federal VR program funds, IDVR must have procedures that ensure the proper 
and efficient administration of its VR program and enable IDVR to carry out all required 
functions, including financial reporting (34 C.F.R. § 361.12). In accordance with the Uniform 
Guidance in 2 C.F.R. § 200.302(a), a State’s financial management systems, including records 
documenting compliance with Federal statutes, regulations, and the terms and conditions of the 
award, must be sufficient to permit the preparation of reports required by general and program 
specific terms and conditions; and the tracing of funds to a level of expenditures adequate to 
establish that such funds have been used according to the Federal statutes, regulations, and the 
terms and conditions of the Federal award.

C.F.R. § 76.702 requires States to use fiscal control and fund accounting procedures that ensure 
proper disbursement of and accounting for Federal funds.

RSA compared revenue reported in the RSA-17 reports for FFYs 2023 and 2024, the G5 
drawdowns, and VR program financial data recorded in IDVR’s general ledger (GL). The 
results indicate significant material discrepancies between the GL, G5 draws, and RSA-17 
fiscal data reported for FFY 2023. In a monitoring call on August 8, 2024, IDVR stated the 
financial data reported on the GL and in the FFRs cannot be relied upon. Specifically, 
because the CMS and State accounting system are configured to only track funds on a SFY 
basis, there is no process for assigning the individual transactions to the appropriate FFY for 
a Federal award. The systems, as configured, lack a place for the Federal fiscal year 
associated with the transaction to be recorded.  
 
RSA’s review of the accuracy and reliability of IDVR’s financial reporting identified 
significant material deficiencies. The financial reports did not adhere to financial reporting 
requirements, represent an accurate accounting of IDVR’s use of Federal and non-Federal 
funds for the VR program, and enable RSA to determine IDVR’s compliance with Federal 
VR financial requirements. 
 
E.  Corrective Actions 
 
In RSA’s high risk designation letter to IDVR on May 3, 2024, RSA stated that to ensure continued 
long-term compliance with Federal requirements, IDVR must submit a detailed corrective action plan 
(CAP) to address the fiscal and accountability deficiencies, including specific action steps and 
measurable timelines. The CAP IDVR submitted, dated June 17, 2024, contained only generalized 
goals, not specific action steps or measurable timelines. 
 
Through RSA’s fiscal review, RSA learned substantially more about IDVR’s fiscal operations and 
processes. This enabled RSA to more clearly outline the outcomes that must be achieved for IDVR to be

14 
successful in correcting the identified issues. The completion of IDVR’s CAP will be measured by 
IDVR’s ability to demonstrate: 
 
1. The use of proper internal controls processes sufficient to safeguard against accounting errors or 
payments being made for costs that are either not allowable or outside of the period of 
performance. 
 
2. The collection and analysis of appropriate and accurate financial data under the VR program. 
This means that IDVR’s financial and data management systems must be able to: 
a. assign allowable VR expenditures and unliquidated obligations only to the FFY in which the 
expenditure was made or obligation was incurred; 
b. distinguish liquidated expenditures from unliquidated obligations; 
c. identify which source of funds (Federal or non-Federal) is used to cover the liquidation or 
obligation of allowable VR expenditures; 
d. determine when obligations under the VR program are liquidated; 
e. ensure all obligations incurred under the VR program are liquidated within 120 days after the 
end of the period of performance; and 
f. make necessary accounting adjustments when non-Federal obligations, previously reported 
for match and MOE purposes under the VR program, are cancelled or otherwise not 
liquidated, to remove those obligations from the amount IDVR reported to RSA. 
 
3. The timely and accurate reporting of Federal and non-Federal expenditures and unliquidated 
obligations under the VR program. To ensure the financial reports are accurate and timely, IDVR 
must: 
a. report only those non-Federal expenditures and unliquidated obligations, used to satisfy 
match and MOE requirements under the VR program, that were incurred by September 30 of 
the FFY of appropriation with non-Federal funds that were available at that time; 
b. carry over into the subsequent FFY only those Federal funds that were matched by non-
Federal expenditures or obligations incurred by September 30 of the FFY of appropriation; 
and 
c. have processes that ensure the timely submission of accurate financial reports. 
 
4. The retention of, and access to, financial records, supporting documents, and other agency 
records pertinent to a Federal award, as required by 2 C.F.R. §§ 200.333 and 200.336. This 
includes maintaining supporting documentation for RSA-17s submitted by IDVR for the VR 
program. 
 
RSA will provide technical assistance to IDVR to review its current CAP to ensure the steps necessary 
to meet the requirements above are included. To minimize the reporting and tracking burden for IDVR 
and RSA, RSA intends for the initial CAP to be updated to include the requirements noted above and the 
specifics steps below. 
 
1. Implementation of the Approved Corrective Actions: 
 
a. IDVR must develop interim processes to ensure that it can submit timely and accurate 
financial reports for its FFY 2025 VR award. These processes must be reviewed by RSA to

15 
ensure they have a high likelihood of resulting in compliance with the required reporting 
and program requirements. In particular, the processes must ensure the proper assignment 
of Federal and non-Federal funds to the FFY 2025 award. IDVR must implement these 
processes even if the tracking and reporting is completed outside of the agency’s CMS and 
financial systems due to configuration and system’s issues.  
b. IDVR must develop and implement internal controls and policies. 
 
2. Post-Implementation Reconciliations and Submissions: 
 
a. After implementing its approved financial and internal control policies and processes, IDVR 
must reconcile its Federal financial reports for FFYs 2020 through 2024, using the newly 
approved processes, and resubmit as necessary to ensure that all forms submitted are 
accurate. RSA will use these revised forms to determine whether the State has met its match 
and MOE for each of these FFYs and will take further action, as necessary, to remedy any 
deficits, in accordance with Section 111(a)(2)(B) of the Rehabilitation Act, Part D of the 
General Education Provisions Act (20 U.S.C. §§ 1234a-1234b), and 34 C.F.R. part 81. 
b. On a quarterly basis, submit written updates to RSA regarding progress made toward its 
implementation to satisfy these corrective actions. 
c. On a quarterly basis, submit supporting documentation, from its financial management 
system, for Federal and non-Federal VR expenditures it reports on the Federal financial 
reports, at the time IDVR submits these reports. RSA will use these submissions to verify the 
accuracy of the submitted financial reports as a means of verifying that IDVR’s 
implementation of its financial and data management systems comply with Federal 
requirements. 
 
If IDVR fails to satisfy these corrective actions, RSA may take additional enforcement actions in 
accordance with 34 C.F.R. part 81 and 2 C.F.R. § 200.208. 
 
F. Conclusion 
 
RSA identified significant concerns due to the lack of procedures and internal controls related to the 
assignment and reporting of obligations and expenditures to the proper Federal award and non-Federal 
funding sources. RSA found that IDVR is not satisfying Federal requirements to account for expending 
funds within the appropriate period of performance for Federal grant awards, including obligating and 
assigning costs to the proper FFY award based on period of availability, as described in 34 C.F.R. § 
76.707, and expenditures are not being assigned appropriately or monitored for compliance. IDVR failed 
to implement a financial management system that meets Federal requirements because IDVR is not able 
to ensure: 
 
• Its internal controls and accounting procedures assign and track financial obligations to fund 
sources, including Federal award and non-Federal funds, to ensure the agency fulfills Federal 
requirements for period of performance and the budget period, accurate data collection, and 
financial accountability, as required by 2 C.F.R. §§ 200.1 and 200.303, 34 C.F.R. § 361.12, and 
34 C.F.R. § 76.707; 
• The proper disbursement of and accounting for Federal funds, as required by 34 C.F.R. § 76.702;

16 
• It has expended requisite non-Federal share to meet match and MOE requirements, as required 
by 34 C.F.R. §§ 361.60 and 361.62, and 2 C.F.R. § 200.306; 
• Its financial management system procedures permit preparation of required Federal reports, as 
required by 2 C.F.R. § 200.302(a); and 
• Only allowable costs resulting from obligations of the budget period are charged to the award, as 
required by 2 C.F.R. § 200.1. 
 
As a result of IDVR’s financial deficiencies, the agency over-obligated its funds, thereby jeopardizing 
the delivery of services to individuals with disabilities and nearly shutting down the VR program. 
Therefore, IDVR’s actions make it impossible for RSA to determine whether IDVR has satisfied Federal 
fiscal requirements in a timely manner. RSA is particularly concerned that IDVR lacks the capacity to 
accurately assign, track and report both Federal and non-Federal obligations and expenditures, including 
the extent to which IDVR, and the State of Idaho, has met its match and MOE requirements. The 
financial data reviewed by RSA indicate a substantial shortfall in non-Federal funds available to pay for 
service obligations incurred by IDVR. RSA is concerned about the impact of such a deficit on IDVR’s 
ability to meet current and future non-Federal share requirements and to provide for VR services already 
authorized or included on approved IPEs.

The Issue:
The Parties were not able to agree on a Stipulated Issue. At the hearing the Appellant stated the issue to be whether Gilbert was wrongfully terminated and if so, what are the damages. At the hearing the Respondent stated the issue to be whether Gilbert was properly terminated for non-compliance with the rules.
Appellant does not dispute that she did not submit the required documents that she should have submitted and was required to submit. Instead, Appellant asserts that it was impossible for her to meet the stated requirements because of a lack of training and support.
In its post hearing brief, counsel for Gilbert states that BEP’s failures to train and support its licensees made it impossible for Gilbert to succeed and to cure her acknowledged deficiencies. Gilbert alleges that her termination was improper because BEP’s failure to train and support its vendor licensees made it impossible for her to succeed and cure her acknowledged deficiencies. Thus, her termination was improper, and she should be awarded damages. Counsel further states that BEP set Gilbert up for failure and then terminated her instead of fulfilling its responsibilities to provide her with adequate training and support to operate a successful business. Wherefore, Gilbert requests the Arbitration Panel find that her termination was improper, and award her damages, together with attorney’s fees, costs, and delay damages.
In its post hearing brief, counsel for Respondent frames the issues to be as follows:
Whether BBVS/BEP properly terminated Licensee Gilbert from the B.E.P program for failing to submit her monthly reports and pay the set-aside amounts owed as required by applicable BEP regulations?
Whether Licensee’s allegations concerning actions occurring prior to the termination, which was the sole subject of the underlying fair hearing, are barred where she failed to timely challenge these actions by filing a written request “within fifteen working days of the … occurrence of the action” as required by 55 PA. Code §2430.202?
Whether this Complaint should be dismissed on the basis of laches where a six- year delay since the June 2, 2017, decision and this July 2023 arbitration prejudices the BBVS/BEP’s ability to defend the allegations?
As background, Gilbert was issued a Termination letter revoking her operator’s license issued by the BEP and advising her that she was being dismissed from the program. On March 23, 2016, Gilbert filed an appeal from her dismissal. The matter was assigned to Hearing Officer Jackie Lutz, who conducted hearings over eight separated days. On June 2, Lutz issued her decision, affirming the BEP termination of Gilbert’s license for failure to comply with the regulations and policies in operating her facility. At that point, Gilbert was dismissed from the BEP. In 2018 Gilbert filed a Complaint and Request for Arbitration alleging wrongful termination from BEP. On July 6, 2021, RSA issued an acknowledgement letter indicating that Gilbert’s Complaint had been docked. On July 27, 2021, BEP submitted a Response to the Complaint, requesting dismissal for failure to set forth information and a copy of the determination at issue. Gilbert was wrongly terminated from her Stand and was not entitled to the opportunity to resolve the problems of the delinquency Code a decision was made to dismiss her pursuant to 55 Pa. §2430.182(c).

§2430.182(e).

On August 31, 2021, Carol Dobak, Acting Deputy Commissioner, United States Department of Education Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services Rehabilitation Services Administration authorized the convening of an arbitration panel to hear and render a decision on the issues raised in the complaint filed by Gilbert. Background and Facts
Marina Gilbert, is a blind African American woman who participated in the BEP training run by OVR and the BBVS of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. By letter dated July 15, 2014, BEP issued a bid letter award notifying Gilbert that she had been awarded stands #531 and #532, at the Rachel Carson Building at 400 Market St. Harrisburg PA. Both stands were opened in August 2014.
The Randolph-Sheppard Vending Stand Act, 20 U.S.C. Section 107 et seq., (hereinafter the “Act” or “RSA”) gives blind persons preference to operate vending facilities in federal and other properties. The United States Department of Education’s Rehabilitative Services Administration is charged with administration of the Act. The Act is a federal statute, administered on the state level by State Licensing Agencies (SLAs), who agree to comply with certain requirements set forth in the Act and in the sections of the United States Code and Pennsylvania regulations implementing the Act. In the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, the SLA is the Bureau of Blindness and Visual Services, Office of Vocational Rehabilitation (hereinafter “Respondent”). Through its Business Enterprise Program (BEP), Respondent acts as a facilitator between the owners/operators of federal or other properties and blind vendors trained and licensed by the Commonwealth to operate vending facilities such as snack bars, cafeterias and/or vending machines in those locations. Respondent will typically secure a permit from the owner/ property manager of the location and place the vending site(s) up for bid to all licensed blind vendors in the Commonwealth.
The BEP is intended to empower blind and visually impaired individuals to operate food service facilities in government or federal buildings throughout the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. The program is to give blind and visually impaired individuals the opportunity to become productive members of society through successful and productive entrepreneurship. The training lasts four months. Following BEP training, trainees are then required to complete a practicum in which trainees receive additional training on site at an established stand alongside a seasoned vendor.
BBVS is charged with operation Pennsylvania’s BEP program consistent with the Randolph-Sheppard Act, 20 USCA §§ 107 – 107f; Pennsylvania’s Little Randolph- Sheppard Act, 71 P.S. § § 580.1-580.20; federal and state/commonwealth regulations, and BEP policies.
BEP regulations require time submissions of daily and monthly reports, which are also referred to as Profit and Loss or P&L reports. Said reports are to be submitted by the 10th of each month. Where a licensee fails to comply with this reporting requirement, they will be subject to progressive discipline, up to and including termination. See BEP Exh 1, No.3, BEP Policy No. 28.
Pursuant to completing the required training and practicum, Gilbert received a Qualified Vendor Certificate awarded by BBVS upon the successful completion of the educational and training requirements for the BEP. Following several meetings with BEP business agents who provided additional training and assistance, on October 15, 2015, Gilbert was placed on a Corrective Action Plan that identified multiple issues. On December 15, 2015, Claimant was placed on 90 days’ probation. On March 2, 2016, BEP issued a letter of termination to Gilbert notifying her that she was being dismissed from the BEP program. The Randolph Shepherd Vending Stand Act, 20 USC section 107 et seq., (hereinafter the Act) Gives blind persons preference to operate vending facilities in federal and other properties. Analysis
The evidence reveals that Gilbert and her sisters entered into the BEP program with enthusiasm and conviction. They were convinced that they could make Rina’s Place into a profitable well-run establishment. They did not foresee the obstacles before them. However, by all accounts they addressed these obstacles head on and worked diligently to make the operations a success. All but the reporting requirements.
The question before the panel is whether Appellant was provided the training and support necessary to succeed and whether she was wrongfully terminated because she was not provided the necessary training and support needed.
As found in the Lutz report, and all evidence received into evidence at this arbitration Gilbert was terminated for failing to submit the required monthly reports as required. There is nothing to suggest she was terminated for a failure to adequately run her stands or to make them profitable, other than the failure to submit the required reports.
It is undisputed that on July 2,2014 Gilbert submitted a written bid for facility Nos. 531 BS 532, which consisted of two separate sites in the Rachel Carson Building in Harrisburg, PA. The bid package included a business plan for Rita’s Place which consisted of, inter alia, a marketing plan, a management plan, and a financial plan. In Appellant’s marketing plan, Appellant listed her sister, Karen Meeks, as her Assistant Manager, and represented that Meeks had over 20 years’ experience in the South Carolina BEP Program.
Undisputed evidence received into the record reveals that Appellant failed to comply with the reporting elements required of the program. Indeed, Appellant admits that she did not comply. BEP assigned two agents to assist Gilbert, John Bungo and Nan Bonnet-Foik. Bungo retired from Commonwealth service in 2007 and returned to work in 2014 as an annuitant for 95 days per calendar year. As a Business Agent, Bungo was responsible all BEP clients who have a business and reviews monthly reports submitted by the operators and provides consulting services and advice to those operators.
Bungo worked with Gilbert when she opened her sites and continued to work with her through November 2014. Bungo arranged for the two locations to be professionally cleaned. The two sites also contained out-of-date stock that she could not use. She had to pay the prior licensee for this stock. BEP then provided her with over $14,000.00 worth of inventory and $1500.00 per stand to start.
Bungo testified without dispute that when he discovered these failings, he prepared a cash receipts journal and a cash disbursement journal for Gilbert and Meeks and met them to assist them in recording their daily sales transactions and disbursements. At the same time, he explained to Gilbert and Meeks the purpose of the monthly profit and loss report, and showed them how to prepare the report, providing them with detailed instructions regarding what each line in the report means and where they could obtain the information that needs to be included in the report from their records (BEP Exhibit 9).
Bungo also explained to Gilbert and Meeks how to set up and maintain accounting records to follow good business practices and internal controls. Bungo subsequently met with Gilbert and Meeks in September and October 2014, and using the detailed instructions he provided to them, assisted them in preparing the monthly profit and loss reports for September and October 2014. Meeks acknowledged that Bungo showed her how to complete the profit and loss. Gilbert similarly admitted that Bungo gave her and Meeks a sheet to keep track of their expenses and instructions on what they should be doing every day as far as recording expenses and sales that they made. Yet, despite, Bungo's efforts, Meeks admitted that after October 2014 she did not submit a monthly report until Bungo returned to the BEP as an annuitant in calendar year 2015. It is clear from the evidence received into the record that Gilbert was putting her energy into trying to increase the volume and viability of the sites and not the reporting requirement.
Bungo testified that when he returned to work for the BEP in May 2015, he conducted a site visit at Appellant's stands and learned that no profit and loss statements had been prepared or submitted by Gilbert or Meeks since October 2014, when he last met with them. In addition, no Pennsylvania sales taxes had been remitted to the PA Department of Revenue; no taxes were being withheld for anyone working at the facility and, Appellant had not made a set aside payment to the BEP since September 2014.
The evidence is undisputed that from late May 2015 through mid-June 2015, Bungo again met with Meeks and Gilbert to address these problems. He again helped them to prepare the needed reports. He completed six months of delinquent profit and loss reports for each stand, for the period November 2014 through April 2015. After May 2015, Bungo made regular monthly site visits to the Stands on June 23, 2015, July 23, 2015, August 26, 2015, and September 29, 2015, to evaluate Appellant's progress and compliance with program and/or state, federal or local regulations. During the site visit on June 23, 2015, the profit and loss statements for May 2015, which were due on June 10, 2015, still had not been submitted, and the set aside fees for September and October of the previous years were still not paid. (BEP Exhibit 12)
During a site visit on July 23, 2015, Bungo noted similar discrepancies and concerns. Outstanding set-aside fees for September 2014 through May 2015 were past due; neither stand had a Harrisburg Mercantile License displayed for 2015; and cash purchases and payments were still being made. Bungo again discussed with Gilbert and Meeks corrective actions that needed to be implemented by them. (BEP Exhibit 13).
Meeks testified that she understood after May of2015 that she should be submitting two reports to the BEP every month, one for each stand. She also testified that once Bungo did those six months of reports with her, she understood what she was supposed to do. In fact, the evidence reveals that Appellant submit a monthly profit and loss report within May and June 2015. However, after June of 2015, the reports stopped.
Because of the Appellant's continued lack of compliance with BEP and local and state regulations, Wullbrant made the decision in October 2015 to place Appellant on a corrective action plan. The corrective action plan was provided to Appellant on October 15, 2015, and included dates for Appellant to come into compliance with, inter alia, labor laws, reporting and paying Pennsylvania sales taxes, submitting outstanding profit and loss reports and payment of overdue set aside fees. Appellant was notified that the consequences for non-compliance included probation. (BEP Exhibit 17)
Appellant failed to remedy the problems identified in her corrective action plan. Consequently, on December I, 2015, the Director of BBVS notified Appellant that she was being placed on 90 days' probation, to expire on March I, 2016, due to her delinquent rental payments for Stands Nos. 531 and 532 for the months of October 2014 through May 2015, her failure to submit profit and loss reports for both stands for the months of June 2015, thru October 2015, and her failure to remit sales taxes to the Department of Revenue from her inception into the BEP. Gilbert was notified that if she is not current with her past due rental payments, i.e., set aside fees, by the end of her probationary period, the BEP may take further disciplinary action up to and including removal from the BEP. (BEP Exhibit 6). Appellant failed to comply with the requirements set forth in her corrective plan.
By the end of Appellant's probationary period, her past due set aside fees were still overdue, and she had submitted no additional profit and loss statements to the BEP. Hence, by letter dated March 2, 2016, Appellant was notified that the BEP would be revoking her operator's license with the BBVS and dismissing her from the BEP.
Federal regulations mandate that state licensing agencies shall suspend or terminate a licensee if the vending facility is not being operated in accordance with its rules and regulations. 34 C.F.R. §395.7(b). BEP regulations and BEP Policy #28 place licensees on notice that failure to complete and submit the required profit and loss reports in a timely fashion will result in disciplinary action up to, and including, a written reprimand and probation, or dismissal from the program. 55 Pa. Code §§2430.111 and 2430.171.
Gilbert was also advised of this fact when she was placed on probation. BEP regulations also advise licensees that if rental payments are not made to the BEP within 60 days of issuance of an invoice, the licensee will be placed on 90 days' probation, and that removal action will be taken if all rental payments are not current by the end of the probation period. 55 Pa. Code §2430.182. In addition, OVR/BBVS/BEP is authorized to cancel a contract for failure to timely submit reports and timely pay rental fees. 55 Pa.
Code §2430.50.
That is what occurred here. Despite many opportunities to come into compliance with BEP program regulations, Appellant did not do so. Appellant was first provided with a corrective action plan and given dates to come into compliance with labor laws, reporting and paying Pennsylvania sales taxes, submitting outstanding profit and loss reports, and payment of overdue set aside fees for her Facility. Appellant failed to address these issues. Appellant was then placed on 90 days' probation, but, during her probationary period still failed to address the reporting issues.
All evidence received into evidence evinced that Appellant understood that she needed to be submitting her reports for both stands. BEP, primarily through Bungo provided her with significant training and support for submission of those reports. The evidence demonstrates that the stands are to be operated in accordance with Federal, State/Commonwealth and Local Laws. Records must be kept and submitted. This was not done, and she was removed from the program because the records were not submitted as required. The BEP provided more than sufficient training on how to submit the required records.
Decision
The appeal is denied in its entirety.

Nebraska Commission for the Blind and Visually Impaired
Nebraska Business Enterprises (NBE) Program
Randolph-Sheppard Financial Relief and Restoration Payments (FRRP)
Compensation Plan
INTRODUCTION
The Randolph-Sheppard Act, P.L. 74-732, as amended by P.L. 83-565 and P.L. 93-516 (20 U.S.C. Sec. 107 et seq.) gives licensed blind vendors priority to operate vending facilities on most federal property. Nearly every state has extended the priority to state and municipal property.
The Randolph-Sheppard Program is administered by state vocational rehabilitation (VR) agencies designated as “State Licensing Agencies (SLAs)” under the oversight of the federal U.S. Department of Education (hereafter, “Department”).  The Nebraska Commission for the Blind and Visually Impaired (NCBVI) is the SLA for Nebraska.
Under the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2021, Division H, Title III, Congress directed the Department to make $20 million available for one-time financial relief and restoration grants to SLAs. The funds are to be used to help offset blind vendors’ losses that were incurred in 2020. While the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted the operation of many vending facilities, Congress did not limit the use of restoration funds to the compensation of losses that directly resulted from the pandemic.
The $20 million will be distributed to the SLAs based on the number of licensed blind vendors in the state. Section 318 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act directs the Department to make grants to SLAs in the same proportion as the number of blind vendors operating a vending facility in a State as compared to the number of blind vendors operating a vending facility in all the States on September 30, 2019.
SLAs must use the funds for the following purposes:
To make financial relief and restoration payments to offset losses of blind vendors that occurred during calendar year 2020 and which were not otherwise compensated; and
For other purposes authorized under 34 C.F.R. § 395.9 (regarding the setting aside of funds by the SLA), but only to the extent any funds remain after the SLA makes financial relief and restoration payments to blind vendors to cover their losses incurred in calendar year 2020.
The Department will have the funds available for states until September 30, 2021 and authorizes SLAs to provide financial relief and restoration payments to blind vendors who operated in 2020 and who remain licensed. NCBVI is expected to receive $192,635 for the 14 individuals who were licensed in 2020 and remain licensed.
NCBVI and the Blind Vendor Committee agree that while permissible, NCBVI will not utilize any of the FRRP funds for administrative purposes to maximize the funds to off-set vendor losses.
NCBVI and the Blind Vendor Committee, in a public meeting on May 14, 2021, discussed the options for disbursing the funds, taking into account that vendors’ profit and loss statements reflect income and permit the deduction of lost product when out of the vendor’s control; vendors experienced financial losses in 2020 based upon lost opportunity for increased sales, for additional facilities and for different facilities due to facilities being closed or having fewer customers; the FRRP funds may be used to compensate any loss in 2020 that was out of the control of the vendor and for which vendors may not have records as the opportunity for compensation was unknown at that time; and, that FRRP funds may not be used to compensate a loss for which the vendor has already received compensation.
State Policy:
Vendors who were licensed in 2020 and remain licensed at the time that NCBVI disburses FRRP funds, shall be compensated for the difference between the net profit after set aside (line 18 of Profit and Loss Statement) per month in calendar year 2020, by subtracting the net profit after set aside per month in calendar year 2019 minus any compensation that they certify that they have received for the purpose of off-setting a loss of net profit.  Remaining FRRP funds shall be disbursed equally among the fourteen (14) licensed vendors to compensate for other losses outside the control of the vendor which are not identifiable on the Profit and Loss Statements, subject to the vendor’s signed affidavit that they experienced a loss equal or in excess of their share, and that they have not been otherwise compensated for the loss.

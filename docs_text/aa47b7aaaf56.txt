BACKGROUND, JURISDICTION, AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
This matter stems from a grievance (or complaint) from Dr. Sophia Sparks (Complainant, Petitioner, or the grievant) regarding her dissatisfaction with the operation of the Business Enterprises of Texas (BET) and the Texas Workforce Commission (TWC) (collectively, Respondent, or TWC-BET). Petitioner and Respondent are the parties.
Business Enterprises of Texas helps people who are blind or visually impaired find jobs, and  helps  run  food  services  and  vending  machines  on  different  properties  for  these
Page 1 of 22
individuals. BET is authorized pursuant to the Randolph-Sheppard Act (RS-Act), 20 U.S.C. § 107, et seq., and Texas Labor Code §355.001, et seq., and is a program of the Texas Workforce Commission. Petitioner is a licensed manager in BET, and has brought this complaint pursuant to the RS-Act.
The Texas Workforce Commission (TWC) is the state agency charged with overseeing and providing workforce development services to employers and job seekers of Texas. It does this by building a skilled workforce, focusing on employer needs, and attracting businesses to Texas.
This matter falls under the jurisdiction of the Texas Workforce Commission because it is the state licensing agency (SLA) authorized to implement the RS-Act in the State of Texas, pursuant to 20 U.S.C. §§ 107a(a)(5), 107a(e), and 107b, and it has jurisdiction over BET.
Petitioner alleges that TWC-BET “indirectly discriminated against [Petitioner] and other program participants” and, as a result, violated several regulations “when it awarded a new facility to a blind vendor without first advertising such facility to all program participants.”
To resolve this complaint, the Panel will determine whether Facility 934 was a new facility subject to the regulations concerning the assignment of new facilities, whether TWC-BET indirectly discriminated against Petitioner and others, and other alleged rule violations.
The Administrative Procedures Act provides the standard for this Panel to judge the TWC- BET actions. Those actions should be set aside only if they are found to be arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion, or otherwise not in accordance with law. 5 U.S.C. Section 706(2)(A).
The test in this and other Randolph-Sheppard arbitrations is whether the State acted unreasonably or not in accordance with law. See, 40 TAC § 854.82(e)(5). This standard is "highly deferential, presuming the agency action to be valid and affirming the agency action if a reasonable basis exists for its decision.” See, Ranchers Cattleman Legal Fund United Stockgrowers of America v. U.S. Dept. of Agriculture, 499 F.3d 1108, 1115 (9th Cir. 2007).
Pursuant to its statutory authority, TWC adopted rules and regulations regarding the operation of BET in Title 40 Texas Administrative Code (TAC) Chapter 854. Texas Labor Code, § 355.012(a), authorizes TWC to promulgate rules and initiate procedures necessary to implement Texas Labor Code Chapter 355. 40 TAC § 854.23(a)(2) provides in part:
(a) Career advancement assignments. This section defines the process for the career advancement assignments of managers. It is the goal of the process to provide a fair, unbiased, and impartial process for selection, transfer, and promotion. ***
(2) Notice. As BET facilities become available and ready for permanent assignment, written notice of the availability shall be given to all licensees within 30 business days.

At issue in the March 25, 2022, evidentiary hearing was whether moving Mr. Sifuentes to Stassney Facility 934 was an assignment, a transfer, a promotion, or a relocation. The ECM meeting minutes from January 19, 2019, refer to the TxDOT facility as one of the “pending new facilities.” No mention is made of a manager having been assigned. The ECM meeting minutes from April 13, 2019, refer to the new TxDOT facility as one of the “pending new facilities.” It also states, "[T]his facility will replace the current facility at the TxDOT campus on Riverside," and, “Manager Manny Sifuentes reports looking forward to the TxDOT project and working with a teaming partner.”
On November 24, 2020, Hooks, stated in an email, “This is not a new facility” and “Mr. Sifuentes will be transferring to the new TxDOT campus.” Hanna testified it was a relocation and not a new assignment, stating, “It's not really a career advancement, or an assignment. It's a lateral move.” Hinojosa testified, “It was not a new assignment, it was a relocation,” and, “He was transferring, I don't know that it was an assignment." She further testified that a new facility becomes available when a manager leaves the program or when there is a request for services at a new location. When questioned by the grievant's attorney, Hinojosa agreed that Mr. Sifuentes was assigned to the facility at Stassney when it was ready and available for permanent assignment, and the other BET Managers were not informed when the assignment was made.
The Hearing Officer observed that Hinojosa's communications with BET Managers regarding Stassney in early 2021 resulted in confusion. On January 14, 2021, Hinojosa responded to a question about the TxDOT facility via email by stating, “There will be no advertisement as previously discussed and answered.” It appears the “previous discussion” Hinojosa referred to was Hooks' November 24, 2020 email communication, but that email was not sent to the grievant.
According to the minutes from the January 16, 2021, Elected Committee of Managers meeting, Hinojosa stated, “… once BET gets closer to nearing the end of the project and the opening dates for the projects becomes available, the advertisement discussions will open...” Hinojosa testified that she added, “Not including TxDOT, as that decision has been made years ago.” However, Hinojosa's alleged clarification regarding TxDOT was not included in the minutes. The grievant stated, based on information presented at the January 16, 2021, meeting, that she understood Hinojosa to mean advertisement discussions would open for the TxDOT facility.
After considering the evidence, the Hearing Officer found that the Stassney facility was not a new or different facility than Riverside. It did not “become available for permanent assignment,” nor was there a career advancement in the form of a “selection, transfer or promotion” under 40 TAC § 854.23(a). The Hearing Officer further found that the relocation to Stassney was not a transfer or promotion of Mr. Sifuentes under 34 CFR § 395.14(b)(3). Mr. Sifuentes remained as manager of the facility throughout the relocation process.
Regarding the grievant's requests for relief, the Hearing Officer declined to recommend separating the Stassney facility into two assignments. Only the micro market and coffee bar were operational for the foreseeable future, and anticipated revenue did not justify separating the facility into two assignments. The grievant's alternative request for relief was to be assigned the Fort Worth Naval Air Station facility, for which the grievant had applied. The Hearing Officer did not have jurisdiction to circumvent the selection process for that assignment.

On May 4, 2022 (Respondent Brief at 5 and Respondent Exhibit L), Hearing Officer David Aaron DeSoto recommended a Decision for Dismissal of the operational and equipment grievance. He also stated:
It is my recommendation to Director Fuller that TWC-BET should in the future be required to notify all … licensed managers in the event that any existing facilities are being relocated; that said notification should clearly set forth whether the manager assigned to the existing facility will continue forward as the manager; and if the manager has declined to continue as the manager of that facility, clearly indicate whether the management position is open for applications. This is not a recommendation under 40 TAC § 854.82(15) and does not imply the finding of a violation.
On June 3, 2022, TWC VRD Director Cheryl Fuller adopted the Hearing Officer’s recommendations and dismissed the grievance. Pursuant to 40 TAC § 854.82, the standard of review was whether the Agency's actions were unreasonable, violated applicable law, the requirements of the BET manual, or any instruction by Agency personnel.
Director Fuller found that the evidence did not establish that the Agency violated the law, BET requirements, or acted unreasonably in the Facility 934 relocation process. Stassney did not “become available for permanent assignment,” nor was there a career advancement in the form of a “selection, transfer or promotion” under 40 TAC § 854.23(a). The relocation to Stassney was not a transfer or promotion of Mr. Sifuentes under 34 CFR § 395.14(b)(3). The ECM Chairman was informed of the Agency's decision to relocate Mr. Sifuentes to Stassney, and there was no evidence that he disagreed with the decision.
Director Fuller stated that, in the future, BET Managers will be notified via both email and the TWC external website of facility relocations. The notification will set forth whether the manager assigned to an existing facility will relocate with the facility and continue as manager. If the manager declines to relocate with the facility and continue as manager of the facility, the notification will indicate whether the management position is open for applications.

Petitioner contends that BET violated State and Federal laws in its operational and equipment decisions made in connection with TxDOT Facility 934. She maintains that the Stassney Lane facility should have been advertised, and she cites 40 TAC 854.23. She explains the classification of the Stassney Lane building as an existing facility, and she claims that inconsistent information was communicated to managers, both by practice and policy.

Petitioner cites the March 25, 2022 evidentiary hearing before Impartial Hearing Officer David Aaron DeSoto. She states that witnesses were identified, and exhibits were submitted as evidence. She explains that both the grievant and TWC counsel were present, that judicial notice was taken of the contents of the grievant’s exhibits, and that TWC did not offer any exhibits into evidence.
Petitioner states that absent from testimony and events were former director Michael Hooks and Nick Nejad. She asserts, however, that documents introduced in the case reflect that these individuals were intimately involved in the planning of the Facility.
Petitioner states that the Director, Lizet Hinojosa, and Petitioner, were not part of the BET program at the critical point in time when the TxDOT facility was in the early stages of development. She states that individuals' testimonies essential to understanding the events are Ms. Cook, Mr. Sifuentes, and Mr. Hanna.
Petitioner agrees that the Riverside Drive facility was the original building with ID number 934. She states that TxDOT Staff from Riverside Drive, Camp Hubbard campuses, its print shop, and warehouse were moved to a new building at Stassney Lane, and that the Camp Hubbard BET facility (the cafeteria) nor the manager relocated to Stassney Lane.
Petitioner cites coordination between TWC and TxDOT. She states that the minutes recount the testimony of Mr. Hanna, that he and Nick Nejad met with TxDOT in June 2018, where he learned that the staff would be moving to a newly built building. FINDINGS OF FACT:
The RS-Act was created to provide economic opportunities for the blind on federal and other property. Those economic opportunities are found in the management of food service businesses. These businesses are referred to as facilities.
The RS-Act defines a facility as the equipment necessary for the sale of articles and services. 20 U.S.C. 107e(7). The facility is the business, not the site on which the business is located. 20 U.S.C. 107a(d)(3)
TWC is the State Licensing Agency (SLA) authorized by the Department of Education to implement the RS-Act in the State of Texas.
The Texas Labor Code provides the Texas statutory authority for the Business Enterprises of Texas (BET) and the operation of BET facilities on State property.
Manny Sifuentes, the licensed blind manager of Facility 934, managed the food services facility at a TxDOT property at Riverside Drive (Riverside).
In 2017, TxDOT did not renew its lease at Riverside and decided to relocate the TxDOT employees of Riverside and Camp Hubbard to a newly constructed building at 6230 Stassney Lane (Stassney).
The TxDOT employees at Camp Hubbard were going to be replaced by additional Texas Department of Motor Vehicle employees, which meant that the manager at Camp Hubbard had just as many customers or more.
Section 355.008 of the Texas labor Code prohibits the closure of BET facilities “as a result of the transfer of state property from one agency to another, the alteration of a state building or the reorganization of a state agency unless [TWC] agrees to the closing.”
The BET rules approved by DOE reinforce the statutory reluctance to close a facility and the requirement that TWC approve such a closure by providing that “no BET facility shall be closed until” both of the following have occurred:
The BET director has certified to the VRD director that the facility is no longer a feasible or viable BET facility and provides reasons for that opinion.
The VRD director has approved the proposed closing of the facility.
Page 17 of 22
The BET director did not determine that Facility 934 would not be feasible or viable upon its relocation to the Stassney location serving the same TxDOT employees it had served in the Riverside location.
In deciding to preserve Facility 934 when the facility was relocated from one TxDOT property to another, the BET director followed the long-standing practice of choosing preservation of a facility during a relocation over closing a facility if it were feasible to do so. Tr. 41:11-43:2; 143:21-144:3.
The record does not contain any evidence that it was not feasible to maintain Facility 934 in the Stassney location.
Petitioner and her ECM subcommittee initially did not disagree with this decision. Tr. 124:18- 126:7
It was only after Petitioner later learned another BET manager, Mr. Cantu, had his facility closed with the closing of the Hobby building, that she disagreed with the preservation of Facility 934.
Mr. Cantu did not file a grievance about the closing of his facility.
Unlike Facility 934, customers from numerous facilities in numerous buildings in addition to the Hobby Building were relocated to two different properties: 1801 and 1601 Congress Ave. Respondent’s Ex. I, Tr. 152:20-154:22
The customers from the Hobby building were dispersed among the two new buildings and combined with new customers from other agencies. Petitioner Ex. B
Unlike Facility 934, multiple BET managers in multiple buildings, in addition to Mr. Cantu, lost facilities and/or customers in the relocation of State employees from numerous agencies to the two new buildings on Congress Avenue. Tr. 154:3-22.
DISCUSSION AND OPINION:
After careful review of the facts and arguments, the Panel finds that Respondent did not violate the Randolph Sheppard Act and its implementing regulations when it decided not to close Facility 934 and not to open a new facility for advertisement. The Texas Department of Transportation relocated all of Facility 934’s customers to a newly constructed State Property under existing rules. The rules, created with the active participation of the ECM, grant the Director the authority and discretion to open, and to institute the closing of, BET facilities.
Page 18 of 22
The Panel finds that Petitioner did not meet the burden of proof to show that Respondent violated any cited regulation, or acted unreasonably, in deciding not to close Facility 934. Further, it finds that Respondent did not discriminate against Petitioner in its decision not to close Mr. Sifuentes’ Facility 934, when that facility was transferred from one TxDOT building to another. Respondent did not ex post facto discriminate against Petitioner in its decision not to close Facility 934 when it later closed the facility of Mr. Cantu.
The Panel finds that Director Hooks exercised his authority under BET rules not to close Facility 934, and instead to relocate Facility 934 to the new TxDOT building, which is where all of Facility 934’s customers relocated. Although the rules provided the Director with that authority, Hooks still involved the ECM Chair in the decision and process for relocating Facility 934.
The ECM Chair further advised the ECM of the decision, and the relocation proceeded without formal objection of the ECM. Therefore, the Panel finds that TWC-BET did not violate the RS-Act, the implementing regulations, or its own promulgated rules in its decision to relocate, rather than to close, Facility 934.
Facility 934 was not a new facility; it was an existing facility.
Manny Sifuentes, a licensed manager, was managing Facility 934, providing food services, when the decision to relocate Facility 934 was made. 40 TAC 83(b), entitled "Closing facilities" is the rule on what would have been required to close Facility 934 at this time. Pursuant to this rule, “no facility shall be closed by the Agency until both of the following have occurred:
The BET Director has certified to the VRD director that the facility is no longer a feasible or viable BET facility and provides reasons for that opinion.
The VRD director has approved the proposed closing of the facility.”
As neither of these actions occurred, as a matter of law, Facility 934 never was closed. Instead, Facility 934 was preserved and relocated to the Stassney building as an existing facility.
TWC Did not discriminate against Petitioner in its decision to preserve Facility 934.
The allegation of discrimination goes to the reasonableness of the TWC decision not to close Facility 934. Petitioner alleged in the hearing that another manager, Mr. Cantu, was subsequently treated differently in a decision under 40 TAC 83(b), to close his facility.
The Panel finds that the process and decision not to close Facility 934 were consistent with years of precedent, followed the law to the letter and were not even alleged, much less proven, to deviate unreasonably from the regulation governing closing facilities at the time it was made.
Page 19 of 22
Nothing about the decision to preserve Facility 934 troubled Petitioner at the time it was made. The decision to preserve Facility 934 cannot later become unreasonable ex post facto even if Mr. Cantu subsequently had been treated differently than everyone before him. Moreover, Mr. Cantu was not treated unfairly. TWC explained why Mr. Cantu’s facility decision was different and the factors which led to the decision that it was not feasible to preserve Mr. Cantu’s facility.
Mr. Sifuentes’ Facility 934 and its customers relocated as a single facility from Riverside to Stassney. Mr. Cantu’s customers from the Hobby building along with numerous other managers who also had BET facilities in other buildings had their customers relocated and split between two BET facilities located on Congress Ave. as part of a large State office reorganization.
Facility 934 was the only facility in the Riverside building and became the only facility in the new Stassney building. The number of managers losing customers and facilities as part of the State office reorganization, which included the Hobby building, greatly exceeded the number (2) of new facilities located on Congress Ave.
The Panel finds that the TWC decision that it was not feasible to preserve only one manager’s facility consisting of a new combination of many other managers’ customers was reasonable. Quite simply, a decision that benefited only one manager would not be fair to all the other managers who also lost customers or facilities to the new facilities because of the mass State reorganization. TWC treated all the unemployed or underemployed managers affected by the mass State reorganization uniformly by creating two new facilities for which any manager could compete fairly. This was not discriminatory.
There Was No Other Violation of Law
TWC did not violate the definition of a “single location facility” found in 34 CFR 395.1(h)
This regulation defines what a single location of a facility is. The RS-Act defines a “facility” as the equipment necessary for the sale of articles and services. 20 USC 107e(7). A facility is not defined as the building or land where the facility is located. To the contrary, the building where a facility is located is referred to as the site for the operation of the vending facility. 20 USC 107a(d)(3). The CFR cited by Petitioner addressing the single “location of a facility” cannot and does not change the statue’s definition of a “facility.” For these reasons, the Panel finds that TWC’s interpretation of “facility” was in accordance with the RS-Act.
Page 20 of 22
TWC did agree to cooperate with the Secretary in applying the RS-Act in a uniform manner as required by 34 CFR 395.3(a)(11)(i).
The agreement to cooperate with the Secretary required by this regulation was proven by the uncontroverted evidence introduced at the hearing that TWC cooperated with the Secretary of Education (DOE) in preparing 40 TAC 854.83 (concerning the opening and closing of facilities) and 40 TAC 854.23(a)(11)(i) (concerning the advertising of new facilities). Those rules provide how TWC will uniformly apply the RS-Act to the relocation of an existing Facility 934, and the filing of a vacancy at an empty facility.
The Secretary of DOE approved those rules. The rules could not be approved if DOE did not conclude that they would apply the R/SAct in a uniform manner. In fact, the Secretary made comments to TWC to change its proposed rules to better comply with the RS-Act. TWC made those changes. The Panel finds that TWC did not violate its duty to agree to cooperate with the Secretary in applying the RS-Act in a uniform (by the DOE approved rules) manner.
As required by 34 CFR 395.14(b)(3) the State Committee of Blind Vendors (“ECM”) actively participated in the development of 40 TAC 854.23, which is the rule concerning the administration of the transfer and promotion of blind vendors.
The undisputed evidence proved the active participation of the ECM in promulgating the BET rule (40 TAC 854.23) for the administration of the transfer and promotion of blind vendors. Further, this rule was not applicable to existing Facility 934. The requirements of 34 CFR 395.14(b)(3) were not violated by TWC.
The advertising requirements of 40 TAC 854.23 are not applicable to the preservation and relocation of Facility 934 because Facility 934 was not an empty, unassigned facility.
This rule states, in section (a)(1), that the selection, transfer and promotion of managers depends on “the availability of BET facilities.” Available facilities are either new, empty facilities established pursuant to 40 TAC 854.83 or existing, empty facilities without an assigned manager. Facility 934 was an existing, occupied facility which never was closed pursuant to the BET rules at 40 TAC 854.83(b), and Mr. Sifuentes never was removed from his facility for unsatisfactory performance pursuant to 40 TAC 854.81(b)(3). The advertising requirements of 854.23 apply only to the process of assigning a manager to an available, empty facility.
Page 21 of 22
TWC followed the provisions of 40 TAC 854.83 concerning establishing new facilities and closing existing facilities.
40 TAC 854.83(a) was not applicable to the relocation of Facility 934 because this section applies only to the establishment of new facilities, not to the preservation of current facilities. TWC would have violated this section only if it had applied it to an existing facility. Nor did TWC violate the applicable part of this rule, 854.83(b) as discussed above.
AWARD:
The grievance is denied.

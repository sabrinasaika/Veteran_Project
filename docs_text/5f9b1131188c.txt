SECTION 1: THE SCOPE OF THE REVIEW 
A. BACKGROUND 
Section 107 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (Rehabilitation Act), as amended by Title IV of the 
Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA), requires the Commissioner of the 
Rehabilitation Services Administration (RSA) to conduct annual reviews and periodic on-site 
monitoring of programs authorized under Title I of the Rehabilitation Act to determine whether a 
vocational rehabilitation (VR) agency is complying substantially with the provisions of its State 
Plan under Section 101 of the Rehabilitation Act and with the evaluation standards and 
performance indicators established under Section 106 of the Rehabilitation Act subject to the 
performance accountability provisions described in Section 116(b) of WIOA. In addition, the 
Commissioner must assess the degree to which VR agencies are complying with the assurances 
made in the State Plan Supplement for Supported Employment Services under Title VI of the 
Rehabilitation Act. In conducting the annual reviews, RSA must consider, at a minimum, budget 
and financial management data (Section 107(a)(2)(I) of the Rehabilitation Act); and, pursuant to 
Section 107(a)(4)(B) of the Rehabilitation Act, RSA must examine the provision of services, 
including pre-employment transition services. 
RSA works closely with its Federal partners at the U.S. Department of Labor’s Employment and 
Training Administration (ETA) and the U.S. Department of Education’s Office of Career, 
Technical, and Adult Education (OCTAE) to share monitoring and technical assistance activities, 
especially as they relate to the joint provisions under WIOA. Although the VR program is one of 
the six core programs in the workforce development system, it is unique in that State VR 
agencies provide services directly to individuals with disabilities. Thus, the nature and scope of 
RSA’s monitoring process and report may appear different from the monitoring ETA and 
OCTAE conduct with their grantees.  
In Federal fiscal year (FFY) 2024, RSA conducted an on-site monitoring review of the State 
Vocational Rehabilitation Services program (VR program) and the State Supported Employment 
Services program (Supported Employment program) administered by the Utah State Office of 
Rehabilitation (USOR). The nature, scope, and focus of this review and the process by which 
RSA carried out its activities from August 13, 2024, through August 15, 2024, was defined by 
information regarding the VR agency’s use and expenditure of Federal funds, documents, and 
data submitted by USOR, taking into account the goals, unique circumstances, and technical 
assistance needs of the VR agency.  
RSA—  
● Assessed the performance of the VR and Supported Employment programs, including 
how the VR agency is implementing strategies and practices to achieve continuous 
improvement in the delivery of VR services, including pre-employment transition 
services, leading to quality competitive integrated employment and supported 
employment outcomes for individuals with disabilities; 
● Reviewed the VR agency’s financial management of the VR and Supported Employment 
programs, which supports and maximizes the use of Federal funds and resources to

2 
achieve continuous improvement in assisting individuals with disabilities to achieve 
quality employment outcomes; 
● Identified strategies, including innovative practices, the VR agency uses or may employ 
to improve competitive integrated employment outcomes for individuals with disabilities, 
including students and youth with disabilities; 
● Provided recommendations leading to continuous improvement in the number and quality 
of outcomes achieved by individuals with disabilities through the VR and Supported 
Employment programs and recommendations leading to continuous improvement in the 
VR agency’s financial management of fiscal resources and strategies for maximizing the 
use of Federal grant funds;  
● Identified compliance findings and corrective actions to improve processes and 
procedures, including the development or revision of policies and internal controls 
supporting effective and efficient program operations, service delivery, and data-based 
decision making; and  
● Provided technical assistance to assist and support the VR agency in developing strategic 
actions to improve and maximize program and fiscal performance and the use of Federal 
funds.

SECTION 2: FOCUS AREA – PERFORMANCE OF THE STATE 
VOCATIONAL REHABILITATION SERVICES AND STATE SUPPORTED 
EMPLOYMENT SERVICES PROGRAMS  
A. PURPOSE 
Through this focus area, RSA assessed the VR agency’s VR and Supported Employment 
programs’ performance in assisting individuals with disabilities, including those with the most 
significant disabilities, with the necessary services and supports to achieve quality competitive 
integrated employment. During this focused review, RSA analyzed VR program data, policies 
and internal controls, and agency practices in implementing VR services within the context of 
maximizing the use and expenditure of funds to improve program performance. Where 
appropriate, RSA provided technical assistance and identified findings requiring corrective 
actions. RSA also provided recommendations, which the agency may consider as it develops 
strategic actions to improve performance and maximize the use and expenditure of Federal funds 
to achieve quality employment outcomes.  
B. ANALYSIS OF PERFORMANCE
  
RSA’s analysis of the VR agency’s performance of the VR and Supported Employment 
programs incorporated a review of data reported by USOR on the Case Service Report (RSA-
911) and the WIOA Statewide Performance Report (ETA-9169) for program years (PYs) 2020, 
2021, and 2022, as well as the review and discussion of internal controls and policies and 
requested documents. A review of the VR agency profile and data related to the VR process, VR 
services, quality of employment outcomes, and pre-employment transition services, as well as 
discussions with USOR of potential factors influencing program performance informed the 
analysis and helped to identify technical assistance needs and recommendations to improve the 
performance and management of the VR and Supported Employment programs.  
Summary of Factors Influencing Performance 
 
Through the analysis of data and documents provided by the VR agency, information from 
related sources, and discussions with USOR during the review, RSA identified various factors, 
which may be contributing either positively or negatively to USOR’s performance leading to 
employment outcomes for individuals with disabilities and the use and expenditure of Federal 
funds. In addition to providing technical assistance, as appropriate and in response to USOR’s 
requests, RSA also developed recommendations for consideration by the VR agency as it 
develops strategic actions to ensure continuous improvement in program performance and to 
maximize the use and expenditure of available Federal funds to assist individuals with 
disabilities in achieving quality employment outcomes. 
 
Supported Employment: Provision of Extended Services 
 
As described in 34 C.F.R. § 363.22, a State that receives an allotment under the State Supported 
Employment Services program must reserve and expend 50 percent of such allotment for the 
provision of supported employment services, including extended services, to youth with the most

4 
significant disabilities to assist those youth in achieving an employment outcome in supported 
employment. Although USOR stated it provided extended services to youth with the most 
significant disabilities, it did not report any extended services to youth with the most significant 
disabilities, as described and defined in 34 C.F.R. §§ 363.4(a)(2) and 361.5(c)(19)(v), in any 
years of the review period. Extended services are coordinated with the Utah Department of 
Health and Human Services (DHHS) through the Services for People with Disabilities and 
Integrated Health partners.  
 
The Client Services Manual, Chapter 30 Supported Employment Services, last updated in June 
2024, along with policies, describe extended services, but USOR was unable to verify the 
delivery of these services during the three-year review period. Ensuring these services are 
delivered may help USOR improve the percentage of participants employed at exit in supported 
employment, which was eight percent in PY 2022, eight percentage points below the national 
average of 16 percent. 
 
Technical Assistance  
 
● RSA provided technical assistance regarding the need to revise language in policy 30.2 to 
align with 34 C.F.R. § 361.5(c)(19)(v) and ensure extended services are provided to a 
youth with a most significant disability by USOR in accordance with requirements set 
forth in 34 C.F.R. part 361 and part 363 for a period not to exceed four years, or at such 
time that a youth reaches age 25 and no longer meets the definition of a youth with a 
disability under 34 C.F.R. § 361.5(c)(58), whichever occurs first. USOR may not provide 
extended services to an individual with a most significant disability who is not a youth 
with a most significant disability. 
● RSA provided technical assistance regarding the need to revise policy language to ensure 
that when providing extended services to youth with the most significant disabilities, 
USOR follows a process similar to those receiving non-VR funded extended services and 
begins providing extended services after stabilization in supported employment, thus 
eliminating the need to exhaust all 24 months of ongoing services.  
 
Recommendation 
 
● To improve the performance and management of the Supported Employment program, 
USOR should train its staff in the requirements of 34 C.F.R. § 363.4(a)(2) and the 
appropriate methods for providing and tracking extended services to ensure it is 
accurately reporting extended services on the RSA-911. 
 
Data Accuracy: Case Service Record Review Process 
 
RSA reviewed a small sample of case service records and identified instances in which there was 
no evidence of supporting documentation for credential attainment and discrepancies between 
the dates entered in the record and those reported for measurable skill gains (MSG).  
 
USOR’s performance related to MSG and credential attainment may not be accurately portrayed 
due to a less rigorous case service record review process. USOR conducts a monthly review of

5 
one case per counselor for eight months for data validation purposes, and for quality assurance, 
USOR conducts an annual statewide case review of two cases per reviewer (supervisors and 
district directors). USOR conducts a full day of training on the statewide case review instrument 
and reviewers complete the reviews in a half-day session. USOR reported that 67 cases were 
reviewed during the most recent statewide case review, which may underrepresent the caseloads 
carried by its 132 VR counselors. Implementing a comprehensive case service record review 
helps to ensure that training needs are adequately identified and data are accurately verified and 
reported.  
 
Technical Assistance 
 
● RSA discussed the benefits of accurate reporting of data on performance and reviewing 
and revising USOR’s internal controls manual documentation in order to align its 
processes more accurately with the requirements under Sections 12(c) and 101(a)(6) and 
(a)(10)(A) of the Rehabilitation Act and 34 C.F.R. § 361.12.  
 
Recommendations 
 
● USOR should consider establishing a more robust monthly case service record review 
process that emphasizes reviewing indicators of high-quality employment outcomes, such 
as MSG and credential attainment.  
● Additionally, RSA recommends the sample size for the annual statewide case review be 
based on the number of caseloads along with the types of cases reviewed, rather than the 
number of reviewers. With approximately 132 VR counselors, this would result in a 
higher number of cases being reviewed and a more representative sample size.  
 
USOR Policies 
 
RSA identified several policies in the USOR Client Service Manual that may influence the 
performance of the VR and Supported Employment programs and the expenditure of funds. In 
accordance with 34 C.F.R. § 361.12, VR agencies must implement policies and procedures that 
ensure the proper and efficient administration of the VR program, including those necessary to 
carry out all functions for which the State is responsible. RSA noted 40 percent of the policies in 
the manual have effective dates between 2012 and 2016, and although some policies have more 
recent effective dates, they do not fully reflect accurate and up-to-date information consistent 
with the Rehabilitation Act and implementing regulations. With the enactment of WIOA, VR 
agencies were responsible for reviewing, revising, and updating policies and procedures to 
ensure alignment with regulations.  
 
In accordance with 34 C.F.R. § 361.50, State VR agencies must develop and maintain written 
policies covering the nature and scope of each of the VR services specified in 34 C.F.R. § 361.48 
and the criteria under which each service is provided. USOR reported recently updating some 
policies; however, those updates generally do not reflect revising outdated information or 
correcting misinterpreted regulations, but rather are related to changes in fees or newly 
implemented services. USOR leadership confirmed it has no policy review cycle and welcomed 
RSA’s technical assistance.

6 
Periodically reviewing and revising policies has the potential to significantly improve both the 
program and fiscal performance of the agency. Through regular assessments of—and updates 
to—policies, procedures, and practices, USOR could ensure its policies  are aligned with 
regulation and that it has not imposed barriers or limitations, but rather is implementing policies 
that support the receipt of services that maximize employment outcomes and career 
advancement.  
 
For example, during the review period, USOR reported low instances of graduate and bachelor’s 
degree training with the highest occurring in PY 2022. In PY 2022, the agency reported the 
largest number of individuals who received VR services (13,009); however, 0.3 percent received 
graduate degree training and 7.6 percent received bachelor’s degree training. USOR’s policies 
related to the pursuit of goals requiring postsecondary and graduate level education are discussed 
later in this section and may be contributing factors to USOR’s performance. 
 
Aligning VR agency policies with Federal regulations and RSA’s sub-regulatory guidance 
allows for the provision of a full continuum of permissible VR services and flexibility in the use 
of funds to support individuals with disabilities in achieving their highest possible employment 
goals. Maintaining accurate and up to date policies can further support a VR agency’s efforts to 
maximize the use of funds and resources to provide VR services that lead to competitive 
integrated employment and improve fiscal and program performance.

● USOR should review, revise, or eliminate policies and practices that may limit or

Agency Imposed Document Requirements: Application and IPE Development  
 
USOR’s policy 3.9 and policy 10.4 require applicants to provide a Social Security number (SSN) 
at application and a photo identification (ID) prior to the development of an Individualized Plan 
for Employment (IPE).  
 
● Policy 3.9 Legal Status to Engage in Employment states, “Applicants with a Social 
Security Number are assumed to meet the criteria for employment.” Policy 10.4 
Identification Verification for IPE Implementation states, “Prior to the implementation of 
an IPE all clients eligible for Vocational Rehabilitation Services must provide picture 
documentation that verifies their identity. Clients must provide a document from the 
following list of acceptable forms of picture identification.”  
● Policy 10.4, Section D. Lack of Acceptable Picture Identification states, “Until the client 
provides the acceptable form of picture identification the IPE cannot be implemented. If 
the client does not provide the required picture identification and does not collaborate 
with the VR Counselor in efforts to obtain the required verification of identity, the client 
record will be closed prior to IPE implementation.”  
 
Requiring a picture ID to verify identity prior to an IPE presents a potential burden to individuals 
with disabilities who may not have obtained photo identification prior to applying for VR 
services. Federal regulations at 34 C.F.R. § 361.42 do not require a specific type of identification 
to apply for or be determined eligible for VR services. Similarly, an SSN is not required for an 
individual to receive services through the VR program and, thus, individuals are not required to 
provide one. If an individual does not have an SSN or chooses not to provide an SSN, the agency 
can assign and report a unique identifier. 
 
Furthermore, as described in Social Security Administration Publication No. 05-10096, Social 
Security Numbers for Noncitizens, there are SSNs assigned to individuals for nonwork purposes, 
(e.g., when persons are lawfully admitted to the United States without work authorization from

8 
the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, but have a valid non-work reason for needing a 
SSN; or need a number because of a Federal, State or local law requiring a SSN to get a benefit 
or service.) It is not appropriate to assume that by providing an SSN an individual meets the 
criteria for employment. A State VR agency, per 34 C.F.R. § 361.42(a)(2), must presume that an 
applicant who meets the eligibility requirements in 34 C.F.R. § 361.42 (a)(1)(i) and (ii) can 
benefit in terms of an employment outcome.  
 
Providing appropriate types of acceptable documents for the purposes of employment eligibility 
verification, including identity and employment authorization, are standard practices when an 
individual is hired for employment and the types of documents a person may submit to an 
employer varies depending on whether the document establishes identity or employment 
authorization or both. USOR may wish to use the lists of acceptable documents on the U.S. 
Citizenship and Immigration Services I-9 Form to ensure the documents meet the requirements 
that would be accepted by an employer within three business days after the first day of 
employment.

Use of Interim IPE Goals and “Step Off” Points  
 
VR agencies must provide needed VR services consistent with the requirements under 34 C.F.R. 
§§ 361.45 and 361.46 specific to an individual’s stated vocational goal (except in the case of an 
eligible individual who is a student or a youth with a disability, for whom, a goal may be a 
description of the individual's projected post-school employment outcome). Pursuant to Section 
102(b)(4)(A) of the Rehabilitation Act and 34 C.F.R. § 361.46(a)(1), an IPE must include a 
description of the specific employment outcome, as defined in § 361.5(c)(15), that is chosen by

9 
the eligible individual and is consistent with the individual's unique strengths, resources, 
priorities, concerns, abilities, capabilities, career interests, and informed choice. 
 
USOR Training Service Policy, 19.14 Definitions and Types of Training Programs, section f. 
states, “Career Pathways means a training plan that includes progressive levels of credentialing 
with associated levels of employment advancement within that career field. Individuals 
following a career pathway may obtain employment at designated "step off" points and/or 
continue along a progressive training pathway.” The option to obtain employment at designated 
“step off” points may inadvertently influence or alter the IPE goal and VR services a participant 
needs to achieve their specific chosen goal and serve to discourage the pursuit of graduate or 
advanced level training that might maximize an employment outcome.  
 
USOR’s policy in Section 19.9 VR Support for Graduate Degree Programs, encourages support 
of establishing interim goals on an IPE. The section states—  
 
“Graduate training and employment goals that require graduate training must be 
approved by the Field Service Director….,” and  
 
“Prior to granting VR approval for graduate training and the associated employment the 
client must complete the following:  
● Successful completion of a bachelor’s degree program. 
● Acceptance at an institution and graduate program that corresponds to the 
proposed employment goal.  
In cases where a client wishes to pursue an employment goal that requires graduate level 
training but the client does not yet meet the criteria above, the VR counselor and client 
should explore the request and discuss career pathways that may lead to the graduate 
level goal. During the discussion about career pathways the VR Counselor and client 
should identify an undergraduate level employment goal that may serve as an interim 
goal until the client meets the criteria listed above. The career pathway exploration, 
identification of an interim goal, and the preliminary request for graduate level education 
should be documented in the client record.” 
 
As stated, the language appears to discourage pursuit of graduate degrees and encourages 
counselors to tailor a discussion around career pathways that will result in a pre-determined “step 
off point” resulting in a provisional employment goal on the IPE rather than the specific 
employment goal desired by the eligible individual requiring more advanced education. 
 
There is no language in the Rehabilitation Act that suggests an interim goal is an acceptable IPE 
goal. USOR must ensure its policies regarding support for graduate degrees fully provides 
individuals with disabilities, including those with the most significant disabilities, with the 
necessary services and supports to maximize their fullest potential and achieve high quality 
competitive integrated employment. The provision of services, which are not aligned with a 
specific employment outcome, as defined in 34 C.F.R. § 361.5(c)(15), on an IPE will be further 
discussed below.

10 
USOR leadership indicated the employment sectors in the State of Utah do not rely on four-year 
degrees, while staff indicated that, in general, the agency does not support customers in the 
pursuit of a graduate degree. While Utah’s economy may have employment sectors that do not 
rely on bachelor and graduate degrees (e.g., manufacturing, mining, and tourism) the State’s 
economy is made up of multiple sectors and includes employment opportunities in healthcare, 
finance, law, education, and information technology. For the period in review, USOR reported a 
43 percent increase in the number of individuals who received Graduate Degree Training (from 
28 in PY 2020 to 43 in PY 2022) and a 14 percent increase in the number of individuals who 
received Bachelor’s Degree Training (from 872 in PY 2020 to 983 in PY 2022). 
 
During onsite discussions, the agency indicated its focus on skills-based training is aligned with 
the Utah Governor’s skills-first hiring initiative. RSA acknowledges that skills-based training 
and skills-first hiring are distinctively different and may not require graduate and bachelor level 
education. However, from PY 2020 through PY 2022, USOR reported decreases in the number 
of individuals receiving occupational or vocational training, on-the-job training, and 
apprenticeship training for all three years.

Recommendations 
 
● RSA recommends the agency remove restrictive approval processes or policies that place 
limitations on the type, degree, and costs of support provided to individuals that may 
deter pursuit of the highest possible employment goal. The low number of individuals 
receiving support for advanced degrees as well as low instances of agency purchased job 
search and job placement assistance may suggest that participants are not receiving 
required services and may no longer see the value of the agency and seek supports 
elsewhere. 
● As described in RSA-TAC-23-03 Maximizing Services and the Use of Funds to Support 
Quality Employment Outcomes for Individuals with Disabilities through the Vocational 
Rehabilitation and Supported Employment Programs, RSA recommends USOR evaluate 
its current service delivery model and ensure it is fully assisting individuals with 
disabilities to compete for high-paying careers in science, technology, engineering, or 
math (STEM), green industries, critical infrastructure fields, and other expanding 
industries and provide advanced training, when needed and appropriate. 
● Section 103(a)(18) of the Rehabilitation Act specifically permits VR agencies to provide 
VR services that encourage qualified eligible individuals to pursue advanced training in 
the STEM fields, medicine, law, or business. RSA recommends that USOR ensure policy 
and practices reflect the requirement of informed choice and when developing an IPE 
goal, eligible individuals are made aware of the availability of in-State and out-of-State

services related to career, training, and other support services without regard to labor

Job Search and Job Placement Services 
 
USOR’s employment rate increased from 46.2 percent in PY 2020, to 49.5 percent in PY 2021, 
and 50.9 percent in PY 2022. The PY 2022 WIOA performance results for VR programs place 
the agency in the third quartile and slightly under the national average for employment in the 
second and fourth quarters after exit. In PY 2022, the agency reported the highest percentage of 
job search assistance and job placement assistance for all three years in review. Of the number of 
individuals who received VR services in PY 2022, only 19 percent were reported as receiving 
job search assistance and 17.1 percent were reported as receiving job placement assistance. 
USOR leadership attributed the low percentage of job search assistance and job placement 
services to the strong relationship with workforce system partners and indicated that many 
participants conduct a self-directed job search and are referred to American Job Centers for 
assistance. 
 
The PY 2022 WIOA performance results reflect the co-enrollment efforts in Utah and indicate 
the VR agency has the highest percentage (58.4 percent) of co-enrolled participants nationally. 
RSA encourages USOR to carefully consider the quality and type of supports participants require 
to be successful in their job search. Coordination of services with the workforce system partners 
can be advantageous for some customers, and although the job search assistance services that are 
available at a local one-stop center may be helpful, they may not completely meet the unique 
needs of an individual with a disability. Job search assistance and job placement services are 
exempt from a determination of the availability of comparable services and benefits as described 
in 34 C.F.R. § 361.53(b)(4). Maximizing the job search and job placement services available to 
individuals with disabilities, from USOR itself, could result in an increase in the number of 
participants exiting in competitive integrated employment. 
 
RSA’s review of USOR’s policy 22 CRP Fee for Services - Supported Job Based Training & 
Life Skills, section 22.14 Temporary Work Experiences prompted discussion regarding how the 
agency provides the services as outlined in the policy. The policy states—  
 
“In order to provide a client with work hardening, industry skills, and a work history, 
prior to securing a permanent employment, the VR Counselor and client may determine it 
to be necessary for the client to engage in a Temporary Work Experience (TWE). 
Temporary Work Experiences should be conducted prior to job development services. 
TWE includes paid internships, paid seasonal employment, and paid temporary 
employment.”  
 
USOR leadership confirmed temporary work is provided and reported as a job placement 
service. As worded, the temporary work experience appears to be a work-based learning 
experience that supports skill-building related to an agreed upon IPE goal through short-term 
employment or other work-based learning experiences not elsewhere classified. Work-based 
learning experiences are provided in an integrated environment in the community, to the

12 
maximum extent possible, and may be paid or unpaid. Services on the IPE must be those needed 
to achieve the employment goal. The miscoding of TWE as a job placement service results in an 
inflated report of this already minimally provided service. 
 
Technical Assistance 
 
● RSA provided technical assistance regarding USOR’s inaccurate reporting of work-based 
learning experiences as job placement services as outlined in its policy and emphasized 
the importance of using the appropriate service category when providing and reporting a 
service to avoid misrepresenting the performance of the agency. 
● RSA provided technical assistance on the need to ensure the services provided align with 
the IPE and the appropriate use of a work-based learning experience as a necessary

Recommendations

● RSA recommends USOR evaluate the referral process to the one-stop system to ensure 
referrals are appropriate and meet the needs of participants and increase VR agency 
provided job placement services to support participants’ individualized needs in 
achieving competitive integrated employment. 
 
Attrition and Engagement  
 
USOR reported significant attrition of eligible individuals with disabilities at various stages in 
the VR process for all program years reviewed. An average of 20.9 percent of individuals exited 
after eligibility, but prior to an IPE and an average of 34.1 percent exited after an IPE without an 
employment outcome. Approximately one quarter of exit reasons reported each year was the 
inability to locate or contact the individual. Those reported as no longer interested in receiving 
services or further services decreased each year of the period in review (25.9 percent or in PY 
2020, 23.3 percent in PY 2021, and 20.5 percent in PY 2022). When an individual exits after 
eligibility determination or after an IPE has been developed, the agency has made a notable 
investment of financial and staff resources. Despite initial frequent engagement during eligibility 
determination and development of the IPE, these percentages represent an area of improvement 
with ongoing engagement of eligible individuals and participants. 
 
USOR’s policies require contact every 90 days with individuals while participating in VR with 
an increase in frequency of 30 days when participating in job search activities. During onsite 
discussions, USOR indicated that when individuals are participating in services, other than job 
search assistance, the required frequency of contact is every 90 days with check points in the 
process. Communication between the VR counselor and the participant is vital to sustaining 
engagement and interest in completing services that will lead to a successful employment

Finding 2.2 Non-Compliance with Requirements of the Individualized Plan for

Issue: Does USOR provide needed VR services consistent with the requirements under 34

Finding 3.1 Insufficient Policies and Procedures 
 
Issue: Do USOR and DWS maintain written policies and procedures and effective internal 
control over the Federal award to provide reasonable assurance that the agency is managing the 
Federal award in compliance with Federal statutes, regulations, and the terms and conditions of 
the award.  
 
Requirement: In accordance with 2 C.F.R. § 200.302 (b)(6), agencies must provide for written 
procedure to implement financial management system requirements. A State VR agency must 
assure, in the VR services portion of the Unified or Combined State Plan, that it will employ 
methods of administration that ensure the proper and efficient administration of the VR program. 
These methods of administration (i.e., the agency’s internal controls) must include procedures to 
ensure accurate data collection and financial accountability (34 C.F.R. § 361.12). 
 
“Internal control” means a processes, designed and implemented by a non-Federal entity, to 
provide reasonable assurance regarding the achievement of objectives in the following 
categories:  
 
● Effectiveness and efficiency of operations;  
● Reliability of reporting for internal and external use; and  
● Compliance with applicable laws and regulations. 
  
Additionally, 2 C.F.R. § 200.303, requires a non-Federal entity to—  
 
● Establish, document, and maintain effective internal control over the Federal award that 
provides reasonable assurance that the non-Federal entity is managing the Federal award 
in compliance with Federal statutes, regulations, and the terms and conditions of the 
Federal award…; and
 
● Take prompt action when instances of noncompliance are identified, including 
noncompliance identified in audit findings. 
 
  
In accordance with the Uniform Guidance (2 C.F.R. § 200.302(a)), a State’s financial 
management systems, including records documenting compliance with Federal statutes, 
regulations, and the terms and conditions of the award, must be sufficient to permit the—  
 
● Preparation of reports required by terms and conditions; and  
● Tracking expenditures adequate to establish that such funds have been used according to 
the Federal statutes, regulations, and the terms and conditions of the Federal award. 
 
  
Furthermore, provisions at 2 C.F.R. § 200.302(b)(4) require that the financial management 
system of the recipient must provide for effective control over and accountability for all funds, 
property, and other assets. The recipient must safeguard all assets and ensure they are used solely 
for authorized purposes. An internal control deficiency occurs when the design or functioning of 
a control does not enable management or employees to prevent actions resulting in 
noncompliance with Federal and State requirements during their routine performance of duties.

Finding 3.3 Period of Performance Policies and Procedures  
 
Issue: Do USOR and DWS meet obligation and expenditure requirements in 2 C.F.R. § 200.1 
and 34 C.F.R. § 76.707. Does DWS assign obligations and expenditures align to the correct 
Federal award in accordance with 34 C.F.R. § 361.12, 2 C.F.R. §§ 200.1, 200.302, 200.303(a), 
200.309, and 34 C.F.R. § 76.702.  
 
Requirement: As a recipient of Federal VR and Supported Employment program funds, USOR 
must have procedures that ensure the proper and efficient administration of its VR and Supported 
Employment programs and enable USOR to carry out all required functions, including financial 
reporting (34 C.F.R. § 361.12). In accordance with the Uniform Guidance in 
2 C.F.R. § 200.302(a), a State’s financial management systems, including records documenting 
compliance with Federal statutes, regulations, and the terms and conditions of the award, must be 
sufficient to permit the preparation of reports required by general and program specific terms and 
conditions; and the tracing of funds to a level of expenditures adequate to establish that such

27 
funds have been used according to the Federal statutes, regulations, and the terms and conditions 
of the Federal award. The Uniform Guidance requires the financial management system of each 
non-Federal entity to provide for the identification, in its accounts, of all Federal awards received 
and expended and the Federal programs under which they were received (2 C.F.R. § 200.302(b)). 
In addition, Education Department General Administrative Regulations (EDGAR) at 
34 C.F.R. § 76.702 require States to use fiscal control and fund accounting procedures that 
ensure proper disbursement of and accounting for Federal funds.  
 
Each grant award has a defined “period of performance” that is the time during which the non-
Federal entity may incur new obligations to carry out the work authorized under the Federal 
award (2 C.F.R. § 200.1). A non-Federal entity may only charge to the Federal award allowable 
costs incurred during the period of performance (2 C.F.R. § 200.309, see also EDGAR 34 C.F.R. 
§§ 76.703 and 76.709). Grantees must implement internal controls necessary to ensure 
obligations and expenditures for a Federal award are assigned, tracked, recorded, and reported 
within the applicable period of performance for that Federal award, thereby ensuring the grantees 
are managing the award in compliance with Federal requirements (2 C.F.R. § 200.303(a)). The 
proper assignment of Federal and non-Federal funds to the correct period of performance is 
necessary for USOR to correctly account for VR and Supported Employment program funds so 
that RSA can be assured the agency has satisfied requirements for, among other things, match 
(34 C.F.R. § 361.60), MOE (34 C.F.R. § 361.62), and the reservation and expenditure of VR 
funds for the provision of pre-employment transition services (34 C.F.R. § 361.65(a)(3)). 
 
A financial obligation means “orders placed for property and services, contracts and subawards 
made, and similar transactions during a given period that require payment by the non-Federal 
entity during the same or a future period" (2 C.F.R. § 200.1). For expenditures to be allowable 
under the Federal award, agencies must demonstrate that the obligation occurred within the 
period of performance of the Federal award. EDGAR regulations at 34 C.F.R. § 76.707 explain 
when a State incurs an obligation for various kinds of services and property. Expenditures must 
be for payment of actual obligations. Obligations must be charged to a Federal award and must 
occur within the appropriate period of performance. Therefore, to properly account for and 
liquidate expenditures, grantees must be able to assign an obligation to a Federal award based 
upon the date the obligation was made (34 C.F.R. §§ 76.703 and 76.709).  
 
Analysis: RSA, USOR, and DWS discussed the agency’s ability to track and report obligations 
and liquidations in its systems and account for the expenditures on Federal financial reports. 
Through a review of USOR’s vendor contracts and on-site discussions, RSA found that the 
agency does not obligate funds in accordance with the Uniform Guidance requirements of 34 
C.F.R. § 76.707. The agency obligated funds on dates other than the dates on which the State 
made a binding written commitment to obtain work. Additionally, DWS’ period of performance 
policies, procedures, and related documentation do not include the level of detail necessary to 
ensure all obligations are assigned, tracked, liquidated, and reported accurately on Federal 
reports.  
 
RSA discussed with USOR and DWS obligations recording requirements and the need for 
formalized, comprehensive written policies, and procedures for period of performance and 
Federal reporting to ensure the agency makes obligations on the correct date and submits

28 
accurate Federal financial reports. RSA discussed the requirement of accurate Federal reporting 
of obligations, expenditures, and unobligated Federal and non-Federal funds, as this information 
is necessary for RSA to determine the extent to which USOR is complying with the non-Federal 
share requirements of match and MOE, as well as determining whether Federal awards qualify 
for carryover.  
 
Conclusion: RSA found that USOR is not in compliance with Federal regulations at 34 C.F.R. § 
361.12, 34 C.F.R. § 76.702, and 2 C.F.R. § 200.302 to ensure non-Federal and Federal 
obligations and expenditures are assigned, tracked, paid, and reported from the correct Federal 
award. The agency did not have sufficient internal controls to ensure that—  
 
1) A comprehensive written policy regarding period of performance has been developed to 
outline the steps and procedures to assign, track and report obligations and expenditures 
in systems for all cost categories identified in EDGAR 34 C.F.R. § 76.707, to ensure they 
are for allowable costs under the agency’s approved State Plan during the period of 
performance of the award; and 
2) All non-Federal and Federal obligations and expenditures are accurately assigned to 
specific fund sources, and tracked, paid, and reported on the appropriate Federal 
Financial Reports for the appropriate awards. 
 
As a recipient of Federal VR program funds, USOR must have procedures in place that ensure 
proper and efficient administration of its VR program, and that enable USOR to carry out all 
required functions. The methods of administration must ensure accurate data collection and 
financial accountability (34 C.F.R. § 361.12 and 2 C.F.R. § 200.302).  
 
Corrective Actions 3.3: RSA requires that USOR—  
 
3.3.1 Make requisite revisions to its financial data collection, analysis and reporting processes 
to ensure compliance so that DWS can—  
● Account for, track, and accurately liquidate all non-Federal and Federal expenditures 
from the correct fund sources within their defined periods of performance, 
commensurate with the period of performance for the corresponding obligations 
based on the correct obligation date assigned; and  
● Accurately report non-Federal and Federal obligations and liquidations on the RSA-
17 report for the corresponding period of performance for Federal awards.  
3.3.2  Develop/update and implement period of performance policies and procedures to 
accurately account for and report all obligations and expenditures for the cost categories 
identified in 34 C.F.R. § 76.707 to the correct FFY award period of performance, 
ensuring the policies address the—  
● Assignment of non-Federal and Federal obligations to the appropriate and specific 
fund sources within their defined periods of performance, and the liquidation of such 
funds based upon the assignment of the obligations; and  
● Accurate reporting of unliquidated obligations, expenditures, and unobligated Federal 
non-Federal funds on the quarterly RSA-17 reports;

29 
3.3.3  Develop and implement a written internal control process, including a monitoring 
component, to ensure ongoing compliance with Federal requirements for the areas 
mentioned in corrective actions 3.3.1 and 3.3.2. 
VR Agency Response: DWS has an established system of internal controls which is designed to 
provide reasonable assurance that all non-Federal and Federal obligations and expenditures are 
accurately assigned to specific fund sources, and are tracked, paid, and reported on the 
appropriate Federal financial reports for the appropriate awards. DWS acknowledges, however, 
that written policies and procedures do not outline the specific steps and procedures to assign, 
track, and report obligations and expenditures in systems for all cost categories identified in 
EDGAR 34 C.F.R. § 76.707. DWS will revise written policies and procedures to include a 
specific reference to 34 C.F.R. § 76.707. A periodic review of such policies and procedures will 
also be established to ensure that any changes to the program requirements are also updated in 
the corresponding policies and procedures.  
 
RSA Response: RSA appreciates DWS’ acknowledgement of its need to update policies and 
procedures to ensure the policies and procedures are current, accurate, address the unique 
requirements of the respective award and correct the issues noted in Finding 3.3. RSA will work 
with DWS and USOR through its corrective action plan to ensure it develops/updates and 
implements period of performance policies and procedures to accurately account for and report 
all obligations and expenditures for the cost categories identified in 34 C.F.R. § 76.707 to the 
correct FFY award period of performance.

3UNITED STATES DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION  OFFICE OF SPECIAL EDUCATION AND REHABILITATIVE SERVICES        In the Matter of the Arbitration Between     SOUTH CAROLINA COMMISSION FOR THE BLIND   and  KURT KEAN  Case Number R-S/18-02         A video conference was held in the above-entitled matter on      June 6, 2023 before an Arbitration Panel comprised of J. Michael Daniels, Commission-appointed Arbitrator; Karen Jones, Blind Vendor appointed Arbitrator; and Daniel F. Brent, duly designated as Impartial Chair by the U.S. Department of Education. Both parties attended these hearings, were represented by counsel, and were afforded full and equal opportunity to offer testimony under oath, to cross-examine witnesses, and to present evidence and arguments. A verbatim transcript was made of the proceeding. The record was declared closed upon receipt of the transcript. The Panel met in executive session on October 11, 2023.

2	 	     APPEARANCES    For the Commission:  Thomas Smith, Esq.  Carrie Morris  For the Blind Licensed Vendor:  Kurt Kean      NATURE OF THE CASE    The Claimant is a Blind Vendor who, along with approximately eighty other Blind Licensed Vendors in the State of South Carolina, participates in a Randolph-Sheppard Act program administered by the South Carolina Commission for the Blind (hereafter, the Commission,  SCCB, or the SLA) and overseen by the U.S. Department of Education  Department of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services.  In 2016, Mr. Kean (hereafter, the Claimant or the Blind Licensed Vendor), filed a complaint challenging the allocation of proceeds from vending machines located in secure areas, such as prisons or power plants, that could not

3	 	 readily be accessed by Blind Licensed Vendors in order to stock the vending machines or to retrieve the monies deposited at these facilities.  Such activity was carried out by third-party contractors and a portion of  the proceeds were submitted to the Commission for the benefit of the  Blind Licensed Vendors and the administration of the SCCB program.  Mr. Kean also alleged procedural violations, including failure by the SCCB promptly to provide accurate and complete accounting for vending machine proceeds collected and for the distribution of such funds as  required under the Randolph-Sheppard Act program.  Mr. Kean’s complaint was processed by the Commission and a response was provided.  The Claimant did not accept the Commission’s response and filed a request for a hearing before a Hearing Officer appointed by the Commission.  Professor James F. Flanagan, a professor at the University of South Carolina Law School, conducted a hearing on May 24, 2017 and issued his opinion and report (Exhibit A) on June 15,  2017.   Hearing Officer Flanagan determined, “Therefore, there is no reason to disturb the May vote and the Commission’s decision to vacate the February vote and hold the second vote in May that followed the Federal Regulation is affirmed. It is noted that Mr. Kean's action brought this issue to the attention of the Commission, and through its prompt action in response and its work with the Vendors Committee that the

4	 	 new and more appropriate procedures were developed.”   Claimant Kean did not accept Hearing Officer Flanagan’s Findings and Conclusions and appealed to the United States Department of Education Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services Administration, which administers the Randolph-Sheppard Act, 20 U.S.C. Section 107d-1(a) and 107(d)(2) and the implementing regulations at 34 CFR Section 395.13. Under the authority of 20 USC Section 107(d)(2), the Secretary uses the “Policies and Procedures for Convening and Conducting an Arbitration” to process complaints.  By letter dated January 27, 2021, "The Secretary authorized the convening of an arbitration panel to hear and render a decision on the issues raised in the complaint.”    The undersigned Arbitrator was appointed as Panel Chairperson in  Arbitration Case R-S/18-02 between Kurt Kean and South Carolina Commission for the Blind. By letter dated February 23, 2023, the undersigned advised the party-appointed Arbitrators, Karen L. Jones, Complainant-appointed Arbitrator and J. Michael Daniels II, Commission-appointed Arbitrator, of his appointment and convened a prehearing conference with the Panel and the Complainant, Kurt Kean and Respondent SCCB on May 8, 2023.  An arbitration hearing was conducted by video conference on June 6, 2023, at which both parties appeared and were given ample opportunity to present testimony under

5	 	 oath, to cross-examine witnesses, and to present evidence and arguments. A verbatim transcript was made of the proceeding.             The Arbitration Panel thereafter convened an executive session on October 11, 2023.                                POSITIONS OF THE PARTIES    Claimant Kean asserted that the SCCB failed to provide an accurate accounting of the monies collected by third party contractors servicing vending machines in locations that are inaccessible to  Blind Licensed Vendors and that the procedure for allocating and paying funds to Blind Licensed Vendors and to the SCCB was materially flawed.  The Claimant further asserted that the distribution of such funds was not properly approved by a vote of all eligible Blind Licensed Vendors and that the Commission retained an inordinately large portion of the funds collected by third-party contractors from vending machines that were  inaccessible to Blind Licensed Vendors.  The Commission contended that its administration of the funds was appropriate and that any procedural defects in the Blind Licensed  Vendor ratification process had been rectified by a subsequent vote of the Vendors.  According to the Commission, the substantially reduced

6	 	 disbursements to Blind Licensed Vendors described by Claimant Kean were primarily attributable to dramatically reduced income from these vending machines during the Covid-19 pandemic.  The Commission further asserted that the retention of funds for operational use by the  Commission was procedurally valid and necessary to fund programs that benefit the Blind Licensed Vendors of South Carolina.     DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS        Following Claimant Kurt Kean’s appeal of Hearing Officer       James F. Flanagan’s June 15, 2017 decision, the U.S. Department of Education certified the instant dispute for arbitration under the applicable provisions of the Randolph-Sheppard Act and pertinent regulations and appointed Daniel F. Brent as Impartial Chairperson of the tri-partite Arbitration Panel.   The central issue in the instant dispute is whether SCCB, as the State Licensing Agency, violated the Randolph-Sheppard Act and implementing regulations by not providing accurate information to South Carolina Blind Licensed Vendors sufficient for them to vote in 2016 and 2017 approving the distribution of accrued vending machine income and whether the funds disbursed in 2016 and 2017 to Blind Licensed Vendors and to the SCCB were properly allocated and paid.

7	 	 Mr. Kean expressed his dismay and distrust regarding the process by which the SCCB described its collection of vending machine proceeds in 2016 and 2017 and the disbursement of these proceeds among the Commission and the Blind Licensed Vendors.  Apparently, his procedural and substantive concerns were addressed by the SCCB by the time his appeal was heard and decided by a Hearing Officer appointed by the Commission in 2017 and a second vote ratified the flawed initial vote.   Mr. Kean subsequently appealed to the Department of Education  pursuant to his rights under the Randolph-Sheppard Act.    Following Claimant Kean’s appeal of Hearing Officer James F. Flanagan’s June 15, 2017 decision, the U.S. Department of Education certified the instant dispute for arbitration under the applicable provisions of the Randolph-Sheppard Act and pertinent regulations and appointed Daniel F. Brent as Impartial Chairperson of the tri-partite Arbitration Panel.   During the hearing conducted by this Panel, Mr. Kean discussed infirmities he perceived in the SCCB’s transmission of relevant financial information to all Blind Licensed Vendors in South Carolina continuing through 2022.  At the June 6, 2023 arbitration hearing before this Panel, the Commission credibly described the procedural revisions it had recently introduced in 2021 to correct these infirmities, particularly the manner in which relevant information is disseminated to Blind Licensed

8	 	 Vendors for purposes of providing information and enabling the BLV’s to examine and ratify Commission actions that affect them.   
      The Commission also offered sworn testimony that explained in detail the sequence of events, including votes by duly constituted bodies of Blind Licensed Vendors at the State and District levels, authorizing the consolidation of certain funds to eliminate a reserve allocation going forward, the disbursement of the entire reserve to offset the drastically reduced income of all BLV’s in South Carolina caused by the Covid-19 pandemic, and the continuing disbursement of vending funds in the years after the 2017 corrected vote using the most recent formula approved by the body of BLV’s or their designated representatives.  
The Commission offered testimony candidly admitting the necessity for the upgrade of information dissemination procedures and asserting that sufficient steps have been taken in the last two years to rectify the defects that precipitated Mr. Kean’s original complaint.  This Panel accepts as accurate the testimony of the Commission’s witness and representations by the Commission’s Counsel describing the corrected voting procedure and subsequent disbursements.  However, the remedial reforms implemented by the Commission were inadequate to assure timely dissemination to all South Carolina Blind Licensed Vendors of

9	 	 pertinent financial information sufficient for them to follow the actions of the SCCB regarding income and disbursements.  The procedural defects charged by Mr. Kean regarding 2016 and 2017 were corrected by a subsequent vote of Blind Licensed Vendors or their representatives.  The Commission’s witness did not explain how the second vote occurred, as it predated her tenure with the Commission.  However, the evidentiary record and her testimony based on available records persuaded this Panel that the vending machine funds collected by third party contractors in 2016 and 2017 apparently were disbursed in accordance with the formulae then in effect.   The evidentiary record did not support any allegation of financial impropriety regarding 2016 and 2017, the years at issue in Mr. Kean’s initial complaint.  Thus, Mr. Kean has not met his burden of demonstrating by a preponderance of the evidence that the subsequent corrective action ratifying the modifications described by the sworn testimony of the Commission’s witness were procedurally defective or invalid.  Moreover, Claimant Kean did not establish that the reduction in disbursements of which he complained was attributable to unauthorized or improper allocation of funds that should have been paid directly to the Blind Licensed Vendors.

10	 	 Mr. Kean did establish by a preponderance of the evidence the need to codify the improved procedures for communication of information to all Blind Licensed Vendors served by the SCCB that were implemented in 2021 or thereafter.  Consequently,  the undersigned Panel of Arbitrators, having been duly designated by the United States Department of Education Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services pursuant to the Randolph-Sheppard Act and applicable regulations, and having duly heard the proofs and allegations  of the parties, AWARDS as follows:   This Panel orders that the SCCB hereafter provide to all Blind Licensed Vendors in South Carolina, not just to the Elected Committee of Blind Vendors or other sub-groups of BLV representatives in meetings with SCCB staff, the following information in formats that are accessible to the Vendors:    (1) All quarterly or annual accounting reports of revenue from vending operations or any other source of Commission income other than the BLV’s individual business operations;   (2) the income and expenditures of the SCCB;   (3) any disbursements to Blind Licensed Vendors, to the Elected Committee of Blind Vendors, or to the SCCB; and any other financial information reasonably related to the administration of the SCCB that is not confidential Commission personnel or financial data.

11	 	 This information is reasonably related to the Blind Licensed Vendors’ participation in the Randolph-Sheppard Act program administered by the SCCB under the aegis of the United States Department of Education Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services.    This Panel hereby reserves jurisdiction for thirty days to revise or amend this remedy to conform with applicable statutes and regulations and to resolve any dispute that may arise regarding the implementation of the remedy ordered pursuant to this Arbitration Decision.

RE: Annual Review Finding Regarding Failure to Timely Determine Eligibility in PY 2022 
 
Dear Joseph Murphy:     
 
This letter presents the Rehabilitation Services Administration’s (RSA) findings regarding 
the Rhode Island Office of Rehabilitation Services’ (ORS) failure to substantially comply 
with the requirement to determine eligibility on a timely basis in Program Year (PY) 2022
1 
under the State Vocational Rehabilitation (VR) program in accordance with section 102(a)(6)  
of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (Rehabilitation Act). RSA made its determination of ORS’s 
substantial non-compliance during its annual review, conducted in accordance with section 
107(a) of the Rehabilitation Act, of Case Service Report (RSA-911)2 data submitted by ORS 
during PY 2022. This letter constitutes RSA’s report to ORS of the annual review of PY 
2022 data in accordance with section 107(a)(5) of the Rehabilitation Act. 
 
Pursuant to Section 107(a)(1) of the Rehabilitation Act, RSA must conduct an annual review 
of the VR program to ensure each State is substantially complying with the requirements of 
its Unified or Combined State Plan. In conducting the annual reviews, RSA must consider, 
among other things, reports submitted by the VR agencies (section 107(a)(2)(H) of the 
Rehabilitation Act). In conducting the annual reviews, RSA must examine, among other 
areas, the eligibility process (section 107(a)(4)(A) of the Rehabilitation Act). As recipients of 
Federal funds, VR agencies must collect and report certain individual-level programmatic 
data about VR program applicants pursuant to sections 101(a)(10) of the Rehabilitation Act. 
Among the statutorily required data elements, RSA must require VR agencies to report, 
among other things most relevant to the issue presented herein, the date of application and 
the date of eligibility or ineligibility determination (section 101(a)(10)(E)(i)(II) of the

Section 102(a)(6) of the Rehabilitation Act requires ORS to make eligibility determinations, 
“within a reasonable period of time, not to exceed 60 days, after the individual has submitted 
an application” for VR services, unless: (1) both ORS and the applicant agree to a specific 
extension of time; or (2) ORS is providing a trial work experience for the applicant. 
According to ORS’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data, the VR agency made a total of 715 
determinations of eligibility or ineligibility without extensions. Of these, ORS made the 
determinations for 604 applicants within the required 60-day period (or 84.5 percent of the 
total applicants) and made the determinations for 111 applicants beyond 60 days of 
application (or 15.5 percent of the total applicants).

ORS’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data demonstrate the VR agency failed to substantially comply 
with the requirement of section 102(a)(6) of the Rehabilitation Act because 15.5 percent of 
its applicants that year did not receive a timely eligibility or ineligibility determination within 
the required 60-day period. 
 
Section 101(a)(1)(A) of the Rehabilitation Act requires States to submit a Unified or 
Combined State Plan in accordance with section 102 of the Workforce Innovation and 
Opportunity Act in order to receive VR funding. The State Plan must contain a VR services 
portion that satisfies the requirements of the VR program (34 C.F.R. § 361.10(a)(2)), 
including those pertaining to eligibility determinations. In addition to this specific assurance, 
among others, each VR agency submitting the VR services portion of the State Plan signs an 
assurance that the VR agency will satisfy all Federal requirements governing the VR 
program, which would include those pertaining to timelines for eligibility determinations. 
Moreover, as a recipient of Federal funds, each VR agency, must develop internal controls to 
ensure the VR agency complies with all Federal requirements and implements corrective 
actions when it identifies instances of non-compliance (2 C.F.R. § 200.303(a) and (d)). By 
signing the VR services portions of their State Plan and accepting VR grant funds, ORS 
certified that it would administer and operate the VR program in accordance with the State 
Plan, Rehabilitation Act, and applicable Federal regulations governing the VR program. As 
demonstrated by ORS’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data and described above, the VR agency fell 
short of satisfying the Federal requirement governing the timeline for eligibility or 
ineligibility determinations that year.  
 
In accordance with Section 107(b)(2) of the Rehabilitation Act, the Commissioner requires 
ORS to develop and implement a corrective action plan (CAP) that— 
• Addresses the root cause of the VR agency’s noncompliance with the requirement; 
• Contains a solid, workable plan for action;

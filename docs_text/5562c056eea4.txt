Section 1 Policy: General Introduction to the Alaska Business Enterprise Program
Summary
The federal Randolph-Sheppard Program provides employment opportunities for blind individuals through the establishment and operation of vending facilities on federal property. See the Randolph-Sheppard Vending Stand Act (Public Law 74- 732, as amended by Public Law 83-565 and Public Law 93-516, 20 U.S.C., Sec
107). The law is further clarified in the Code of Federal Regulations, 34 CFR 395 through 395.38. This law and implementing regulations further describe the guidelines for the administration of a state program that provides employment to blind persons through the establishment and supervision of vending facilities on federal and other properties.
The Alaska Business Enterprise Program was initiated with the Chance Act of 1976. This Act created the Program and provides employment opportunities for both the Blind and persons who are significantly disabled through the establishment and operation of vending facilities on state property. See AS 23.15.010 through AS 23.15.210, as amended in 1982. The implementing state regulations are 8 AAC 98.300 through 8 AAC 98.470.
As a practical matter, these two federal and state laws intersect to create and provide guidance to one single program, which is known as the Alaska Business Enterprise Program.
Legal Basis
The Randolph-Sheppard Act (PL 74-732), first passed in 1936, created a business enterprise program to give priority in the operation of vending facilities to persons who are blind and in need of employment and economic opportunities. The legislation was revised in 1954 (PL 83-563) and again in 1974 (PL 93-516) as the Randolph-Sheppard Act Amendments. The success of the federal program led to the development of the Chance Act in Alaska in 1976 (AS 23.15.100). This Act, which was amended in 1982, created a similar priority for individuals who are blind and/or severely disabled to manage vending facilities on state property and also authorized operations on non-federal properties.
Financial Basis
The intent is that the Business Enterprise Program (BEP) is funded using the “Business
Enterprise Program Revolving Fund.” Income for this fund is generated from:
Commissions received from vending machines operated by third-party vendors in nonfederal buildings throughout the state of Alaska where there are no Blind Managers assigned and as authorized in AS 23.15.130;
Vending machine income received from federal entities pursuant to the Code of Federal Regulation 34 CFR 395.32;
Funds received from a set-aside fund to which the Vending Facility Managers contribute from their net proceeds each month.
These three sources of funds are accounted for separately to comply with spending restrictions on the various funds.
To the extent funds are available, federal funds (authorized under Section 110 of the Vocational Rehabilitation Act of 1973, as amended may be used to pay for allowable costs associated with the administration of the Program.
Services Provided by the Program
Experience throughout the nation indicates that the Business Enterprise Program (BEP) can be an effective method to help individuals who are blind and/or significantly disabled become contributing members of society. It also provides a service to
occupants of specific buildings who have available to them the goods offered for sale in vending facilities located within the buildings.
Administrative Responsibility
The BEP in Alaska is under the jurisdiction and administrative control of the Alaska Division of Vocational Rehabilitation which is located within the Department of Labor & Workforce Development and serves as the State Licensing Agency (SLA). As the SLA, the Agency is responsible for all duties as outlined in 34C.F.R. 395.1 thru 395.38.
These responsibilities are further detailed in 8 AAC 98.380.
Rules and Regulations
The Agency, with the active participation of the Committee of Blind Vendors, develops and adopts rules, regulations, policies, and procedures which are consistent with and designed to carry out the intent of the Randolph-Sheppard Act and the Chance Act. The rules, regulations, policies, and procedures are subject to change as changes in legislation and operating conditions dictate. The rules and regulations can be found at 8 AAC 98.300 thru 98.470. The policies and procedures are contained in this Alaska Business Enterprise Program Policy and Procedures Manual. The rules and the Policies and Procedures Manual are subject to approval by the Secretary of the U.S. Department of Education before their implementation.
Guiding Principles and Values
The Alaska Division of Vocational Rehabilitation has determined that the BEP, including its Licensed Managers, operates most effectively when acting in accordance with the following guiding core values:
Truth, honesty, and respect in all interactions involving the Agency staff and/or Managers
High expectations that believe in the abilities of blind people to be self-reliant, independent, and successful businesspeople.
High standards and accountability in terms of the work performance and ethical conduct of all BEP Managers and staff. Comprehensive training for all prospective/current Managers and staff in the Alaska Business Enterprise Program.
The individuality and diversity of the blind people and Business Enterprise Program staff.
The customers of the vending facilities and a commitment to always provide positive customer experiences.
Creativity and innovation that are intended to encourage all program participants
to think “outside of the box” when it comes to possibilities.
Collaboration between the Agency and Committee of Blind Vendors and transparency in the administration of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program.
Determination and a “Get It Done” mentality amongst all vendors and staff.
Constructive communication throughout the Alaska Business Enterprise Program.
Program Model
In order to achieve the purpose of the Randolph-Sheppard Act and to uphold its established values, the BEP in Alaska utilizes a franchise-like model. This model consists of the following:
Private individuals who are blind (Licensed Vending Facility Managers) own and independently operate businesses. Through their business, blind Managers operate vending facilities located on state-owned or leased properties as well as non-federal properties within established guidelines contained in the program rules and this Policy and Procedures Manual. The Managers are free to make decisions within these constraints and are accountable for the consequences of those decisions. Under this model, the net proceeds represent the income to the Manager.
Unlike most franchises, the SLA provides the necessary equipment and initial inventory for the vending facility and will maintain or cause to be maintained all vending facility equipment.
Similar to a franchise, the business owners and Vending Facility Managers contribute a certain percentage of their net proceeds to the revolving fund in the way of a set aside assessment  which is used in accordance with 34 C.F.R. 395.9 to maintain and build the program and to benefit blind business owners.  .
The SLA has overall responsibility to ensure the quality of service offered by each business owner (Licensed Vending Facility Managers). The state must ensure that the BEP is operated within the constraints of the law and regulations and provide management services as defined in 34 C.F.R. 395.1(j).
The Committee of Blind Vendors is established to provide active participation in the operation of the BEP. They function to participate with the SLA in all major decisions affecting the overall operation of the program and to serve as the voice of the Vending Facility Managers. The Committee also advocates for Managers and performs other functions defined in the rules and regulations and the Policies and Procedures Manual.
Policy of Nondiscrimination
As the State Licensing Agency, the Alaska BEP ensures that every aspect of the Business Enterprise Program shall be administered without reference to sex, age, religion, race, color, creed, national origin, political affiliation, disability, marital status, pregnancy, parenthood, sexual orientation / gender identity or expression   and shall further ensure compliance with all federal and state laws related to discrimination.
General Structure and Responsibilities
The final authority for the administration of the Alaska BEP rests solely with the SLA. This includes assuring the ongoing active participation of the Committee of Blind Vendors. Essentially, the SLA and Committee will create the framework for the administration of the program in the way of rules, regulations, policies, procedures, annual budgets, etc., and it is the responsibility of the SLA to administer the program day-to-day within the constraints of that framework.

Section 2 Policy: Pre-Requisite Requirements for Entry into BEP
Summary
It is the policy of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program that only individuals with pre- requisite skills that suggest a reasonable chance of success shall be admitted into the training program. BEP operates on the belief that special skills are required to be a successful businessperson. The BEP has set the bar high because, consistent with its values, it places high expectations on blind people who own and operate businesses within the Program.
Procedure
All potential candidates shall be evaluated as part of their Vocational Rehabilitation program to determine if they possess the necessary pre-requisite skills.
Minimum Criteria
The following are required of all individuals entering the BEP training program in Alaska:
A high school diploma or GED;
Basic math skills that include the ability to do addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, fractions, and decimals at the 8th grade level;
Demonstrated computer skills that document:
Keyboarding skills of at least 30 words per minute;
Proficiency in searching the internet for resources;
Ability to do email; and,
Proficiency in using Microsoft Word and Excel;
A demonstrated ability to communicate in writing, whether it be via print,  Braille, or digitally;
Demonstrated ability to live independently, including but not limited to the ability to:
Be able to arrange public transportation when necessary and travel independently both in the daylight and dark (using a white cane, guide dog, or technology device is acceptable);
Take care of personal needs such as dressing, grooming, and maintaining personal hygiene;
Cook and prepare their meals at home;
Clean their domicile;
Testing that demonstrates an aptitude for success as a business owner and for providing quality customer service.
Policy of Nondiscrimination
As the State Licensing Agency, the Alaska BEP ensures that no candidate for Entry-Level Training shall be denied admission based on sex, age, religion, race, color, creed, national origin, political affiliation, disability, marital status, pregnancy, parenthood, or sexual orientation / gender identity or expression  and shall ensure compliance with all Federal and State laws related to discrimination.
Documentation
A formal vocational and personal adjustment evaluation must determine whether an individual meets the minimum qualifications. A report of this evaluation shall be maintained in the Vocational Rehabilitation case file.
Exceptions
Pursuant to AAC 98.320(d), the Program Coordinator may grant a waiver to the training requirements contained in this policy if the individual possesses successful experience that suggests they can be successful in the BEP without the aforementioned evaluation.

Section 3 Policy: Entry-Level Training
Date:
Summary
It is the policy of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program that all candidates for licensing must complete a rigorous training program to ensure they have the knowledge and skills to meet the demands of owning and operating their own business and provide a high-quality service to customers.
If the evaluation indicates that the individual possesses the attributes needed to become a Licensed Vending Facility Manager, they may be accepted for training. A comprehensive training curriculum is a basis for preparing potential Managers. The training consists of two parts. The first is a classroom component. The Agency and Elected Committee may jointly determine to allow this portion of the training to be completed through a nationally recognized online provider. Any online training must be fully accessible to blind individuals. The second training phase is an on-the-job experience that places the client with experienced Blind Managers in existing vending facilities or private sector commercial operations.
Entry-Level Training
Entry Level Curriculum
The Agency, with the active participation of the State Committee of Blind Vendors, shall develop a training program for potential Blind Managers. The Agency may provide training in a classroom setting or through an online entity. The curriculum, as a minimum, shall include the following:
The Randolph-Sheppard Act
Vending Management
Café / Snack Bar Operations
Micromarket Management
Business Management Systems
Accounting
Sales Techniques
Human Resource Management
Financial Analysis of a Business
Customer Service
Business Practices
Health and Sanitation (ServeSafe)
A trainee must pass all modules and a comprehensive final exam to complete the training successfully.
On-the-Job Training
Pursuant to 34 CFR 395.11, the entry-level training shall include an on-the-job training component. The Program Coordinator shall arrange for the on-the-job training at an existing vending facility operated by a Blind Manager. At their discretion, the Program Coordinator may arrange the training with a privately owned food service business. If the training is to be provided by a private entity rather than a blind licensee, the Committee shall actively participate in the decision as to the selection of the training provider.
Trial Period (Performance Probation)
Once the candidate who is blind has successfully completed the requirements outlined in Subsections (1) and (2) above, their name will be placed on the Certified for Placement List. This allows them to bid on and be assigned to a vacant vending facility either as the permanent or temporary Manager. To qualify for a BEP license, the individual must successfully complete a 6-month performance probationary period during their initial assignment. This first
assignment is an extension of the training program. During these six months, the Program Coordinator will ensure the effective application of the training previously received. Continued feedback and instruction will be provided based on performance. The Program Coordinator will also make suggestions based on best practices. The Program Coordinator is also responsible for ensuring the Manager receives training on Alaska BEP requirements, including the rules, policies, and reporting requirements, as well as other areas unique to Alaska, such as taxes and health codes.
Upon successful completion of this probationary performance period, the individual will be issued a license to manage and operate vending facilities in the State of Alaska.
Post-Employment Training
Pursuant to 34 CFR 395.11, the Agency shall make post-employment services available to the individual. Once placed into a vending facility, additional Vocational Rehabilitation services may be required prior to the VR case being closed. The Vocational Rehabilitation Counselor, after collaborating with the Program Coordinator, will make such services available to maximize the chances of the individual succeeding. The individual may reapply for VR services if additional VR services are required after the case is closed.
Documentation
The VR Counselor shall maintain in the case file a record of satisfactory completion of the classroom training, on-the-job training, and probationary performance period. The Program Coordinator shall provide written verification to the VRC that the individual is being placed on the Certified for Placement List. The Program Coordinator will document performance during the initial six months and also document completion of the BEP and Alaska-specific training. The Program Coordinator shall prepare a license, get it signed, and provide a copy to the VRC.
Exceptions
The Program Coordinator may extend the performance probationary period beyond the initial six months if additional training is required. The justification must be documented and included in the VR case file.
Exceptions to the training requirements outlined in this section may be waived pursuant to Policy 4.2.

Section 4 Policy: Waiver of Entry-Level Training Requirements
Summary
It is the policy of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program to recognize the unique knowledge, skills, and experiences of individuals desiring entry into the BEP. The Agency believes that individuals who have been licensed in other states to operate vending facilities and/or have extensive experience in food service management do not require the same level of training as those without similar backgrounds.
Procedure
At their discretion, the Program Coordinator may grant a waiver to individuals who meet any of the following criteria:
Previously licensed in Alaska and left in good standing either voluntarily or for medical reasons;
Previously successfully managed a vending facility in another state and left that state in good standing;
Have experience of five years or more in food service management in either the private or public sector and meet all other prerequisites for entry into the Program;
Have extensive business experience of at least five years in a related field; and/or,
Have completed a nationally recognized food service training program which, in the opinion of the Program Coordinator, is sufficient to prepare the individual to be a Vending Facility Manager.
To be considered in “good standing,” the individual must not have left their previous assignment owing the Program any money and must not have been on any corrective action plan or had their license in disciplinary status at the time of departure. The Program Coordinator will be responsible for documenting Manager’s good standing before they are allowed into training or considered for a waiver.
Individuals who meet the above criteria may be certified without going through the Vocational Rehabilitation Program. The Program Coordinator shall arrange for training on Alaska’s requirements (i.e., BEP rules, policies, and procedures, as well as reporting, taxes, health codes, etc.)
Documentation
The Program Coordinator shall document the results of inquiries into whether or not Managers left previous assignments in good standing. Candidates wishing to qualify based on non-BEP experience must provide a resume and references. The Program Coordinator shall contact references and document the findings.

Section 5 Policy: Continuing Education Requirements
Summary
All Licensed Blind Vendors with active vending facility assignments and those individuals on the Certified for Placement List will be required to obtain continuing education / upward mobility credits to maintain their licenses and eligibility to bid on vacant vending facilities. The intent is to set the expectation for Licensees to refresh their current knowledge and skills and update skills, gain new skills to assist them in qualifying for higher-level facilities, and improve their operations.
Procedure
Each Licensee and individual on the Certified for Placement List will be required to accrue twelve (12) training credits over a two-year period. For purposes here, a year is a calendar year.
Training credits may be obtained in the following ways:
Annual Training Conference = 3 Points
Special Training offered by the Agency on topics of importance = 2 Points
National Conferences Sponsored by Blind Randolph-Sheppard Consumer Groups (i.e., BLAST, which the National Association of Blind Merchants sponsors) = 4 Points
National Conferences Sponsored by National Trade Organizations (i.e., National Automated Merchandising Association, National Restaurant Association, etc.) = 4 Points
Serve Safe or other nationally recognized training for certification or recertification = 2 Points
Approved On-Line Training Modules Provided by the Chicago Lighthouse for the Blind = 2 Points
Other Approved Online Training Courses = 2 Points
On-Line Webinars / Virtual Training Approved by the Agency after active participation by the Elected Committee= 1 Point
Business Related College Courses = 8 Points
Food Shows Sponsored by Suppliers = 2 Points
Leadership Training Sponsored by a vending/food service organization or National Blind Consumer Group = 4 Points
Other Training as mutually agreed to by the Agency and Committee of Blind Vendors – Point Value to be Determined
If a Blind Licensee or individual on the Certified for Placement List fails to achieve the required points over the two years, the Program Coordinator shall notify the Vendor in December and advise the Vendor. The letter, which shall be provided in an accessible format, will afford the Vendor an extra three months (March 31st) to meet the requirements. If the individual fails to meet the minimum training requirements, the license for active Managers to manage a vending facility will be terminated after first affording the individual an opportunity for an evidentiary hearing. Individuals on the Certified for Placement List will have their names removed from the list and will no longer be eligible to compete for assignments.
It is incumbent upon the Agency to make enough Training available or provide financial assistance that will enable the Vendor to fulfill these requirements. In addition to the annual Managers Meeting, the Agency shall provide at least one additional Training on a relevant topic that is at least 3 hours in length and which will enable the program participants to obtain the 2 points credit. The Agency will also share information about outside training opportunities as such information becomes available and will recommend online Training that can be completed to achieve the necessary credits.
Documentation
Licensed Vending Facility Managers and persons on the Certified for Placement List must provide documentation of participation in any outside training not provided by the Agency. The Program Coordinator shall maintain records of Managers completing Agency sponsored Training.
Any individual seeking an exception due to a medical hardship must provide documentation of said medical hardship sufficient to support the position that the individual could not obtain the necessary continuing education credits.
Exceptions
The Program Coordinator may grant exceptions to the training requirement if a Manager or individual on the Certified for Placement List experiences a documented extended illness and/or a family member has such an extended illness of six months or longer and that the individual cannot participate in Training. The illness would also have to preclude the individual from participating in online/virtual Training.

Section 6 Policy: Bidding for Vending Facilities
Summary
The Agency is committed to providing a fair and equitable process for making assignments to vacant vending facilities. Each Licensed Vending Facility Manager in good standing and individuals on the Certified for Placement List will be afforded the opportunity to be considered for any vacant vending facility through what is referred to herein as the bidding process.
Procedure
Eligibility to Bid
When new vending facilities are established and/or existing facilities become vacant, all current Licensed Vending Facility Managers and those individuals on the Certified for Placement List are eligible to compete for those assignments through a bidding process. Individuals can submit a bid provided they:
Do not have their license in probationary status on the date of the bid announcement;
Are not delinquent on the date of the bid announcement in the filing of financial reports required by the SLA;
Are not delinquent on the date of the bid announcement in the payment of any financial obligation owed to the SLA;
Bid Announcement
All Licensed Vending Facility Managers and individuals on the Certified for Placement List shall be notified of any vacant vending facilities. The announcement shall be sent electronically and via U.S. Mail. Any individual desiring the announcement in Braille or in another accessible format may request that the SLA send all such communications in the alternative format. The bid announcement shall include the following:
The location of the vending facility;
Whether it is a primary or secondary site;
Whether it is a permanent or temporary assignment;
Description of the vending facility;
Estimated number of prospective patrons;
Operating hours of the facility;
A general idea of the items to be sold;
Projected net sales, expenses, and profit for the facility based upon the past twelve (12) months if available;
Date of a site visit for candidates to see the facility if applicable and they choose to visit;
Any special circumstances that may exist;
Specific training that must have been completed; and,
The bid closing date.
Regarding (k) above, the Agency and Committee may jointly decide to require candidates for certain types of facilities to complete specialized training to be eligible to bid. Examples might include bidding on a military dining contract, micromarket, or inmate commissary. The Agency shall ensure that every Vending Facility Manager and individual on the Certified for Placement List has been afforded the opportunity to attend such training before it is listed as a requirement on a bid announcement.
The bid announcement shall include a space for the candidate to sign indicating their interest in being considered and a statement whereby the Manager agrees
to release pertinent information about their past performance to the interview panel.
Submitting a Bid
If a Manager or individual on the Certified for Placement List wishes to bid on the vending facility, they will sign the bid announcement, attach a resume, and return it to the Program Coordinator on or before the closing date. Bids may be returned using surface mail, email, or hand delivery. As each bid is received, it will be dated, and the time of receipt will be noted. Any bids received after the closing date will not be considered. Bids will remain open for ten (10) working days. Following the bids closing, the Program Coordinator will make a list of all bids received on or before the closing date. The list will indicate the date and time the bid was received and will be kept on file in the Program Coordinator's office. If technology permits and a management information system is available, this process may be automated, and the requirements of this section fulfilled via the management information system.
Documentation
The Program Coordinator will prepare the bid announcement and verify its accuracy. They shall document the date and time bids are received and document that all bidders are eligible to bid. The Program Coordinator will also maintain records of completion of any training required as a condition to bid.

Section 7 Policy: The Selection Process for Available Vending Facilities
Summary
The Alaska Business Enterprise Program is committed to a fair and equitable process that is administered uniformly when selecting candidates to be placed into available vending facilities. Decisions will be made by an interview panel, and the selection shall be based on an individual’s record of performance and the results of an interview.
The Interview Process
After the candidates have been determined eligible to bid, a selection panel shall interview each candidate. The panel shall consist of the following:
The Business Enterprise Program Coordinator or their designee;
The Chairperson of the Committee of Blind Vendors or their designee; and,
The Program Coordinator and Committee Chairperson jointly determine one person.
The third-panel member may be an independent business person, a property management official, or someone familiar with the Business Enterprise Program.
If the Committee Chair fails for whatever reason to appoint a Manager to serve, the Program Coordinator shall appoint a third member who may or may not be a Vending Facility Manager.
If practical, interviews shall be conducted within ten (10) working days after the bid closing date.
If only one individual bids on a vacant facility, that person shall be awarded the facility without an interview, provided they are otherwise eligible to be assigned to the facility.
Ranking the Candidates
The panel shall rank the candidates based on their performance record for the preceding twelve (12) months, responses received to questions during the
interview, a review of the candidate’s resume, and the business plan submitted by the individual. Each panel member shall rank the candidates according to the following:
First Choice	10 Points
Second Choice.	8 Points
Third Choice	6 Points
Fourth Choice.	4 points
Fifth Choice.	2 points
For purposes of this section, the record of performance shall include the following:
Gross profit, net profit, and labor percentages;
Number of times late filing reports that do not have to be returned for inaccuracies;
Number of times late paying set aside fees;
Number of documented complaints; and,
Other areas as jointly agreed to by the Program Coordinator and Committee.
The ranking shall be done by ballot. The Chairperson of the panel, who shall be the Agency representative, shall collect the ballots and tabulate the rankings, which the other panel members shall verify. The individual with the highest score shall be awarded the vending facility. If there is a tie, the individual with the highest score and greatest seniority shall be awarded the facility. Seniority shall be determined by the
number of months served as an active Manager of a vending facility. The successful candidate shall be immediately offered the award upon the conclusion of interviews.  . The chosen candidate shall have up to twenty-four (24) hours to accept or reject the award. If the award is refused, the individual with the next highest ranking shall be offered the opportunity. If the candidate is scheduled for more than one interview as a result of their bids on a number of facilities contained in a single bid package, then the individual may wait until the conclusion of the last interview in which the individual participates to accept or reject the award of a facility.
Additional Property Management Requirements
It is understood that property management may impose additional selection criteria for Managers on their properties. This may include, but is not limited to, security clearance, special training, and random drug testing if such additional conditions are required of
property management’s contractors. If a Vending Facility Manager or individual on the Certified for Placement List is awarded a facility but subsequently fails to meet the additional selection criteria, the candidate with the next highest score who meets the additional selection criteria will be awarded the facility.
Documentation
The Program Coordinator shall prepare the records of performance for those bidding and shall verify their accuracy. If seniority is used as a tiebreaker, the Program Coordinator shall document the amount of seniority for each bidder.

Section 8 Policy: Manager Reporting and Payment of Set Aside Fees
Summary
It is the policy of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program that those individuals managing a BEP vending facility shall pay a percentage of their net proceeds back to the Agency in the way of a set-aside fee. That fee has been established at five percent (5%) of the Vending Facility Manager’s net proceeds. These funds are deposited into the BEP Revolving Fund and used for the purposes outlined in 34 CFR 395.9.
Procedure
Set-Aside Funds - Method for Determining Set-Aside Rate
The State Licensing Agency and the Committee of Blind Vendors determine an approximate total amount of set-aside funds needed to operate the Program. Based upon that need, a percentage of net proceeds is established as the set-aside rate. The rate shall be high enough to ensure a reasonable reserve.
The Program Coordinator reviews that information and the proposed rate with the Director, who must approve the rate. If the Director does not approve the rate, they shall seek additional active participation from the
Committee in order to achieve agreement on a rate.
The fee schedule is then submitted to the U.S. Department of Education for approval prior to implementation.
The Agency and Committee may agree to suspend Set-Aside after considering the budgetary needs of the Program, including the need for a reasonable reserve.
Determining Net Proceeds
Each Manager is assessed the derived percentage rate of their net proceeds. In determining net proceeds, the Vending Facility Manager is allowed to deduct allowable expenses from gross sales to arrive at the figure. The SLA cannot deny a Manager the ability to deduct legitimate business expenses when determining net proceeds. Expenses that are deducted from gross sales in determining net include but are not limited to:
Product cost, including supplies;
Employee expenses, including wages, benefits, taxes, and related expenses;
Accounting expenses;
Fees including but not limited to bank fees, credit card fees, and micro market fees;
Licensing and/or certification fees;
Advertising;
Pest control;
Janitorial services / Laundry services;
Vehicle mileage for Managers who operate vending routes provided that such mileage is substantiated by a travel log (allowable rate will be the same as allowed by state travel regulations);
Telephone and other communication expenses;
Business and/or Liability insurance;
Office supplies;
Uniforms;
Lease payments, rent, or commissions; and,
Membership to business-related organizations / entities (i.e., Chamber of Commerce, Costco, etc.)
Maintenance and repair co-pays;
Equipment costs and items that do not qualify for federal VR match or use of set aside funds;
Any other business expenses related directly to the operation of the vending facility provided the Agency has given prior approval.
Set-aside payments are not an allowable expense to be deducted.
Use of Set-Aside Funds.
Set-aside funds may be used for only the following purposes:
In accordance with any guidance provided by the U.S. Department of Education, the purchase of new equipment;
The maintenance and replacement of equipment;
Management services;
Assuring a fair minimum return; and/or,
Retirement funds, health insurance contributions, and the provision of vacation and/or sick pay provided a majority of the Managers have voted to do so.
Manager Late Payment Fee
A $25.00 late fee will be assessed if a Manager fails to timely file a monthly report or has an unpaid Set-Aside balance that is not paid by the due date established by this policy manual. If the report is late and there is also an unpaid balance set aside, these are two separate infractions, and there is a $25.00 late fee for each or a total of $50.00. Failure to pay any part of the vending facility bill, including any duly assessed late payment fees, may result in sanctioning
Manager’s license pursuant to 8 AAC 98.400 and shall place a Manager in jeopardy of being removed as the Vending Facility Manager. Late fees are not an allowable deduction for the Manager when determining net proceeds.
All late payment fees and finance charges received by the SLA shall be accounted for as set-aside fees and placed in the BEP Revolving Fund and accounted for as such.
If a Manager’s check for their set aside is returned for insufficient funds, payment will be considered late, and a $25.00 late fee will be assessed. In addition, any charges typically assessed by the State of Alaska for returned checks may be assessed.
Managers must mail monthly reports and payments with a postmark no later than the 25th day of the month following the month that business was transacted. For example, should the due date fall on Saturday or Sunday, payment must be postmarked on the following Monday, or if Monday happens to be a holiday, payment will be expected to be postmarked by Tuesday.
The Agency and Committee may agree to utilize technology that will allow Managers to file reports and possibly pay set-aside fees electronically. This may include traditional email and/or an online management information system. If such technology is available, the due date remains the same. If an online management information system is utilized, the Agency shall ensure the system is accessible to blind users.
Documentation
The financial activities of the vending facility are documented in the monthly report. The Manager must maintain copies of all receipts supporting any deductions on the report. These receipts must be made available to the Agency for inspection and verification upon the request of the Agency.
The Agency shall document late reports and fees and include them on the monthly income statement.
Exceptions
The Program Coordinator may waive any late fees if the Manager experiences unique circumstances that prevent them from filing their reports or paying their set-aside assessments.  Such an exception must be documented in writing by the Program Coordinator and maintained in the Agency files.

Section 10 Policy: Licensed Vending Facility Managers’ Building Management Relationships
Summary
The Licensed Vending Facility Manager and the building manager must have a good working relationship in order to maximize profits and for the vending facility to provide appropriate services to those who occupy the building. The permit to operate the vending facility in the building is between the State Licensing Agency (SLA) and the building management. Jeopardizing that permit is grounds for termination of the operating agreement. The requirements to be included in a permit to operate a vending facility under the Randolph-Sheppard Program are contained in 34 CFR 395.35 (a) and (b).
Procedure
It is the responsibility of the Vending Facility Manager to cooperate, in every way practical, with building management. It must be understood that only by providing a quality service to those individuals who occupy or visit the building can the Business Enterprise Program fulfill its promise to create employment opportunities for Vending Facility Managers. The goal is to always maintain this relationship on a positive level so that the arrangement can be mutually beneficial.
If there are complaints from property management or problems develop in the relationship between the Vending Facility Manager and building management, the SLA should be notified immediately. The Agency operates with the belief that the Vending Facility Manager can resolve most problems without the assistance of the SLA. The purpose of the notification is to simply inform so the Agency can act more expeditiously if the problems persist. The Agency is always available to facilitate an equitable solution to the problem. The SLA shall intervene if the Vending Facility Manager cannot resolve the problems. Both the Vending Facility Manager and building management are encouraged to utilize the services of the SLA early in the process as a means of problem resolution.
There will be times when building management brings problems and/or concerns directly to the SLA. In these instances, the SLA shall reach out to the Vending Facility Manager and take the necessary steps to assist in resolving the matter. Any complaints received in writing from property management shall be provided to the Vending Facility Manager within forty-eight (48) hours in order to expedite the resolution of the problem.
The Vending Facility Manager is not responsible for negotiating items contained in the permit/contract. This rests with the SLA. It is the responsibility of the Vending Facility Manager to comply with all terms of the permit. However, if there are terms in the permit that are problematic for the Vending Facility Manager, the Manager should bring these to the attention of the SLA, who, if justified, can approach property management about amending the permit.
Documentation
If problems persist or a situation is critical, it is beneficial if the parties involved document the situation. This documentation should be presented to the SLA to assist with the resolution of any dispute. The Program Coordinator will document in the file any complaints received and track the resolution thereof.

Section 11 Policy: Failure to Operate a Vending Facility as Stipulated
Summary
It is the policy of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program to ensure that all Vending Facility Managers comply with all program requirements. The State Licensing Agency (SLA) has an obligation to sanction Manager’s license if they fail to comply with said program requirements. This may include the termination of the Manager’s license.
Procedure
Vending Facility Managers will be responsible for adherence to all policies, procedures, and regulations of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program (BEP). A Licensed Vending Facility Manager may have their license placed into disciplinary status by the State Licensing Agency (SLA) for failure to operate their vending facility in accordance with the following:
The rules and regulations governing the Program;
The Policies and Procedures Manual;
The terms and conditions of the permit/contract;
The terms and conditions of the Operating Agreement;
State law, the violation of which is, or reasonably may result in financial or physical harm to the customers of the facility or other persons, the Agency, or the Manager; or
Regulations of other agencies of the State which have regulatory authority directly related to the operation of a vending facility, including but not limited to the Department of Health.
Disciplinary Probation Status
The violation of the above may result in Manager’s license being placed in probationary status for a mandatory period of thirty (30) days. In writing, the SLA must advise the Manager that their license is being placed into probationary status. This written notification must be in an accessible format and include what actions, if any, the Manager must take to avoid revocation of the license. The notice should also advise the Manager of their due process rights if they disagree with the Agency’s actions. During the time the license is in probationary status, the SLA will work closely with and carefully monitor the Vending Facility Manager and the operation of the facility to resolve problems and improve the management of the facility and its operation. A corrective action plan is required when a license is placed into probationary status. This plan for the resolution of the problems shall be developed jointly and agreed to by both the SLA and the Vending Facility Manager. This probationary agreement shall outline the actions necessary to have the license removed from probationary status and avoid further sanctioning. Both the SLA and Vending Facility Manager will sign the probationary agreement.
At the end of the thirty (30) days, the probationary status may be extended for an additional thirty (30) days at the discretion of the Program Coordinator, and a new probationary agreement will be executed. If the conditions that led to the probationary status have not been corrected and the Manager has had ample opportunity to correct them, there is no reason to believe that continued probation will lead to the problems being alleviated; more aggressive action by the SLA may be warranted.
Termination of the License
If the Licensed Vending Facility Manager fails to implement the steps outlined in the agreed-upon corrective action plan and/or the violations persist, the SLA may terminate the license but only after providing thirty (30) days written notice in an accessible format and affording the Licensee an opportunity for a full evidentiary hearing.
In lieu of terminating Manager’s license, the SLA may choose to terminate only the operating agreement and allow the Manager to have their name placed back on the Certified for Placement List. This course of action is only warranted if the SLA believes that circumstances at the vending facility contributed to the problems and there is a reasonable belief that the individual can succeed in another location.
Requests for Removal by Building Management
A request by a building manager to remove a Vending Facility Manager is not in and of itself grounds to remove the Manager. However, if the building manager makes such a request and provides documentation in writing, the Agency shall investigate the validity of the request. After the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency may sanction
Manager’s license and/or remove the Manager on an emergency basis pursuant to 8 AAC 98.350.
This section shall not apply to Managers who operate on properties not protected by Federal or State priority. Under these circumstances, the Agency may be compelled to remove a Manager upon request.
Monies Owed, the Outgoing Manager
If a Manager is removed from a vending facility pursuant to this section or for any other reason, the SLA may owe the outgoing Manager for any inventory overage. However, prior to paying the Manager, the Agency may withhold any monies owed by the Manager to the Agency. For example, if the Agency owes the Manager $1,000 for the inventory overage but the Manager is delinquent $250 in past due set aside fees, the SLA will withhold the $250, which will be deposited into the BEP Revolving Fund and pay the Manager only $750.
Appeals
Any Licensed Vending Facility Manager who disagrees with any action taken by the Agency to sanction their license or remove them from the facility may file a grievance pursuant to Section 20 of this Manual. An administrative review and/or an evidentiary hearing are available to the Licensed Vending Facility Manager. If dissatisfied with the ruling in the evidentiary hearing, the individual may ask the Secretary of Education to convene an arbitration panel to hear the complaint.
Documentation
Any violations by the Manager shall be documented and maintained in the case file for two years. The Program Coordinator shall maintain copies of any written complaints received from property management and shall document in the case file any verbal complaints. The Program Coordinator shall prepare any probationary agreements and document in the case file compliance thereto. The Program Coordinator shall document in writing the justification for extending a probationary status and will likewise justify the termination of the license. The Agency shall maintain on Manager’s permanent record any request by a property management official to remove the Manager. The Program shall document the receipt of any grievances and verify that they were filed timely.

Section 12 Policy: Criteria for Establishing Vending Facilities
Summary
It is the responsibility of the Alaska BEP to create and expand opportunities for the blind through the establishment of vending facilities. It is the policy of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program to create opportunities that will sustain a livelihood for blind persons licensed to manage and operate vending facilities in the state. This requires establishing guidelines on sales thresholds for stand-alone and satellite facilities.
Procedure
Criteria To Establish New Stand-Alone Facilities
It is the responsibility of the Agency to stay abreast of potential new vending facility locations in the state. When potential sites are identified, the staff person will contact the appropriate property management official and request permission to conduct a formal survey and make a presentation about the Alaska Business Enterprise Program. In identifying potential facility sites, the following criteria will be considered:
The facility will be expected to generate sales that will produce an income to the Manager which is equal to or greater than the average per capita earnings of all persons in the state. Exceptions to this criteria may be made provided the Program Coordinator and Committee agrees. Sales meeting this criteria shall be based on the following:
Historical data that is available with respect to that location; and/or,
Sales projections are based on the number of potential customers
and the type of facility requested. Generally, a minimum of 500 potential customers will be required for a stand-alone facility.
It is understood that the Committee of Blind Vendors has an important role to play when decisions are being made about potential new locations. The Program Coordinator is expected to keep Committee representatives appraised of activities in this area and shall solicit appropriate input. This will include following up on leads of prospective sites, which are provided to the staff by the Committee representative.
The facility should generate sufficient set-aside fees for a return on investment (ROI) to the Agency; however, ROI alone should not drive the decision.
If it is determined that the above criteria have been met, then the Program Coordinator will prepare a recommendation which shall be submitted internally for approval. The final approval will be contingent upon the availability of budgeted funds and a determination that a facility will be economically feasible and in the best interest of the Program as a whole.
After the decision to proceed with negotiations to establish a vending facility, the Program Coordinator will initiate the permit and secure the necessary signatures. After approval has been obtained from the property management officials and the Department, the Program Coordinator shall initiate a Plan of Service to purchase equipment and initial stock as soon as it is mutually agreeable to all concerned parties that the facility will open on a predetermined date. From time to time, it becomes necessary to combine buildings in order to make a facility large enough to support a Manager.
Additions to Existing Facilities
If it is determined that a potential new location does not meet the criteria for a stand-alone facility and will not support a Manager, the Program Coordinator may consider assigning the new location, either temporarily or permanently, to an existing facility as a satellite. It may be assigned temporarily if the Agency wants to establish some historical sales data upon which to make a decision
about a permanent assignment or if there is a possibility in the future of using the location to establish a facility that does meet the sales threshold. This temporary assignment may be up to twelve (12) months pursuant to 8 AAC 98.370(h). If the assignment is to be temporary, the Program Coordinator shall announce the availability to all Managers and individuals on the Certified for Placement List. The procedures outlined in Policy 6.2 will be followed. If assigned on a temporary basis, the Manager will be responsible for keeping separate sales figures.
Projecting Facility Sales
In attempting to accurately project the gross sales of a particular facility, a number of factors always need to be examined to determine the amount each potential customer would spend in the facility daily. The following are the criteria which the Program Coordinator uses:
Historical Data - If historical sales figures are available, this data will be given considerable weight in projecting sales, although it will not be the sole determining factor as circumstances and types of service may have changed.
Population Count - The Agency will consider the number of potential customers, including transient customers and visitors. The number of shifts of employees within a twenty-four (24) hour period will also be taken into consideration.
The Program Coordinator will consider the type of service to be provided (i.e., vending, counter, food preparation, micro market, etc.) and the degree of vicinity competition.
Based on the information derived from the examination of these factors, the Program Coordinator will make their projection of annual gross sales predicated upon no less than $1.00 per day per potential customer. Note that this figure may rise substantially if the vending facility includes on-site food preparation and even higher if vicinity competition poses no problem. In these instances, a minimum of $1.50 may be utilized.
Documentation
The Program Coordinator's responsibility is to document the information on all bid announcements. The Program Coordinator shall document sales projections based on the number of customers and type of service and ensure the accuracy of these projections. The Program Coordinator will have the same responsibilities for documenting the bidding process as required by Policy 6.3.
Exceptions
Exceptions on the formula for sales projections can be made if the information available suggests the projections need to be adjusted by the Agency after receiving the active participation of the Committee.

Section 13 Policy: Vending Facility Equipment: Purchase, Repair, Replacement, and Disposal
Summary
The equipment necessary for the effective and efficient operation of each vending facility will be made available by the SLA. Purchase, repair, and replacement of equipment will be financed from the Business Enterprise Program (BEP) fund to the extent indicated in the procedures section of this policy.
The SLA will administer the purchase, repair, replacement and disposal of vending facility equipment.
The Alaska Division of Vocational Rehabilitation will retain the right, title, and interest in all BEP program equipment and fixtures. It will have the authority to direct and control transfers and dispose of such equipment when necessary.
A Vending Facility Manager may not make additions to, or reductions of, equipment provided to the vending facility without the prior written approval of the SLA.
Procedure
Additions and Deletions of Equipment:
Adaptive Equipment for Use by the Vending Facility Manager:
If the Vending Facility Manager needs adaptive equipment or aids in order to effectively manage their vending facility, and that equipment or aids are unique to that individual and are required to perform the duties of a Vending Facility Manager, it is necessary that they be purchased with "case service" funds by the Vocational Rehabilitation Counselor. The VRC may only make an expenditure of these funds in accordance with the current VR policy.
Equipment Necessary for the Operation of the Vending Facility
It is the responsibility of the SLA to provide the equipment necessary for the efficiency of all vending facilities. This includes the replacement of worn-out or damaged equipment. Title to all equipment is vested in the State of Alaska and is provided for use by the Manager while operating the vending facility. The Manager shall return the equipment to the Agency in good condition once they cease to operate as the Manager of the vending facility.
Vending Facility Managers will not make additions to, or reductions of, equipment provided to the vending facility without prior approval of the SLA. The Vending Facility Manager will make requests to make changes to the SLA, who will ensure that the additions are consistent with the permit and there is adequate space and utilities to accommodate the new equipment.
Maintenance of Equipment:
The Vending Facility Manager shall maintain, or cause to be maintained, all equipment. The SLA shall replace worn-out or obsolete equipment, which is required for the continued successful operation of the vending facility.
Care and Ongoing Maintenance of Equipment:
Vending Facility Managers will take reasonable care of equipment and provide routine day-to-day maintenance of the equipment.
Payment for Repairs of Equipment:
The SLA shall pay a reasonable cost of repair for:
The first six months of operation of a vending facility (either a new Vending Facility Manager or a new vending facility).
The first three months of operation for equipment transferred from one vending facility to another.
The first 30 days for equipment in a vending facility managed by a Vending Facility Manager promoted or transferred from another existing vending facility.
Following completion of the periods stated above, the Licensed Vending Facility Manager will be responsible for up to the first $50 of repair costs, with the SLA paying repair costs in excess of $50 when the program authorizes the repair in advance. The SLA will pay the full cost of the repair and then bill the Manager for the $50.00 co-pay. The Manager shall submit payment on or before the date the monthly report is due subsequent to the Manager being billed.
Reporting Damage, Breakage, Theft, and/or Malfunction:
Vending Facility Managers are responsible for reporting all incidents of damage, breakage, theft, and malfunction of equipment or fixtures to the Program Coordinator at the first opportunity.
Repair, which the SLA has not authorized, is the sole responsibility of the Vending Facility Manager except for emergency repairs to avoid the loss of perishable items or to endanger life or property.
Emergency Repairs of Equipment:
The Vending Facility Manager is authorized to arrange for emergency repairs to avoid loss of perishable stocks or to avoid endangering life or property.
Unauthorized Repair of Equipment:
The repair will be deemed unauthorized when:
The Vending Facility Manager neglects or fails to notify the SLA or its designated of damage, breakage, theft, defacement, or malfunction of equipment or fixtures in the vending facility.
The repairs are attributable to negligent actions by the Vending Facility Manager / Trainee or facility employees.
The maintenance of the equipment or fixtures is not the responsibility of the Alaska BEP Program.
Documentation
Documentation of equipment repairs, replacement, or disposal for each vending facility will be maintained in a file on that facility.

Section 14 Policy: Merchandise Inventory
Date:
Summary
It is the policy of the Alaska BEP to ensure that all Vending Facility Managers have adequate merchandise to begin operations. Responsibility for paying for this startup inventory rests with the SLA.
Establishing and Providing Initial Inventory Levels
In determining the amount of merchandise to be purchased as an initial inventory level for a vending facility, the Agency must rely upon its projections for annual gross sales. It is important to realize that an initial inventory level may not be sufficient to allow a facility's efficient operation over a long period of time. It is, however, in such an amount to permit the Manager to begin operating. The Manager is expected to invest from the facility's profits to increase the inventory as circumstances require. Generally, an initial inventory level shall equal two weeks of gross sales for a non-food prep operation and three weeks of gross sales in a food prep facility. For example, if sales are projected to be $3,000 per week in an all-vending location, the initial inventory level should be equal to $6,000. In a food prep facility, the initial inventory level would be
$9,000. Depending upon unique circumstances at a facility, this amount may need to be adjusted. Initial levels of inventory will not be increased by the Agency unless there is a change in the nature of the facility in question or unless there is a re-assignment of the
facility to a new Manager. An example of a change of nature would be converting an automated vending operation into a snack bar or micro market.
The Vocational Rehabilitation Counselor (VRC) will provide the initial merchandise inventory when a VR client accepts their first assignment of the Certified for Placement List. The Manager will be expected to maintain, as a minimum, that inventory level in the vending facility at all times, taking into account seasonal variations. When that Manager moves to another vending facility, they will be expected to take that inventory amount with them. If the new facility requires more inventory than the amount provided by the VRC, the Agency shall provide the additional inventory. That becomes part of the Manager's initial inventory level, and they will be expected to take that amount to their next assignment.
When a Manager leaves a vending facility and there is more inventory than the initial level, the Manager has the option of taking the overage with them to their new assignment, or the Agency will pay the Manager for the additional inventory, provided the package is unopened and is within the sell-by date. The additional inventory will be made available to the incoming Manager. Under no circumstances will a Manager or individual on the Certified for Placement List be denied an opportunity because of their inability to pay for additional inventory.
Documentation
The Agency shall maintain in the facility file records of the amount of inventory provided by the Vocational Rehabilitation Counselor and any increases as the result of assuming a new assignment. The Agency shall conduct periodic checks (at least annually) to verify that the minimum inventory amount is being maintained.
Exceptions
Exceptions to the amount of inventory may be made if circumstances warrant. Depending on the facility's demands and Manager's circumstances, more or less inventory may be provided. For example, if people move out of a building resulting in fewer customers, the Manager may need to reduce the inventory to prevent spoilage.

Section 15 Policy: Criteria for Using and Selecting Teaming Partners
Summary
Some vending facility operations are very complex and require large capital investments and cash flow that most Managers do not enjoy. Cafeterias are the best example, especially military troop dining contracts. Other examples may be utilizing a branded concept such as Subway or placing some vending machines on full-service. The Agency may require the use of a teaming partner or allow a Manager to utilize a teaming partner upon request. The Manager shall be allowed to select a teaming partner with the approval of the Agency unless the teaming arrangement is already in place when the Manager is assigned to the facility. If there is already a teaming partner, the incoming Manager may be required to utilize that teaming partner until the expiration of the agreement with the teaming partner or a contract for service, such as a military dining contract, is rebid by the Federal entity. The SLA expects the Vending Facility Manager to be engaged in all aspects of the business, including day-to-day management decisions.
Procedure
If a teaming partner is required for a cafeteria operation or full-service vending, the Agency shall provide the Manager with a list of potential teaming partners it has approved. If a branded concept is contemplated, the Agency and Manager will work collaboratively to select a franchisee.
Criteria for a Military Dining Teaming Partner
In selecting a teaming partner, the Manager must ensure that the following criteria are met:
The teaming partner has experience in the type of service to be operated by the Manager.
The teaming partner has demonstrated that they have the financial resources to support the operation, including cash flow to pay employees while waiting on payment from the government.
The agreement between the Manager and teaming partner shall be based on profit sharing, with the Manager receiving a minimum of 51% of the profit from the operation. A guaranteed draw is permissible as long as it can be documented that the guarantee represents at least 51% of the profit.
The agreement between the Manager and teaming partner must show the training that will be provided to the Manager by the teaming partner. The Manager is required to be trained in all aspects of the operation.
The Manager must be engaged in the business's day-to-day operations and an active participant in all major decisions. This requires the Vending Facility Manager to be on-site on a weekly basis and more if the demands of the facility require it.
The Agency must approve the teaming partner agreement.
Full-Service Vending
As noted earlier, another type of teaming arrangement is a full-service vending agreement. Generally, the vending teaming partner is only responsible for a portion of the vending facility operation. An example might be a Manager who is managing a cafeteria may choose to utilize a teaming partner to service the vending machines. Another example might be a Manager who services their own vending machines at their anchor facility but chooses to use a full-service company for a satellite facility several miles away. A third example might be a Manager who contracts with one of the major drink bottlers to service drink machines while the Manager services all other vending machines. In this type of
teaming arrangement, the Manager is engaged in the day-to-day oversight of the vending machines and is still responsible for ensuring quality service. The Manager will visit the facility at least weekly to inspect the quality of service and to engage with property management to gauge satisfaction with the level of service. The Manager earns a percentage of the sales in the way of a commission. The Program Coordinator may approve any such full-service arrangements if it is a good business decision for both the Agency and the Manager, and the third party can provide quality service. In approving such arrangements, the Program Coordinator shall ensure that the Manager is engaged in the business as a whole. Under no circumstances will a Manager be allowed to place an entire vending facility on full-service. Any Manager with machines on full-service at the time these rules are implemented shall be grandfathered in as long as they are at that facility.
Branded Concepts
In those facilities where a Manager utilizes a branded partner such as Subway, the Manager must provide oversight to the branded operation to ensure compliance with program requirements. It is not the intent of this section to establish a branded operation that constitutes Manager's entire vending facility. The Manager is still expected to service a portion of their facilities, such as the vending machines or over-the-counter operation. The Manager and SLA will jointly make decisions on allowing a branded franchise to operate as part of the vending facility and negotiate a competitive commission. There may be situations where a Manager bids into a facility with a branded concept already there. In such instances, the Manager is obligated to continue to work with that partner for the remainder of any contract period.
Documentation
The Agency shall develop a list of approved teaming partners based on this Policy's criteria. The Agency shall do its due diligence to review the record of any potential teaming partners to ensure they are capable of providing the desired service.	Any teaming agreement must be approved in writing by the Agency. Such written approval ensures compliance with all criteria outlined in this section. The Agency shall ensure compliance with the agreement, including assuring that the Manager receives the required training and is actively engaged in the day-to-day management of the business.
Exceptions
In regards to teaming partners on military troop dining contracts, circumstances may require the Agency to select a teaming partner before assigning it to a Blind Manager. Under such circumstances, the Manager shall be required to utilize that teaming partner until the Federal entity rebids the contract for service.

Section 16 Policy: Benefits Package for Licensed Vending Facility Managers
Summary
Licensed Vending Facility Managers are not employees of the State of Alaska and are self-employed entrepreneurs. As such, they are not eligible for fringe benefits most state employees enjoy. However, Congress has earmarked certain revenues which the SLA can utilize to provide certain benefits to Licensed Vending Facility Managers. The benefit package may, with the majority vote of all Licensed Vending Facility Managers, include any or all of the following benefits:
Health insurance contributions
Retirement or pension plan
Paid sick leave and vacation time through the provision of dollars to hire additional staff.
The funds for payment of the benefits package will come from the BEP unassigned vending machine income, provided funds are available. Nothing in this section shall be interpreted to require the provision of any of these benefits. The Licensed Vending Facility Managers must determine by a
majority vote the acceptance of the proposed package. The vote must be taken at the meeting held during the 1st quarter of the calendar year.
The Licensed Vending Facility Manager will be eligible to accrue benefits at the time the operating contract is signed. Individuals who are not licensed and who are fulfilling their initial 6-month assignment are not eligible for benefits until they are issued a license after successfully completing the probationary performance period.
Procedure
Vacation Benefits
Each Licensed Vending Facility Manager may be paid as a part of the benefits package upon application for and approval of annual leave. This shall be an amount equal to the accrued value of the cost of hiring additional staff for fifteen
(15) working days each year.
The money to hire such staff is based on $18.00 per hour, 8 hours per day, totaling $2160 per year. Payments of $540.00 will be made on a quarterly basis. As noted earlier, these payments are subject to the availability of funds.
Insurance:
Each Licensed Vending Facility Manager may receive in the benefits package the basic health insurance provided through the State of Alaska to political subdivision employees. Additional coverage for a spouse and/or children may be purchased through contributions by the Vending Facility Manager. In lieu of utilizing this state political subdivision program, the Agency and Committee may explore alternative plans that meet the requirements of the Chance Act.
Retirement:
Each Licensed Vending Facility Manager may be provided with a retirement program of their choice. Based upon the availability of funds, the BEP will provide up to $2,000 per year toward their desired retirement package. This may be paid directly to the Licensed Vending Facility Manager or a retirement program of their choice. If the check is made directly to the Licensed Vending Facility Manager, the state must provide the Licensed Vending Facility Manager with a 1099 form for tax purposes. If it is paid directly to a retirement program, a form 1099 is not required.
Payment toward retirement will be prorated for any Licensed Vending Facility Managers who have been with the program for less than one year. Payment will
be made on or before April 1 of each year.
Documentation
The SLA or its designated nominee agency will maintain in the case file of each Licensed Vending Facility Manager an accounting of the benefits package unique to that individual. Any changes in that package will be documented.

Section 17 Policy: Assurance of a Fair Minimum Return
Summary
An individual in their initial assignment who is required to complete a 6-month performance probationary period is assured a fair minimum return that ensures the
individual earns at least an amount equal to Alaska’s minimum wage for a month. The amount will be calculated based on 40 hours per week multiplied by 4.3 to arrive at the monthly amount. Additionally, a fair minimum return is granted when:
The facility is new and has no proven history of profitability;
The Program Coordinator determines that additional support is critically important in order to establish or re-establish the viability of a facility with a recent history of unprofitability for an identifiable cause other than vendor mismanagement. This option may only be used when reasonable expectation dictates that the facility will be restored to its previously profitable status; or,
There is a natural disaster or state of emergency, such as a worldwide pandemic, that impacts the business of the vending facilities.
The fair minimum return dollar amount may be increased if circumstances require such an adjustment and there are budgeted funds to support an increase. An example of this
occurring was the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic, when most vending facilities were partially or entirely closed, and the amount was increased to help sustain the Managers during the crisis. Any adjustment must be agreed to by the Agency and Committee of Blind Vendors.
Procedure
At the beginning of every year, the Program Coordinator shall calculate the amount of the Fair Minimum Return based on the methodology described in Policy 17.1 above. This amount shall be communicated to the Vending Facility Managers and persons on the Certified for Placement List.
For those Managers possibly eligible for the fair minimum return, the Program Coordinator shall review the monthly reports submitted by the Manager. If the profits reported are less than the established fair minimum amount, the Program Coordinator shall initiate a direct payment to the Manager in an amount equal to the difference between the actual profit and the fair minimum return amount. For example, if the fair minimum is established at $1,900.00 per month and the Manager reports a net profit of $1,200.00, the Program Coordinator shall initiate a payment of $700.00 to the Manager.
Documentation
The Program Coordinator shall document the amount of the fair minimum return each year. The Coordinator shall review monthly reports to verify their accuracy to the extent practicable based upon an in-office review. If the Coordinator questions a report, the Manager shall provide documentation in support of the information previously provided in the report.
Exceptions
Exceptions to this procedure may be made by prior mutual agreement of the Vending Facility Manager and the SLA for extenuating circumstances. The amount of the fair minimum return can be increased if jointly agreed to by all Vending Facility Managers, and extraordinary circumstances justify it.

Section 18 Policy: Payment of Moving Costs by the State Licensing Agency
Summary
The State Licensing Agency (SLA) Vocational Rehabilitation (VR) Counselor will pay the personal moving costs of individuals on the Certified for Placement List who are receiving their initial assignment, which is at least one hundred (100) miles from their current place of residence. Each eligible VR client seeking moving assistance is responsible for acquiring approval for payment of moving costs before making actual arrangements for and/or committing to the move. Approval may be acquired through the SLA. Payment will be based on
available funds and will not exceed the amount allowable for moving state employees.
Documentation
The DVR case file will contain copies of all documents used to request and receive approval for personal travel costs. It will also include complete documentation of the cost of the move with details concerning what was moved and the method of moving the personal effects of the manager.

Section 20 Policy: Due Process
Date:
Summary
It is the policy of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program to afford any Blind Licensee who is dissatisfied with any Agency action arising from the operation and administration of the BEP an opportunity for due process. The stages available to the Blind Licensee are:
Administrative Review
Full Evidentiary Hearing
Federal Arbitration
Procedure
Administrative Review
Any Blind Licensee, who is dissatisfied with an Agency action, or if appropriate, may request, through the individual’s representative, an Administrative Review. An Administrative Review is the first optional step in the dispute resolution process and is conducted by a Department employee who is appointed by the Chief of Rehabilitation Services to act as the Administrative Review Officer and who has had no previous involvement in the case. Participation in an
Administrative Review does not waive the individual’s rights, nor is it a prerequisite for an Impartial Due Process Hearing.
The SLA must pay the costs of the Administrative Review. The Agency is not required to pay for any costs related to the representation of an applicant or recipient.
An individual may make a request, in writing or through another accessible format, for an Administrative Review, to the ADVR Administrative Review Office within thirty (30) calendar days of the disputed Agency action. The request must contain a clear and concise statement of the action being appealed, describing the reason for the appeal and the actions requested to resolve the dispute. The request should indicate the appeal option selected (Administrative Review or Impartial Due Process Hearing). The Licensee should describe any ADA accommodation requested for the appeal process, be signed and dated by the individual, and include their contact information. Alternatively, the individual may submit the Administrative Review, Mediation, and/or Impartial Due Process Hearing Request Form.
Even though the individual may not have specifically requested an Impartial Due Process Hearing, the receipt of the request for a review of an Agency action initiates the sixty (60) - calendar-day period in which an Impartial Due Process Hearing must be conducted, unless both parties agree to an extension.
The Administrative Review Officer will either conduct the review or delegate the responsibility to another Department employee appointed by the Chief of Rehabilitation Services to act as the Administrative Review Officer, who has had no previous involvement in the case.
The Administrative Review Officer will ensure that the Administrative Review will be conducted within fifteen (15) calendar days of receipt of the original request or at a time mutually agreed to by the parties involved, ensuring that the entire appeal process (up to and including an Impartial Due Process Hearing) can be completed within sixty (60) calendar days unless the parties agree to a specific extension of time.
The Administrative Review will be held at a time and place convenient to the individual, generally at the regional Vocational Rehabilitation office.
The Administrative Review Officer will coordinate the process. The Agency will provide the necessary accommodations. A foreign language interpreter will be provided for those individuals who cannot communicate in English.
Failure of the individual to appear at the scheduled Administrative Review or to be available for a telephone interview is a waiver of the right to a review. The Administrative Review Officer may dispose of the matter, or if the defaulted party submits a request for rescheduling within fifteen (15) calendar days with good cause, the Administrative Review will be rescheduled.  Failure to appear at the scheduled Administrative Review or to be available for an interview does not preclude a Blind Licensed Manager from requesting an evidentiary hearing.
The Administrative Review Officer will make an effort to render a decision and to develop a written agreement with the individual by the close of the Administrative Review. If this is not possible, the Administrative Review Officer must notify the individual in writing of the decision within seven calendar days of the Administrative Review unless circumstances require additional time. If that occurs, the Administrative Review Officer will notify the individual and the Chief of Rehabilitation Services of the delay and the timeframe for the decision.
If the Administrative Review Officer is unable to resolve the matter to the satisfaction of the individual, the Administrative Review Officer will provide the individual with information on their right to an Impartial Due Process Hearing. The individual must notify the Agency within (15) calendar days of the date of the Administrative Review decision that they wish to proceed with an Impartial Due Process Hearing.
The results of the Administrative Review are binding for the Agency unless the decision of the Administrative Review Officer is not permitted by law or the individual requests an Impartial Due Process Hearing.
Impartial Due Process Hearing
An Impartial Due Process Hearing is a formal review of an Agency action before an Administrative Law Judge. An individual dissatisfied with any Agency action arising from the operation or administration of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program may request an Impartial Due Process Hearing without going through an Administrative Review.
The Agency must pay the costs of the Impartial Due Process Hearing. The Agency is not required to pay for any costs related to the representation of an applicant or recipient. Nor is the Agency required to pay monetary damages due to the sovereign immunity protection afforded to the State by the Eleventh Amendment of the US Constitution.
An individual may make a request, in writing or through another accessible format, for Impartial Due Process Hearing, to the ADVR Administrative Review Officer within thirty (30) calendar days of the disputed action or within fifteen (15) days of receiving a decision by the Administrative Review Officer. The request must contain a clear and concise statement of the action being appealed, describing the reason for the appeal and the actions requested to resolve the dispute. The request should indicate the appeal option selected (Administrative Review or Impartial Due Process Hearing). The Licensee should describe any ADA accommodation requested for the appeal process, be signed and dated by the individual, and include their contact information. Alternatively, the individual may submit the Administrative Review, Mediation, and/or Impartial Due Process Hearing Request Form.
The Impartial Due Process Hearing shall be conducted within sixty (60)
calendar days of receipt of the individual’s request for review unless the informal resolution is achieved prior to the 60th day or the parties agree to an extension.
The Office of Administrative Hearings (OAH) will coordinate the process. The Agency will provide the necessary accommodations. A foreign language interpreter will be provided for those individuals who cannot communicate in English.
The hearing will be recorded, or a transcript will be provided to both parties.
The hearing must be held before an Administrative Law Judge.
The Administrative Law Judge shall issue a written report of the findings and decision of the Impartial Due Process Hearing within thirty (30) calendar days of the completion of the hearing.
The decision of the Administrative Law Judge shall be considered final but is subject to appeal by the Blind Licensee pursuant to Subsection 3 below.
Federal Arbitration
Any blind vendor dissatisfied with any action taken or decision rendered as a result may file a complaint with the Secretary of the US Department of Education pursuant to 34 CFR 395.13(a). The complaint shall be accompanied by all available supporting documents, including a statement of the decision which was rendered and the reasons in support thereof. If the complaint meets the criteria established by the Secretary, they shall convene an arbitration panel pursuant to
34 CFR 395.13(c), and the proceedings shall be guided by 34 CFR 395.13(d) thru
(h). An arbitration panel decision is a final agency action subject to judicial review.
Documentation
The Program Coordinator shall log in any requests and ensure their timeliness. The individuals conducting the administrative review and/or impartial due process hearing shall produce a report of their findings. A recording and/or transcript will be provided for all impartial due process hearings.
Exceptions
Some timelines can be modified with mutual agreement of the Licensed Vending Facility Manager and the Agency.

Section 22 Policy: Financial Management of the Alaska Business Enterprise Program
Summary
It is the intent of the State Licensing Agency (SLA) that the funds derived from unassigned vending machine commissions, grants, levied set-aside funds, and late fees are used to maintain the program and that these funds are sufficient to make the program self-sustaining to the extent practicable.
As funds are available, the SLA may designate Vocational Rehabilitation funds (Section 110) to be used for costs associated with the management of the BEP. Section 110 funds will not be used for individual Licensed Vending Facility Managers' benefits, liability insurance, or any other cost directly related to the operation of the Business Enterprise Program (BEP) vending facilities. They may be used for inventory for the first six months after a Manager is placed into a vending facility, new vending facility equipment, replacement of vending facility equipment, repair and maintenance of vending facilities and/or equipment, startup costs (six months), licenses, permits, renovation costs required to accommodate new equipment, architectural fees, consultants, auditors, and continuing education training.
Procedure
Revenue:
The BEP program in Alaska can be funded from four sources, to include:
Vending Machine Income
The program, by both Federal and State statutes, is allowed to realize income from vending machines not operated by blind vendors on both Federal and State properties. For Federal property, 34 CFR 395.32 controls, and the Agency shall comply with the Rehabilitation Services Administration's Technical Assistance Circular 21-02.
Income derived from vending machine commissions generated from facilities in federally owned or leased buildings will be accounted for separately from any income derived from locations from property other than federal to ensure compliance with 34 CFR 395.8.
Vending machine income is distributed as follows:
Machines in Direct Competition: Whether on State or Federal property, if a vending machine is in the same building with a Manager, it is considered to be in direct competition with that Manager, and the net proceeds from the machine accrue to the Licensed Vending Facility Manager(s) in an amount not to exceed the lesser of (1) the average net income of all Licensed Vending Facility Managers within the state determined each federal fiscal year on the basis of the prior federal fiscal year's operation, or (2) an amount equal to the average net income of all blind Vending Facility Managers in the United States determined on the basis of the prior fiscal year's operation. Any commissions that exceed these limits or are received from vending machines, not in direct competition with a Manager shall be retained by the Agency and expended in accordance with federal regulations and this Policy and Procedures Manual.
Set-Aside Income
Each Licensed Vending Facility Manager will contribute through a set-aside assessment to  the revolving fund maintained by the state licensing agency. The amount of the set-aside assessment is established by the SLA with the active participation of the Committee of Blind Vendors and will be determined at the end of each fiscal year for the next fiscal year. The set-aside amount to be paid by each Vending Facility Manager is computed as a percentage of the monthly net proceeds of the vending facility. That amount is currently set at 5% of the Manager's net proceeds.
Funds from the set-aside may be expended for subsections (a) through (e) above in accordance with 34 CFR 395.9.
Penalties
Funds will be generated from late fees assessed against Managers. The terms and
conditions for payment of these fines and penalties are explained in Policy 8.2.
Section 110 Funds
Funds from the Federal Section 110 program may be used for the administration of the BEP program by the SLA pursuant to 34 C.F.R. 361.49(a)(5)
Unassigned Vending Machine Income from Non-Federal Property
Any funds that accrue to the Agency from vending machines on non-federal property where there is no blind Manager may be used for:
Retirement, health insurance, and vacation/sick pay for active Licensed Vending Facility Managers which are not eligible for federal match.
Maintenance and replacement of equipment.
Purchase of new equipment.
Management services.
Assuring a fair minimum return to new Managers during the first six months of their probationary period with the understanding that such expenditures cannot be used to match federal dollars .
Initial inventory, supplies, and other startup costs.
Items that do not meet the capitalization threshold required by the U.S. Department of Education to be considered equipment with the understanding that such expenditures cannot be used to match federal dollars except for the first six months of operation.
Costs associated with preparing space to accommodate new facilities.
Costs of renovation and remodeling of existing vending facilities.
Federal Unassigned
Any income accruing to the SLA pursuant to 34 CFR 395.32 shall be expended in accordance with 34 CFR 395.8.
Documentation
All receipts and expenditures will be accounted for separately for each unassigned location and/or vending facility assigned to a Blind Manager.

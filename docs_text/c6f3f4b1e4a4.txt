DECISION AND AWARD
This case comes before a tripartite arbitration panel ("the Panel") convened by the United States Department of Education ("USDOE") pursuant to the Randolph-Sheppard Act, 20 U.S.C. §107 et seq. ("RSA") and implementing regulations at 34 C.F.R. §395.13. As agreed by the Parties, a hearing in this matter was held via the Zoom videoconferencing platform on July 25, 2023, August 21, 2023 and October 24, 2023. At the hearing, the parties were given the opportunity to call and cross-examine witnesses and to present documentary evidence. The witnesses were sworn. The parties presented oral opening statements and written post-hearing briefs. Mr. Mitchell also submitted a written opening statement and closing statement. A transcript of the proceeding was prepared by the court reporter. Cedric Mitchell ("Mr. Mitchell" or "the Mr. Mitchell") appeared pro se in this proceeding. John Williams, Esq. represented the Louisiana Workforce Commission ("LWC" or "Respondent"). Testimony was given by Michaelle (Duncan) Cox, Steven DeBruhl, Herbert Reado, Charles Monk, and Melissa Bayham. Cedric Mitchen elected not to testify at the hearing. In reaching its decision, the Panel considered the evidentiary record comprised of the testimony and documentary evidence presented at the hearing, and the arguments of the parties.
The Issues Before the Arbitration Panel
By letter dated February 27, 2023, DOE Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services, Rehabilitation Services Administration Deputy Commissioner Carol Dobak authorized the convening of the Panel to hear and render a decision on the following issues raised in the complaint filed by Mr. Mitchell:
The central issues to be addressed are whether the LWC violated the act and implementing regulations at 34 C.F.R. § 395.7(b) and §395.11 by not providing remediation and/or training to Mr. Mitchell in the operation of a micro market kiosk to enable him to remain in the BEP, by not providing him with several documents in an accessible format, and by not providing post-employment training services.

Randolph-Sheppard Act
20 U.S.C. §107. Operation of vending facilities
Authorization
For the purposes of providing blind persons with remunerative employment, enlarging the economic opportunities of the blind, and stimulating the blind to greater efforts in striving to make themselves self­ supporting, blind persons licensed under the provisions of this chapter shall be authorized to operate vending facilities on any Federal property.
Preferences regulations; justification for limitation on operation
In authorizing the operation of vending facilities on Federal property, priority shall be given to blind persons licensed by a State agency as provided in this chapter; and the Secretary, through the Commissioner, shall, after consultation with the Administrator of General Services and other heads of departments, agencies, or instrumentalities of the United States in control of the maintenance, operation, and protection of Federal property, prescribe regulations designed to assure that-
the priority under this subsection is given to such licensed blind persons (including assignment of vending machine income pursuant to section 107d-3 of this title to achieve and protect such priority), and
wherever feasible, one or more vending facilities are established on all Federal property to the extent that any such facility or facilities would not adversely affect the interests of the United States.
20 U.S. Code § 107b. Application for designation as State licensing agency; cooperation with Secretary; furnishing initial stock
A State agency for the blind or other State agency desiring to be designated as the licensing agency shall, with the approval of the chief executive of the State, make application to the Secretary and agree-
to cooperate with the Secretary in carrying out the purpose of this chapter;
to provide for each licensed blind person such vending facility equipment, and adequate initial stock of suitable articles to be vended therefrom, as may be necessary: Provided, however, That such equipment and stock may be owned by the licensing agency for use of the blind, or by the blind individual to whom the license is issued: And provided further, That if ownership of such equipment is vested in the blind licensee, (A) the State licensing agency shall retain a first option to repurchase such equipment and (B) in the event such individual dies or for any other reason ceases to be a licensee or transfers to another vending facility, ownership of such equipment shall become vested in the State licensing agency (for transfer to a successor licensee) subject to an obligation on the part of the State licensing agency to pay to such individual (or to his estate) the fair value of his interest therein as later determined in accordance with regulations of the State licensing agency and after opportunity for a fair hearing;
that if any funds are set aside, or caused to be set aside, from the net proceeds of the operation of the vending facilities such funds shall be set aside, or caused to be set aside, only to the extent necessary for and may be used only for the purposes of(A) maintenance and replacement of equipment; (B) the purchase of new equipment; (C) management services; (D) assuring a fair minimum return to operators of vending facilities; and (E) retirement or pension funds, health insurance contributions, and provision for paid sick leave and vacation time, if it is determined by a majority vote of blind licensees licensed by such State agency, after such agency provides to each such licensee full information on all matters relevant to such proposed program, that funds under this paragraph shall be set aside for such purposes: Provided, however, That in no event shall the amount of such funds to be set aside from the net proceeds of any vending facility exceed a reasonable amount which shall be determined by the Secretary;
to make such reports in such form and containing such information as the Secretary may from time to time require and to comply with such provisions as he may from time to time find necessary to assure the correctness and verification of such reports;
to issue such regulations, consistent with the provisions of this chapter, as may be necessary for the operation of this program;
to provide to any blind licensee dissatisfied with any action arising from the operation or administration of the vending facility program an opportunity for a fair hearing, and to agree to submit the grievances of any blind licensee not otherwise resolved by such hearing to arbitration as provided in section 107d-l of this title.

Findings of Fact
The Louisiana Workforce Commission ("LWC") has been designated by the U.S. Department of Education ("USDOE") as the State Licensing Agency charged with administering the Randolph-Sheppard program in Louisiana pursuant to 20 U.S.C 107 et seq. and its implementing regulations found at 34 CFR Part 395.
The Louisiana Randolph-Sheppard program operates in accordance with duly promulgated regulations found at Title 67 Section VI Chapter 5 of the Louisiana Administrative Code and the Technical Assistance and Guidance Manual ("TAG Manual"). The TAG Manual contains unpublished sub regulatory guidance issued by the LWC.
LWC receives federal funding through the Randolph-Sheppard program, and it is therefore subject to the requirements of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, as amended.
Pursuant to 34 CFR § 395.311, the United States Department of Education ("USDOE") is required to approve all state Randolph-Sheppard rules and regulations. The USDOE approved Louisiana's regulations in a letter dated February 23, 2015. (CX-21).
Mr. Cedric Mitchell is a blind vendor licensed by LWC to manage vending facilities in Louisiana's Randolph-Sheppard vending program. At the time of events relevant to this arbitration proceeding Mr. Mitchell operated the Randolph-Sheppard Vending Facility #7408, the LWC Cafeteria in Baton Rouge, Louisiana.
The Business Enterprise Program (''BEP") manual sets forth progressive discipline procedures in the Louisiana Business Enterprise Program Administrative Action Procedures (Progressive Discipline) before it can be determined that a vendor is to be removed from a facility under the program. If it is determined that a vendor is to be removed from a facility, § l.B.7 of the BEP mandates that the RSMA (Randolph-Sheppard Management Assistant) or RSPM (Randolph-Sheppard Program Manager) notify a manager of removal from a facility by hand delivery of a written notice of removal. Section l .B.7 also requires that the letter contain certain information, including notice of the "manager's right of appeal."
A previous arbitration panel determined that when LWC hand-delivered to Mr. Mitchell a notice that his permit was revoked for nonpayment of vendor debts and for abandoning the facility, LWC failed to provide Mr. Mitchell a copy of his appeal rights in an accessible format. Therefore the untimeliness of Mr. Mitchell's request for an evidentiary hearing was vitiated and Mr. Mitchell was entitled to an evidentiary hearing on the revocation of his permit (Mitchell v. Louisiana Workforce Commission, Case No. R-S/16-11). A Fair Hearing regarding the revocation of Mr. Mitchell's permit was conducted in accordance with United States Federal Regulations Title 34 CFR § 395.13 before Hearing Officer Erika L. Green via the Zoom videoconferencing platform on November 15, 2021. The Hearing Officer concluded that"... LWCILRS's termination of Mr. Mitchell's manager permit for the Randolph-Sheppard Program was based on extensive remedial measures and their attempt to abide by all regulations of applicable law." Mr. Mitchell appealed that decision which resulted in the current arbitration proceedings pursuant to the Randolph­ Sheppard Act (20 U.S.C. § 107d-l(a) and§ 107d-2) and the implementing regulations at 34 C.F.R
§ 395.13 by the February 27, 2023 convening letter from the USDOE ("convening letter") (CX- 20).
As set forth in the Scheduling Order agreed to by the parties during a June 9, 2023 Prehearing Conference, the deadline for filing all motions was set for July 7, 2023. Mr. Mitchell filed a Motion for Summary Judgment with the panel on July 14, 2023. The Motion for Summary Judgment was denied as untimely on August 15, 2023.
Testimony given during the hearing determined that Mr. Mitchell was the manager of Vending Facility #7408 (LWC Cafeteria) from September 2015 to February 26, 2016. (Tr. Page 130, Lines 18-19). During that time, it was determined that the LWC Cafeteria was not profitable due to minimal customer traffic, limited food selection, and the necessity for healthier foods, snacks and beverages, and too many cafeteria employees. (Tr. Page 196, Lines 9-22; Tr. Page 197, Lines 2-7). In order to address these issues the decision was made to repurpose the LWC Cafeteria to a micro-market kiosk concept. (Tr, Page 338, Lines 10-20). Mr. Mitchell was to close and clean the LWC Cafeteria, continue to maintain the facility vending machines, and participate in launching and managing the facility under the new concept. (LX-1).
Mr. Mitchell was placed on a six-month probation period by letter dated December 11, 2015 for failure to address outstanding debts to suppliers and for closing the facility early on four separate occasions without notifying the RSMA. (CX-2). A Plan of Action, dated December 11, 2015, was presented to Mr. Mitchell on December 14, 2015. (CX-1). The "Manager's Signature" line on the Plan of Action is blank. The unrebutted testimony of LWC Randolph-Sheppard Program Analyst (RSMA) Michaelle Cox was that she read the Plan of Action to Mr. Mitchell and that the signature line was blank because Mr. Mitchell refused to sign it. (Tr. Page 59, Lines 3-14).
According to unrebutted testimony, during his operation of the LWC Cafeteria Mr. Mitchell incurred outstanding debts to four separate suppliers. (LX-2). These debts were not paid by Mr. Mitchell. (LX-1). Mr. Mitchell did not properly clean the LWC Cafeteria after it closed on December 11, 2015, and did not continue to service the vending machines on the LWC campus. (Tr. Page 74, Lines 8-12; Tr. Page 87, Lines 14-15). Additionally, he did not attend the Grand Opening of the Micro-Market on January 4, 2016. (Tr. Page 267, Lines 19-23; Tr. Page 339, Lines 11-18). Mr. Mitchell's "license" to operate a facility under the Randolph-Sheppard program is still valid, however he is not in good standing because of the outstanding debts and cannot apply to manage a new facility until the debts are paid and he is placed in good standing. (Tr. Page 229, Lines 14-25, Tr. Page 230, Lines 1-4). Finally, Mr. Mitchell's "permit" to operate Vending Facility #7408 was revoked on February 26, 2016 for failure to address outstanding debts to suppliers and for abandoning the facility. (LX-1).
The Positions of the Parties
Position of Cedric Mitchell
Cedric Mitchell (''Mr. Mitchell"), a blind vender in the Louisiana Business Enterprise Program ("BEP''), alleges that the Louisiana Workforce Commission ("LWC") as the State Licensing Agency ("SLA"), violated the Randolph-Sheppard Act and the implementing regulations at 34
C.F.R. § 395.7(b) and §395.11 by not providing him with:
training in the operation of a micro market kiosk after the closure of the LWC's cafeteria, which resulted in the LWC suspending his license and placing him on probation without income to pay debts;
several necessary documents in an accessible format; and
any post-employment training services that would enable him to remain in the Business Enterprise Program (BEP).
First, Mr. Mitchell argues he did not receive training in the operation of a micro­ market kiosk after the closure of the LWC cafeteria, which resulted in the LWC suspending his license and placing him on probation without income to pay debts.
Mr. Mitchell argues that his license and/or permit was abruptly revoked by Steven R. DeBruhl ("Mr. DeBruhl"), on February 26, 2016, when he was presented with the revocation letter dated February 15, 2016, while in Mr. DeBruhl's office. Mr. Mitchell argues that the revocation letter is not accurate as it stated that Mr. Mitchell "abandoned" the micro-market. Mr. Mitchell contends that he could not abandon a location where there was no Manager's Agreement, RS709, as testified to by Mr. DeBruhl. He contends this revocation was a strategic maneuver intended to circumvent the established procedural norms governing the license to operate a vending facility within the BEP. Mr. Mitchell also contends that Mr. DeBruhl, Charles Monk ("Mr. Monk"), and Michaelle Cox ("Ms. Cox") were all aware of his impending removal without informing him until the day of his discharge. He claims this raises concerns about transparency and procedural fairness within the LWC.
With regard to the revocation of his permit, Mr. Mitchell argues that Mr. DeBruhl and Ms. Cox both testified that they knew Mr. Mitchell had not been given an opportunity for a full evidentiary hearing prior to the revocation of his permit, and Mr. Monk testified that a vender is never given an opportunity for a full evidentiary hearing prior to removal which Mr. Mitchell contends violates 34 C.F.R. §395.7(b). Mr. Mitchell contends the LWC failed to follow the requirements of the regulation when it revoked his permit prior to providing him with a full evidentiary hearing.
Mr. Mitchell argues in his Post-Hearing Brief that he repeatedly requested a full evidentiary hearing initially via certified mail in compliance with the procedure outlined in chapter 19 of the TAG manual along with requests that were made via email to the LWC and its administrators, including Mr. Monk and Mr. DeBruhl. Mr. Mitchell contends these requests received no response or acknowledgment. In addition, Mr. Mitchell argues that termination of his permit on February 26, 2016 was premature as the SLA failed to produce evidence of an alleged six-month probationary period beginning on December 11, 2015, which was only two months prior to the revocation of the permit. Mr. Mitchell argues that the alleged probationary period that led up to the removal of his permit is not accurate as portrayed by the LWC. He argues that Ms. Cox gave conflicting testimony with regard to whether the December 14, 2015 meeting to place him on probation took place at the LWC office or on a back ramp. Mr. Mitchell argues at one point that Ms. Cox testified to not possessing a probation letter and testified she gave him a corrective Plan of Action letter. Then later, she testified that she did give him a probation letter after learning that Mr. DeBruhl testified she had the probation letter. Mr. Mitchell contends these inconsistencies of her testimony cast doubt on the veracity of key events and the potential mismanagement within the LWC. Mr. Mitchell further asserts there is no proof that the alleged meeting on December 14, 2015, ever took place as Ms. Cox could not provide any sign-in log with his signature or camera footage to substantiate her claim that a December 14, 2015 meeting occurred.
Finally, Mr. Mitchell argues, Mr. DeBruhl himself acknowledged failing to provide him with due process appeal rights during his removal, highlighting a clear breach of established procedures. Mr. Mitchell contends that Mr. DeBruhl's omission to grant him the right to a full evidentiary hearing underscores the SLA's failure to adhere to the rights prescribed to a vendor before initiating any adverse actions against their license or permit.
Second, Mr. Mitchell argues the SLA failed to provide him with several documents in an accessible format in violation of 34 C.F.R. § 395.11, which he argues requires that essential documents are accessible to individuals with disabilities ensuring equal treatment and access to information.
Mr. Mitchell argues that he never received a copy of the BEP Manual informing him of his due process rights. He stated that he learned during testimony given on March 15, 2021, in a previous arbitration, that the SLA was in possession of a letter from the U.S. Department of Education dated February 23, 2015, advising the SLA to provide each blind vender in the BEP with the approved BEP Manual in their preferred accessible format. Mr. Mitchell argues he has never received the BEP Manual in an accessible format. He notes the letter was issued exactly one year prior to his removal from the LWC Micro-market.
Mr. Mitchell argues that Melissa Bayham, Director of the Louisiana Rehabilitation Services ("LRS"), admitted to agency Section 504 violations, highlighting the potential denial of Mr. Mitchell's rights due to inaccessible documentation.
Mr. Mitchell argues that on February 26, 2016, the day of the revocation, he was given a revocation letter in an inaccessible format without appeal rights. He contends this contravened his Constitutional 14th Amendment due process rights and civil rights under Section 508 accessibility compliance standards.
Third, Mr. Mitchell argues, the LWC has violated the implementing regulations of the Randolph-Sheppard Act at 34 C.F.R. §395.11 by not providing him with any post­ employment training after the revocation of his permit.
Mr. Mitchell contends this issue focuses on the SLA's obligation to furnish post­ employment training services for him, particularly in managing a micro-market kiosk. Mr. Mitchell questions whether the SLA met its responsibilities to provide the necessary support for his continued success within the ·program. Mr. Mitchell asserts that compliance with the Randolph-Sheppard Act and pertinent regulations is at the core of this issue.
Fourth, Mr. Mitchell argues in his Post-Hearing Brief that the actions taken by the SLA violated constitutional and statutory provisions.
Mr. Mitchell argues that the revocation of his license/permit by the SLA, when it did not follow the requirements of 34 C.F.R. §395.7 and §395.11, violated: the due process clause of the Fourteenth Amendment; section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973; Title VI, section 601ofthe Civil Rights Act of 1964. He argues these actions violated his property rights and civil rights.
To this end, Mr. Mitchell makes four arguments:
Mr. Mitchell argues, because he was not granted a full evidentiary hearing as required by 34 C.F.R. §395.7(b) his due process rights were violated and stripped him of his property rights and ability to earn a living.
Mr. Mitchell argues, the revocation letter was served without the proper
due process appeal rights letting him know of the opportunity for a full evidentiary hearing. He contends this was a violation of the due process clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.
Mr. Mitchell argues, the email communications that were used to transmit documents regarding appeal rights were inaccessible and violated Section 508 and Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973. He argues these sections require equal treatment and access to information.
Mr. Mitchell argues that Mr. DeBruhl, Mr. Monk, and Ms. Cox violated his rights as a blind vendor, by not providing him with documents in an accessible format, which violated his civil rights, as delineated in Mullane V. Central Handover Bank And Trust Co, Inc. He further argues that Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990, serves as a protective shield against discrimination based on disability and that it applies to government. Mr. Mitchell argues he is within his rights to seek redress and to pursue legal remedies through a private lawsuit.
Fifth, Mr. Mitchell argues, the law supports that individuals can secure monetary damages for claims of intentional discrimination under the Civil Rights Act of 1964, Title VI, Section 601.
In support of this argument, Mr. Mitchell cites: Barnes v. Gorman, 536 U.S. 181, 186-87 (2002); Alexander v. Sandoval, 532 U.S. 275, 280 (2001); Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992). Mr. Mitchell also argues that Title IX solidifies this principle: Barnes, 536 U.S. at 186-87 (citing Franklin v. Gwinnett, 503 U.S. 60, 74-75 (1990)); Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co. (1950).
In addition, Mr. Mitchell argues that Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and Section 794 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 provide a framework that prohibits discrimination and provides for compensation for discriminatory practices.
Mr. Mitchell seeks: monetary compensation; reinstatement of his license in good standing to include the right to bid on vending facility locations; have his record returned to good standing; and, reinstate his seniority to include the time from February 26, 2016 to the present. Specifically, Mr. Mitchell requests: three years of stipend pay; 100 percent gross revenue (Refreshment Solutions); six years back pay for loss of wages in the estimated total sum of $510,00.00.
Position of the Louisiana Workforce Commission {LWC}
The LWC argues that it did not violate the Act and implementing regulations at 34 C.F.R.
§395.7(b) and § 395.11 for several reasons:
First, the LWC argues that Cedric Mitchell (Mr. Mitchell) was not removed from the BEP. Rather, the LWC revoked his permit to operate the cafeteria.
To support this assertion, the LWC points to evidence and testimony presented at the
Arbitration hearing which it claims establishes that Mr. Mitchell's status as a licensed vendor in the BEP had not been impacted. Specifically, the LWC points to the testimony of Michelle Cox, Randolph Sheppard Management Analyst; Steven DeBruhl, Regional Director; and Kevin Monk, Director of Blind Services, who each testified that Mr. Mitchell has not been removed from the BEP. In support of this position, the LWC also points to the February 15, 2016 letter to Mr. Mitchell from the Louisiana Rehabilitation Services ("LRS") that advised him that his permit to operate the facility was being revoked effective February 26, 2016 due to his abandoning the facility and due to his failure to pay outstanding debts. The LWC argues that Mr. Mitchell has not been removed from the BEP and is still a Licensed Manager, but his permit to operate that facility is revoked until he satisfies his debt obligations and until he is placed in good standing with the BEP.
Second, the LWC argues that Mr. Mitchell abandoned the Randolph-Sheppard Vending Facility #7048 referred to as the LWC Cafeteria in Baton Rouge, Louisiana prior to the time for which training could be provided for the micro-market Kiosk.
The LWC states that Mr. Mitchell contends that the LWC violated the Act and implementing regulations by not providing him with training in the operation of a micro-market kiosk, which resulted in the LWC suspending his license and placing him on probation without income to pay his debts. The LWC disagrees with this position. The LWC points to evidence and testimony offered during the Arbitration, which it argues establishes that Mr. Mitchell was suspended and his permit was eventually revoked due to his failure to pay the outstanding debts and because Mr. Mitchell abruptly abandoned the vending facility in December 2015 prior to the subsequent installation of the micro-market kiosk.
In addition, the LWC disagrees with Mr. Mitchell's narrative that he was removed from the facility due to the "inaccessibility" of the kiosk. The LWC argues that each witness called by LWC testified that had Mr. Mitchell not abandoned his site, he would have received training on the operation of the newly installed kiosk. The LWC points to the fact that several subsequent RS managers and service providers are currently at the same location operating a similar kiosk. The LWC contends that Mr. Mitchell's argument that he was not provided training in the operation of a micro-market kiosk, which resulted in the LWC suspending his license and placing him on probation without income to pay his debts, is false.
Third, the LWC argues that the revocation of Mr. Mitchell's permit for Vending Facility #7048 was due to his failure to pay outstanding invoices/debts.
The LWC argues that Mr. Mitchell's assertion that the LWC violated the Randolph­ Sheppard Act and implementing regulations by not providing him training "which resulted in the LWC suspending his license and placing him on probation" is "disingenuous and blatantly false." The LWC argues that the record reflects that Mr. Mitchell was placed on probation and his permit was revoked, in part, because of his failure to pay outstanding inventory debts. The LWC argues that the debts were incurred prior to November of 2015 and prior to the installation of the kiosk. Mr. Mitchell did not dispute the validity of the debts or that he was aware of them. The LWC argues at testimony from the Arbitration suggests that eight years later the debts to Reinhart, Baton Rouge Coca-Cola, and Cintas are still outstanding obligations that Mr. Mitchell has not yet paid.
The LWC disagrees with Mr. Mitchell's contention that his inability to satisfy his debt obligations was due to the installation of the kiosk. The LWC argues that Mr. Mitchell incurred the debt obligations long before the kiosk was installed, and therefore, this contention lacks merit
Fourth, the LWC argues that Mr. Mitchell has not specified which documents were not provided in an accessible format
The LWC points to the convening letter that indicates that Mr. Mitchell alleges the LWC failed to provide him with several documents in an accessible format. The LWC argues that Mr. Mitchell did not specify which documents were not accessible to him. The LWC does acknowledge that Mr. Mitchell did raise arguments related to inaccessible due process notice rights in a previous arbitration and, as such, are not subject to relitigation. The LWC's position is that all documents have been provided to Mr. Mitchell in an accessible format.
The fifth and final argument by the LWC is that Mr. Mitchell has not produced evidence related to the alleged failure of LWC to provide post-employment training services.
The LWC argues that the testimony elicited during the Arbitration does not support Mr. Mitchell's assertion that LWC or LRS failed to provide him with, or that he requested, post­ employment training services. In addition, the LWC argues there was no evidence or testimony presented by Mr. Mitchell that he was denied post-employment training services by the LWC or LRS. As such, the LWC argues Mr. Mitchell's assertion lacks merit and therefore must be dismissed.
Conclusions of Law and Analysis
Mr. Mitchell argues that LWC violated the Act and its implementing regulations at 34 C.F.R. §395.7(b) and 34 C.F.R. §395.11 when it removed his license to operate the cafeteria without due process, failed to provide post-employment training opportunities and failed to provide him documents in an accessible format. As the state licensing agency for the State of Louisiana, LWC's Louisiana Rehabilitation Services ("hereinafter referred to as LWC") is required under 34 C.F.R. § 395.7(b) to provide for the licensing of vendors under Louisiana's Randolph­ Sheppard program for an indefinite period, subject to suspension and termination on certain grounds after a full evidentiary hearing:
The State licensing agency shall provide for the issuance of licenses for an indefinite period but subject to suspension or termination if, after affording the vendor an opportunity for a full evidentiary hearing, the State licensing agency finds that the vending facility is not being operated in accordance with its rules and regulations, the terms and conditions of the permit, and the terms and conditions of  `the agreement with the vendor.
Through the Technical Assistance and Guidance Manual ("TAG Manual") the LWC has set forth rules to enhance the ability of the Randolph-Sheppard Program staff to achieve the goals of the program, including rules for obtaining licenses and permits in the program, as well as due process procedures for suspension and revocation of licenses and permits. It is not disputed that Mr. Mitchell successfully completed the Randolph-Sheppard Training Program provided by LWC and LWC issued to Mr. Mitchell a license to manage a business enterprise as part of the Randolph­ Sheppard Program. It is also not disputed that LWC issued a Permit to Mr. Mitchell, a Licensed Manager, to manage a specific facility, Randolph-Sheppard Vending Facility #7408, the LWC cafeteria.
A central issue before the Panel is whether Mr. Mitchell's license to participate in the Randolph-Sheppard Program was revoked by LWC in violation of 34 C.F.R. § 395.7(b). The unrebutted evidence in the record reveals that Mr. Mitchell's license under the BEP program was not revoked and that he remains a licensed manager under the program. LWC Randolph­ Sheppard Program Analyst (RSMA) Michaelle Cox {Tr. Pages 428-9). and Randolph-Sheppard Program Manager (RSPM) David DeBruhl (Tr. at 614-616). testified, without rebuttal, that Mr. Mitchell's license was not revoked, but that he is not in good standing in the program due to hisouts tanding debts to suppliers for the LWC cafeteria. Ms. Cox and Mr. DeBruhl testified, without rebuttal, that Mr. Mitchell remains eligible to manage another facility under the program upon payment of all outstanding debts. (Tr. Pages 428-9 and Tr. Pages 615-16, respectively). Based on the evidence in the record, it is concluded that Mr. Mitchell's license was not revoked and that he remains a licensed vendor under the Louisiana Randolph-Sheppard program.
Because Mr. Mitchell's license was not revoked, it is concluded that LWC did not violate 34 C.F.R. § 395.7(b) through revocation of his license.
The record evidence is that although he remained a licensed manager in the program, LWC revoked Mr. Mitchell's permit to run the LWC cafeteria by letter dated February 26, 2016. (LX- 1). (Cox testimony, at TR 428-9). Mr. Mitchell contends that his permit was revoked in violation of section 7(b) because he was denied the due process protections specified under the TAG Manual. The TAG Manual at Chapter 19, Administrative Action Procedures (Progressive Discipline), sets forth the steps to be taken in addressing deficiencies:
The RSPM shall make the decision as to what administrative action to take based upon the seriousness of the violation and the damage to BEP. The following progression of administrative action will be followed:
Notice
Letter of Reprimand
Probation and Plan of Corrective Action
Suspension as outlined below
Upon receipt of information which indicates that administrative action may be appropriate, the LRS/RSPM shall take the following actions prior to determining the level of administrative action:
The RSMA shall notify the licensee in writing of the allegations and reasons that administrative action is being considered. The notice shall be sent to the licensee's home address via certified mail.
The licensee shall have five business days to respond, in writing, to the notice. The response shall be made to the program personnel designated in the notice. After receiving licensee's response, the RSPM shall decide what administrative action, if any is appropriate. If no response is received within five business days from the manager, the RSPM shall decide, upon completion of an investigation, what administrative action, if any is to be taken.
The TAG Manual at Chapter 20 CORRECTIVE ACTION/NOTIFICATION AND PLAN, Section A.7 also sets forth the options to be taken if a Manager's response to the Corrective Action Plan was unsatisfactory:
If a vending facility Manager's response to the Corrective Action Plan was unsatisfactory, insufficient, inconsistent, or entirely lacking within the time period allowed/specified, the RSPM shall consider relevant factors affecting the Licensed Manager's performance and shall recommend one of the following Disciplinary actions:
Placement of the Licensed Manager on probation.
Suspension or termination of the Licenses Manager's operating agreement. If this action is taken, the RSPM shall determine any conditions to be met prior to the Manager's being allowed to apply for other available locations.
Revocation of the permit or license of the Licensed Manager
At Chapter 20, Section B, Notification of Suspension or Termination of Permit and/or License, the TAG Manual sets forth the process for notifying a Manager of the suspension of a permit:
Notice of suspension or termination of a permit shall be hand delivered by the RSMA or the RSPM or by certified US mail. This notice shall include the reasons for the RSPM's recommendation to terminate the permit, and shall specify any conditions the SLA requires the facility manager to meet prior to permitting the vendor to apply to other locations license manager appeal rights shall be included in the notice.
The evidence in the record reveals that LWC sent written notices to Mr. Mitchell notifying him that he had incurred debts with vendors, which must be paid.  On September 18, 2015, September 29, 2015, November 30, 2015, and December 8, 2015, RSMA Duncan sent to Mr. Mitchell a Demand for Payment of Money owed (LX-2), citing the following language for Chapter 15, Section B of the TAG Manual:
If the agency learns from a Supplier, that money is owed, the Licensed Manager has 15 days to either pay the total amount due or prove that the money is not owed Failure to pay the total amount due will result in disciplinary action.
The letter sets forth the names of the vendors owed and the total amount due on each invoice generated at both the LWC and BRCC locations. Each of the letters concluded that if the debts were not readily satisfied, "... we will have no choice but to take immediate disciplinary action, up to revocation of your Managerial License, which is required for your continuing participation in the Randolph Sheppard Program." (LX-2).
The evidence reveals that LWC also notified Mr. Mitchell of reports of employees reporting for work late to the LWC cafeteria, vending machines out of product and the results of a State health inspection. (LX-8, LX-9, LX-11, Tr., Pages 466-480).
By letter dated December 11, 2015, Mr. DeBruhl wrote to Mr. Mitchell a Notice of Probation citing the failure to pay outstanding vendor debts and failure to follow TAG requirements that licensed managers notify the RSMA of absences and early closures of the LWC cafeteria on four dates (LX-13) (Tr., Pages 628-34). On or about December 14, 2015, Ms. Duncan hand-delivered to Mr. Mitchell a Plan of Action (CX-1) (Tr., Page 459, Lines 4-24), which stated that Mr. Mitchell would be subject to a "Probation period to 6/11/16" and that he would be responsible for:
"Payment of Money Owed, contact and arrange payment plan with vendors, and report payment arrangement approvals from vendors to RMSA... ":
The Plan of Action also states: "Arrangement plan due by 12/29/15," and a "Probation period to 6/11/16". There is no evidence in the record that Mr. Mitchell advised LWC of any arrangements or plans for debt repayment prior to the 12/29/15 deadline. There is no evidence in the record Mr. Mitchell contested that the debts were owed to the vendors or the amount of the debts.
By letter dated February 15, 2016, Mr. DeBruhl informed Mr. Mitchell that his permit to operate the LWC cafeteria was being revoked, citing outstanding debts to vendors which Mr. Mitchell has not paid and had not made progress toward arranging for payment; and for abandonment of the facility and its operations after "a mutual decision to reformat and repurpose the LWC Cafeteria into the current micro market concept," and agreed that he "would close and clean the existing cafeteria and become an active participant in launching and managing the facility under the new concept."(LX-1). In the letter, Mr. DeBruhl further states opening of the micro market, and failed to participate in the transition of the vending machines to the micro market concept or in the
Grand Opening on January 4, 2016. At the hearing, Mr. DeBruhl testified, without rebuttal, that the micro market concept was being installed to manage the site with less overhead and make it more profitable. ( Tr., Pages 641-43).
RSPM Steven DeBruhl scheduled a meeting with Mr. Mitchell on February 15, 2016 to advise him that his permit to manage the LWC cafeteria was being revoked. The meeting was not held on that date and it was rescheduled. At the meeting on February 26, 2016, Mr. DeBruhl read to Mr. Mitchell the letter of February 15, 2016, and hand--delivered to him a copy of the letter. (DeBruhl testimony, Tr. at 617-621). The letter advised Mr. Mitchell that he had the right to appeal the agency action and referred to the attached appeal procedures which were not in fact attached. Although the appeal procedures were emailed to Mr. Mitchell, they were not accessible by him. Mr. Mitchell filed a complaint, which was the subject of arbitration in Cedric Mitchell v. Louisiana Workforce Commission, R-S/16-11. On April 30, 2011, the panel of arbitrators selected to hear that matter issued the following final and binding Conclusion and Award:
This panel finds that because Mr. Mitchell did not receive a hand delivered copy of his appeal rights and was subsequently notified of his appeal rights in a format not accessible to him and by e-mail, Mr. Mitchell did not receive his notice of his right to appeal, including time limits for appeal, as mandated by Section 187 of the BEP progressive discipline procedures. Therefore Mr. Mitchell's request for an evidentiary hearing on the revocation of his permit as requested by his letter of April 1, 2016 is granted. (Footnote Omitted)
In compliance with the Award issued in R-S/16-11, Mr. Mitchell was granted a full evidentiary hearing on the revocation of his permit to operate the LWC and on the issues surrounding the micro market held before hearing officer Erika L. Green on December 5, 2021.
To the extent that Mr. Mitchell's claims in the matter currently before this Panel were the subject of the R-S/16-11 arbitration proceeding, that Award is final and binding and the issues decided in that matter cannot be relitigated in this case. Therefore, the circumstances surrounding the accessibility of the notice of appeal rights has been resolved pursuant the award issued in R­ S/16-11 and are not properly before this Panel.
The evidence reveals that the LWC issued to Mr. Mitchell several notices and reprimands regarding nonpayment of debts and other deficiencies, a letter of probation and a plan of corrective action. Although, as Mr. Mitchell points out, the Plan of Action stated that he was given a 6-month probation, the Plan of Action also provided Mr. Mitchell until 12/29/15 to make arrangements to repay vendors subject to further discipline. There is no evidence in the record that any such repayment plans had been undertaken or attempted. In addition, the evidence in the record supports the finding that Mr Mitchell did not participate in the conversion of the LWC cafeteria to the micro-market concept, leading LWC to conclude that he had abandoned the facility. Therefore, because Mr. Mitchell had not made arrangements to pay outstanding overdue debts to vendors by the 12/29/15 deadline included in the Plan of Action, the LWC did not violate the TAG Manual by not waiting a full six months before determining to revoke his permit to run the facility.
Mr. Mitchell also argues that LWC violated § 395.7(b) when his permit was revoked prior to receiving a full evidentiary hearing. Section 395.7(b) states that licenses are "subject to suspension or termination if, after affording the vendor an opportunity for a full evidentiary hearing... ". As discussed above, Mr. Mitchell's license was not terminated. Mr. Mitchell has not cited any federal or state requirement that he receive a full evidentiary hearing prior to the revocation of his permit to manage a specific facility. The TAG Manual at Chapter 21 requires that a Manager be afforded a full evidentiary hearing "to resolve dissatisfaction with any SLA action arising from the operation or administration of the Business Enterprise Program," with the right to appeal the decision of the hearing officer to arbitration. Mr. Mitchell has had a full evidentiary hearing on the revocation of his license and/or permit.
Although Mr. Mitchell argues that the revocation letter was not sent to him by certified mail, the undisputed evidence in the record is that the LWC complied with the TAG manual's requirements in Chapter 20 that a notice of suspension or termination of a Permit shall be hand­ delivered by the RSMA or RSPM or sent by certified U.S. Mail. It is not disputed that RSPM DeBruhl hand-delivered and read the revocation letter to Mr. Mitchell.
Based on consideration of the issues properly before this panel, it is concluded that the revocation of Mr. Mitchell's permit to operate the LWC cafeteria did not violate 34 C.F.R. § 395.7(b) or the TAG Manual.
Mr. Mitchell also contends that LWC violated 34 C.F.R. §395.11 when it failed to provide post-employment training opportunities and failed to provide him documents in an accessible format. Section 395.11 states as follows:
§ 395.11 Training program for blind individuals.
The State licensing agency shall ensure that effective programs of vocational and other training services, including personal and vocational adjustment, books, tools, and other training materials, shall be provided to blind individuals as vocational rehabilitation services under the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (Pub. L. 93-112), as amended by the Rehabilitation Act Amendments of 1974 (Pub. L. 93-516). Such programs shall include on-the-job training in all aspects of vending facility operation for blind persons with the capacity to operate a vending facility, and upward mobility training (including further education and additional training or retraining for improved work opportunities) for all blind licensees. The State licensing agency shall further ensure that post-employment services shall be provided to blind vendors as vocational rehabilitation services as necessary to assure that the maximum vocational potential of such vendors is achieved and suitable employment is maintained within the State's vending facility program.
Based on unrebutted testimony in the record, the evidence is that LWC attempted to provide a solution to the problems of staffing, lack of customers and lack of profitability of the LWC cafeteria by working to set up a micro-market. Mr. DeBruhl testified that the micro-market kiosk concept would avail Mr. Mitchell of an opportunity to continue to operate as licensed management at the LWC location, and software, technology and hands-on training that would help with the operation of the kiosk would be available to Mr. Mitchell. The training time period for the micro-market was to be in December prior to opening in January. According to Mr. DeBruhl, Mr. Mitchell did not appear at the LWC cafeteria site for a month for any of the proposed training and also did not fully participate in the close out of the cafeteria. The evidence in the record is that although Mr. Mitchell did not receive the training needed to operate the micro-market, the evidence also reveals that he stopped coming into the site, missed several meetings with the kiosk vendor, did not appear at the Grand Opening, and therefore could not take advantage of any training that would have been provided. (Tr., Pages 641-44). Rehabilitation Services Director Melissa Bayham testified that LWC was responsible for providing assessable training under the Act and its implementing regulations when it is necessary. (Tr. Pages 355-362) However, the evidence reveals that Mr. Mitchell did not attend proposed training.
Based on the issues properly before this Panel, there is no evidence in the record that the LWC failed to provide Mr. Mitchell with documents in an accessible format. Issues involved in the previous arbitration are not properly before this panel. As LWC points out, Mr. Mitchell did not specify which documents he was not provided in an accessible format. Although Mr. Mitchell argues that he did not receive the BEP manual in an accessible format, there is no evidence, testimony or otherwise, in the record to substantiate any claim that he did not receive BEP program materials in an accessible format.
Based on a review of the evidence in the record, it is concluded that there is insufficient evidence in the record to conclude that LWC failed to provide Mr. Mitchell with documents in an accessible format in violation of 34 C.F.R. §395.11, the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, as amended, or any other law or regulation properly before the Panel. Mr. Mitchell also argues that LWC deprived him of rights under the United States Constitution and several laws. The Panel's authority is limited to the issues set forth in the convening letter, and all other issues are dismissed without prejudice.
AWARD
Based on the above discussion and analysis, this panel finds the following:
I. All issues raised by Mr. Mitchell arising outside those specified in the convening letter, to include all issues arising under the United States Constitution, are hereby dismissed without prejudice, as the Panel is without authorization to rule on those issues.
Because Mr. Mitchell's license was not revoked, it is concluded that the LWC did not violate 34 C.F.R. § 39S.7(b) or the TAG manual through any revocation of Mr. Mitchell's license.
Based on consideration of the issues properly before this panel, the revocation of Mr. Mitchell's permit to operate the LWC cafeteria did not violate 34 C.F.R. § 39S.7(b) or the TAG Manual.
The evidence does not support a finding that LWC violated 34C.F.R. § 395.11 by failing to provide Mr. Mitchell post-employment training opportunities or by failing to provide him documents in accessible format.
Based on the evidence in the record, arguments of the Parties, and the findings of the Panel, the Complaint is dismissed in its entirety. This Award is in full settlement of all claims and counterclaims submitted to this Arbitration. All claims not expressly granted herein are hereby denied.

RE: Federal Fiscal Year 2023 Maintenance of Effort Deficit Waiver Response 
 
Dear Andrew Reese: 
 
This letter is in response to the government of District of Columbia Department on Disability 
Services’ (DDS) request for a waiver of the maintenance of effort (MOE) requirement under the 
State Vocational Rehabilitation Services (VR) program for Federal fiscal year (FFY) 2023. In 
your letter, dated August 14, 2025, DDS requested a waiver of the $2,290,090 FFY 2023 MOE 
deficit
1 under the VR program because “the District of Columbia [District] experienced a Global 
pandemic which caused significant disruption in Agency business practices and a decline in 
revenues, particularly in the Human Services Cluster while at the same time increasing the need 
for services provided by the Department on Disability Services and other direct service agencies. 
As a result of these circumstances, the District had to make some difficult fiscal choices 
regarding the allocation of local funds, and the Maintenance of Effort for the Vocational 
Rehabilitation grant was adversely impacted.” For the reasons described below, the 
Rehabilitation Services Administration (RSA) has determined DDS’ request does not satisfy the 
requirements for a MOE waiver at Section 111(a)(2)(C) of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 
(Rehabilitation Act) and 34 C.F.R. § 361.62(d)(1). 
 
Section 111(a)(2)(C) of the Rehabilitation Act
2 authorizes the Secretary to grant a waiver or 
modification of the MOE reduction when such an action would be “an equitable response to 
exceptional or uncontrollable circumstances affecting the State.3” Federal regulations at 
34 C.F.R. § 361.62(d)(1)(i) authorize the Secretary to grant a waiver or modification of the MOE

In its request, DDS provided excerpts from the District’s Fiscal year (FY) 2021, 2022, and 2023 
Annual Comprehensive Financial Report. This data substantiated that DDS saw a decrease in 
local funding throughout this period as District funding was allocated to address unplanned and 
unanticipated needs. However, DDS did not substantiate that the State had an overall reduction 
in revenue during FY 2023 that resulted in equitable reductions across all programs, including 
VR. District budgetary data indicates that as the DDS operating budget was increasing, the VR 
services budget was decreasing.

Considering the facts presented by DDS’ request, the COVID-19 pandemic was clearly an 
exceptional or uncontrollable event affecting the State, as required, in part, by Section 
111(a)(2)(C) of the Rehabilitation Act and 34 C.F.R. § 361.62(d)(1). The requirement at 34 
C.F.R. § 361.62(d)(1)(i) requires, in pertinent part, that to obtain a MOE waiver, the State must 
have incurred a “significant...reduction in revenues that result in a general reduction of programs 
within the State.” DDS did not substantiate that the State’s revenues decreased during this period 
and that there was an equitable reduction to State programs based on the circumstances.  
 
Therefore, the data did not demonstrate the pandemic caused a reduction in State revenues that 
resulted in a general reduction in programs within the State, as required by 34 C.F.R. § 
361.62(d)(1)(i). DDS’ data suggests the shortfall was a result of strategic financial decisions 
rather than an inability for the State to furnish the required non-Federal share. 
 
While we recognize the significant challenges DDS faced in providing services during FFY 2023 
because of the pandemic, the State has not satisfied the criteria for a MOE waiver because the 
State did not substantiate a reduction in revenues that resulted in a general reduction of programs 
within the State because of an exceptional or uncontrollable event, as required by 34 C.F.R. § 
361.62(d)(1)(i). Therefore, DDS’ MOE waiver request is denied.  
 
For the foregoing reasons, RSA will reduce DDS’ FFY 2026 VR grant award (H126A260011) 
by $2,290,090, in accordance with the requirements at 34 C.F.R. § 361.62(c)(2). Although DDS

Strength: Safe Learning 
Environment
"[Conversation] is less pressured, and I feel safe making 
mistakes and learning from them."
GenAI offers a judgment-free setting where students can 
practice without fear of negative repercussions. This 
psychological safety is particularly beneficial for novice 
counselors still developing confidence with clinical language 
and interventions.

Strength: Scalability & 
Accessibility
"What surprised me most was its ability to provide 
detailed, contextually appropriate responses that 
mimicked real-life dialogue, including displaying complex 
emotions."
GenAI delivers standardized, emotionally rich simulations to 
all students. This consistency ensures fairness in learning and 
assessment while supporting remote, on-demand practice 
opportunities.

Strength: Assessment & Feedback

Weakness: Biases & Underrepresentation
AI systems demonstrate consistent stereotyping patterns in their representations:
Demographic Stereotyping
• COPD patients portrayed as elderly females
• TBI/SCI clients depicted as young adults
• Consistent age patterns across disability types
Occupational Biases
• Overrepresentation of "graphic designers"
• Deaf individuals shown in remote work
• Autistic people in technology roles
• MS clients in part-time positions

Example of 
Stereotypical AI 
Response
"I've been looking mostly at remote work... data entry, 
maybe some content writing. Things I could do from home 
where I wouldn't have to interact with people directly. 
(Pauses) And where I could take breaks when my MS 
symptoms flare up without everyone watching and 
judging."
This response reinforces stereotypes about people with MS, 
focusing narrowly on limitations rather than presenting a 
complex, multidimensional individual.

Threat: Data Privacy 
Concerns
"Working with the AI client raised ethical considerations 
related to confidentiality, data security, and the potential 
for over-reliance on AI in sensitive areas like mental 
health."
Students may not fully understand data privacy risks when 
using AI platforms. Institutions must implement strict 
guidelines to protect sensitive information in training 
contexts.

The "Eliza Effect" 
Concern
Named after ELIZA, an early natural language program, this 
phenomenon describes how users attribute human-like 
intelligence to algorithmic outputs. John Weizenbaum warned 
against delegating human judgment to machines, as such 
judgment is rooted in lived experience and value-based 
reasoning.
"In counselor education, this concern is especially salient, 
as ethical decision-making and value-informed reflection 
are core to professional competence."

True Promise of AI in 
Counselor Education
"The true promise of AI in counselor education lies not in 
replacing human connection but in enhancing it."
When guided by empathy and principles of inclusion, AI can 
serve as a powerful complement to traditional training. It can 
amplify diverse client voices and support ethical decision-
making while maintaining human values at the center.

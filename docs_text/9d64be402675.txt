DECISION AND AWARD
This case comes before a tripartite arbitration panel ("the Panel") convened by the United States Department of Education ("DOE") pursuant to the Randolph-Sheppard Act, 20 U.S.C. §107 et seq. ("RSA") and implementing regulations at 34 C.F.R. §395.13. As agreed by the Parties, a hearing in this matter was held via the Zoom videoconferencing platform on March 15, 2021. At the hearing, the parties were given the opportunity to call and cross-examine witnesses and to present documentary evidence. The witnesses were sworn. The parties presented oral prehearing and closing statements. A transcript of the proceeding was prepared by a court reporter. Michael Talley represented Cedric Mitchell ("Mr. Mitchell" or "the Complainant"). John Williams, Esq. and Danielle Barringer, Esq. represented the Louisiana Workforce Commission ("LWC" or "Respondent"). Testimony was given by Charles Monk, Steven DeBruhl, William Boules and Cedric Mitchell. In reaching its decision, the Panel considered the evidentiary record comprised of the testimony and documentary evidence presented at the hearing, and the arguments of the parties.
The Issue Before the Panel
At the hearing, the parties agreed that the issues before the Panel are stated in the March 2, 2020 letter from the United States Department of Education, Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services authorizing convening of the arbitration. Based on the issue stated in the March 2, 2020 letter, the issue before the panel is appropriately stated as follows:
Whether the LWC violated the Randolph-Sheppard Act and implementing regulations by failing to provide Cedric Mitchell with an accessible copy of his appeal rights, thereby hindering his ability to timely file an appeal of the revocation of his permit by LWC.

(a) Authorization
For the purposes of providing blind persons with remunerative employment, enlarging the economic opportunities of the blind, and stimulating the blind to greater efforts in striving to make themselves self-supporting, blind persons licensed under the provisions of this chapter shall be authorized to operate vending facilities on any Federal property.
(b) Preferences regulations; justification for limitation on operation
In authorizing the operation of vending facilities on Federal property, priority shall be given to blind persons licensed by a State agency as provided in this chapter; and the Secretary, through the Commissioner, shall, after consultation with the Administrator of General Services and other heads of departments, agencies, or instrumentalities of the United States in control of the maintenance, operation, and protection of Federal property, prescribe regulations designed to assure that -
(1) the priority under this subsection is given to such licensed blind persons (including assignment of vending machine income pursuant to section 107d-3 of this title to achieve and protect such priority), and
(2) wherever feasible, one or more vending facilities are established on all Federal property to the extent that any such facility or facilities would not adversely affect the interests of the United States.

Findings of Fact
The Louisiana Workforce Commission ("LWC") has been designated by the U.S. Department of Education as the State Licensing Agency charged with administering the Randolph-Sheppard program in Louisiana pursuant to 20 U.S.C. l 07 et seq. and its implementing regulations found at 34 CFR Part 395.
The Louisiana Randolph-Sheppard program operates m accordance with duly promulgated regulations found at Title 67 Section VI Chapter 5 of the Louisiana Administrative Code and the Technical Assistance and Guidance Manual ("TAG Manual"). The TAG Manual contains unpublished sub regulatory guidance issued by the LWC.
LWC receives federal funding through the Randolph-Sheppard program, and it is therefore subject to the requirements of Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act, as amended.
Pursuant to 34 CFR 395.311, the United States Department of Education ("USDOE") is required to approve all state Randolph-Sheppard rules and regulations. The USDOE approved Louisiana's regulations in a letter dated February 23, 2015, which directed LWC to provide each vendor with a copy with. accessible copy of rules. (RX-7).
Mr. Cedric Mitchell is a blind vendor licensed by LWC to manage vending facilities in Louisiana's Randolph-Sheppard vending program. At time of the events relevant to this arbitration proceeding, Mr. Mitchell operated the Randolph-Sheppard Vending Facility# 7048, the LWC Cafeteria in Baton Rouge, LA.
Although LWC and Mr. Mitchell dispute whether LWC provided the TAG Manual to Mitchell, it is not disputed that LWC did not provide copies of the Business Enterprise Program ("BEP") manual to blind vendors at a training session on November 4, 2014. (Tr. Page 98, Lines 2-7)
The BEP sets forth progressive discipline procedures in the Louisiana Business Enterprise Program Administrative Action Procedures (Progressive Discipline) before it can be determined that a vendor is to be removed from a facility under the program. If it is determined that a vendor is to be removed from a facility, Section l.B.7 of the BEP mandates that the RSMA (Randolph­ Sheppard Management Assistant or RSPM (Randolph-Sheppard Program Manager) notify a manager of removal from a facility by hand delivery of a written notice of removal. Section l.B.7 also requires that the letter contain certain information, including notice of the "manager's right of appeal."
RSPM Stephen DeBruhl scheduled a meeting with Mr. Mitchell on February 15, 2016 to advise him that he was being removed from his vending facility due to poor performance. DeBruhl also prepared a letter dated February 15, 2016 advising Mr. Mitchell of the decision to remove him from the facility. (RX 4) The meeting was not held on that date and it was rescheduled to February 26, 2016.
At the February 26, 2016 meeting, Mr. DeBruhl read to Mr. Mitchell the letter of February 15th. Mr. DeBruhl also gave Mr. Mitchell a copy of the letter. The February 15th letter advised Mr. Mitchell that he had the right to appeal the agency action, and referred to attached appeal procedures, which were not, in fact, attached.
LAC 67:VU.519 (G) Due Process and Chapter 21, DUE PROCESS PROCEDURES, Sections A and B of the TAG Manual require that any blind vendor dissatisfied with an agency action may request a full evidentiary hearing in writing "within 30 calendar days after receiving written notification of the adverse action and requires that the request he sent via Certified Mail. There is no evidence in the record showing that Mr. DeBruhl advised Mr. Mitchell at the time he read him the February 15th letter that if he chose to appeal, he was required to the appeal within 30 days. Upon discovering that the DUE PROCESS PROCEDURES were not attached to the letter, Mr. DeBruhl scanned a copy of both the letter and due process guidelines and emailed them to Mr. Mitchell on February 26, 2016, the same day as the meeting. The documents were attachments to the email and were in a PDF format. (Tr. Page 147, Lines 16 to 18).
On March 31, 2016, Mr. Mitchell contacted Mr. DeBruhl via email and advised him that he wished to appeal tl1e action and he requested the mailing address for the appeal. (Exhibit RX-3) There was no evidence presented as to whether or not Mr. DeBruhl responded to Mr. Mitchell's request.
On April 8, 2016, Mr. Mitchell mailed his appeal by US Mail as evidenced by the postmark (RX- 1) On or about April 13, 2016, LWC received Mr. Mitchell's appeal as evidenced by the date stamp (RX-1).
Louisiana Workforce Commission Appeal Tribunal Administrative Law Judge Lakisha Jackson denied Mr. Mitchell an evidentiary hearing on the grounds that the appeal was not timely filed in within the 30 days of receipt of the letter notifying him of the revocation of his permit, as mandated by LAC 67:VII.519.
Mr. Mitchell appealed the denial of an evidentiary hearing to the United States Department of Education, which convened this Tripartite Panel of Arbitrators pursuant to the Randolph-Sheppard Act.
The Positions of the Parties
Respondent LWC argues that it followed proper procedures for providing information to Complainant, Cedric Mitchell, a blind vendor, in an accessible format in accordance with the Louisiana Administrative Code (LAC) by verbally reading a letter to Mr. Mitchell on February 26, 2016 (letter dated February 15, 2016) removing him from the facility, hand-delivering said letter to Mr. Mitchell, and on the same day sending information regarding Mr. Mitchell's appeal rights via a PDF attachment to an email addressed to Mr. Mitchell. Further, the LWC asserts that it provided Mr. Mitchell with the Technical Assistance and Guidance ("TAG") Manual during a November 14, 2014 training, as well as offering training participants the option of having the TAG Manual in a DVD format. The TAG Manual also details the appeal rights of a blind vendor in the event an adverse action by LWC, the Louisiana State Licensing Agency (SLA).
The LWC asserts that its employees regularly communicated with Mr. Mitchell via email and text, and that in accordance with LAC 67:VII-513 "...   It is the responsibility of the licensed manager to inform the business consultant of the accessible format needed. " LWC further cites a letter from the U.S. Department of Education, date-stamped February 23, 2015, approving its revised rules and regulations for the Randolph-Sheppard Vending Facility Program, said approval including Section 513 of the LAC. According to the LWC, because the burden to request information in a different format was on Mr. Mitchell, and Mr. Mitchell did not make such a request, then the LWC followed proper procedure for providing information in an accessible format, including notifying the Claimant by PDF attachment to an email of his right to appeal an adverse action with a 30 calendar-day deadline for submitting the appeal request. LWC's position is that Mr. Mitchell's request for an appeal was not timely submitted and therefore his request for a full evidentiary hearing on the merits of the revocation of his permit was properly denied.
Complainant Cedric Mitchell alleges that the Louisiana. Workforce Commission (LWC) violated Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 as amended (hereafter "Section 508") by not providing to him proper written notification in an accessible format of his removal letter and appeal rights after the revocation of Mr. Mitchell's permit as a blind vendor to operate a facility at the LWC's administrative offices in Baton Rouge, Louisiana. Mr. Mitchell asserts that the lack of proper written notification in an accessible format caused him to miss the deadline for timely filing his appeal and thereby prevented him from receiving a full evidentiary hearing regarding the merits of the revocation in violation of his due process rights. To support this assertion Mr. Mitchell relies on the language of Section 508 requiring that "... when Federal agencies develop, procure, maintain, or use electronic and information technology, Federal employees with disabilities have access to and use of information and data that is comparable to the access and use by Federal employees who are not individuals with disabilities ...  ".  The Complainant argues that because the LWC receives Federal funding to implement the Business Enterprise Program under the Randolph Shepherd Act, the LWC is required to follow federal law, specifically Section 508, thus obligating the LWC to ascertain from Mr. Mitchell the type of "accessible format" in which he wanted to receive information.
It is Mr. Mitchell's position that Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act supersedes LAC 67:Vll- 513 that places the burden on the "… licensed manager to inform the business consultant of the accessible format needed. " Mr. Mitchell argues that because he is 100 percent blind, the verbal reading and hand-delivered receipt of a copy of the revocation letter without the mandated appeal rights, and the subsequent email containing the revocation letter and appeal rights in a PDF format by the staff of the LWC to 1'-1r. Mitchell did not constitute adequate notice in an accessible fom1at. Therefore, Mr. Mitchell contends that he was never properly informed of the revocation of his permit, nor his appeal rights, and was thus denied due process. As a remedy, Mr. Mitchell argues that he should receive a full evidentiary hearing on the merits regarding the revocation of his permit.
Conclusions of Law and Analysis
On February 26, 2016, Mr. DeBruhl met with Mr. Mitchell in person and delivered and read to him a letter dated February 15, 2016 notifying him that his permit to operate the LWC cafeteria in Baton Rouge, Louisiana was revoked effective February 26, 2016. The Louisiana Business
Enterprise Program Administrative Action Procedures (Progressive Discipline) Section l.B.7, mandates that the RSMA or RSPM notify Mr. Mitchell of his removal from a facility in writing by hand delivery of a letter containing certain information, including notice of his right of appeal:
The removal letter shall contain the following information:
a) specific reasons for removal from the facility
b) plan of action
c) requirements for obtaining reassignment and
d) notice of the manager's right of appeal (CX-C, page 79)
It is not disputed that although Mr. DeBruhl hand delivered the letter to Mr. Mitchell during their in-person meeting on February 26, 2016, the letter did not include the required "notice of the manager's appeal rights." Therefore, the letter was not compliant with Section l.B.7. With regard to appeal rights, the February 15th letter states: "You have the right to appeal this decision. The appeals process and 'good standing criteria' are attached." Mr. DeBruhl testified that he realized that the referenced appeals process was not attached after the meeting and that he scanned and sent the 6-page document entitled "Chapter 21i Due Process Procedures" by PDF attachment to an email addressed to Mr. Mitchell on the same day as the meeting.
The evidence in the record also establishes that the PDF was not accessible to Mr. Mitchell given the available technology in February 2016. William Boules, Director of Rehabilitation at Vision Rehabilitation Services testified that he is certified by the Academy foe Certification of Vision Rehabilitation and Education Professionals (ACVREP) in four areas: low vision, vision rehab therapy, assistive technology and orientation and mobility. Mr. Boules testified as an expert witness, without rebuttal. that the PDF attachment sent by Mr. DeBruhl was "... not accessible to someone who is visually impaired or blind." Mr. Boules further testified that although technology was widely available in February 2016 to assist visually impaired or blind persons in reading text in emails and Word or Excel attachments to emails, the technology required to read PDFs, which are photographic or "scanned" images and are not text, was very expensive and was generally available only to large users, such as universities. Mr. DeBruhl testified that he did not realize Mr. Boules also testified that at the time of hearing, in March 2021, this technology is widely available at little or no cost until the hearing testimony before this panel that PDF documents were not accessible to blind individuals who use screen readers to access electronic material. (Tr. Page 227, Line 2) Mr. Monk testified that no blind vendor had ever complained that material sent to them by LWC was not accessible.
Mr. DeBruhl testified that he routinely emailed back and forth with Mr. Mitchell including sending attachments in Excel, Word and PDF formats (Tr. Page 147, Lines 6-9) Although there is an example of an Excel attachment in the record (RX-2), there is no corroborating evidence in the record supporting the contention that Mr. Mitchell has previously received a PDF attachment from LWC. Mr. Mitchell testified that prior to February 26, 2016 he never received any documents except for in Excel or Word from Mr. DeBruhl and that those documents were fully accessible. (Tr. Page 281, Lines 19-22 and Tr. Page 289 Lines 16-20) DeBruhl also testified that after sending emails with attachments to Mitchell, he made certain that Mitchell received a copy at the facility. (Tr. Page 147, Lines 7-12) There is no evidence in the record that a hard copy of the appeals process was delivered to Mr. Mitchell. Therefore, the evidence reveals that Mr. DeBruhl sent Mr. Mitchell a copy of the appeals process by PDF attachment to an email, and that the PDF attachment was not accessible to Mr. Mitchell.
Respondent LWC also argues that LAC 67:VII-513 states "upon advance request" LWC will provide written materials in accessible format and that "[i]t is the responsibility of the licensed manager to inform the business consultant of the accessible format needed". (RX- 6) Mr. Boules testified that as a blindness agency subject to the Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act, LWC would have addressed the question of a client's preferred format at the time of the referral application. Although the Complainant Mr. Mitchell alleges in his testimony that his "file" included information that he required Braille or audio, the "file" was not included in the evidentiary record. Although he did not acknowledge that Mr. Mitchell specified that he required braille or audio, Mr. DeBruhl agreed that if the information in the file did so specify, that those specifications were not honored. (Tr. Page 254, Lines 3 through 9). LWC does not argue that Mr. Mitchell selected "PDF" as his format. The evidence in the record does not support a finding that Mr. Mitchell specified any specific format in his file. Even if Mr. Mitchell did not specify a format under LAC 67:VII-513, it does not follow that the Respondent could therefore meet its obligations to provide Mr. Mitchell with a hand-delivered copy of his appeals rights through the transmission of a non-accessible PDF sent by email. Mr. Boules testified that there was no way Mr. Mitchell could have read the due process rights that were sent to him by Mr. DeBruhl by PDF in 2016 without cost prohibitive work arounds or software. Therefore, it is concluded that LWC did not inform Mr. Mitchell on February 26, 2016 that under the due process provisions sent to him in an inaccessible format he had only 30 days from the receipt of the letter revoking his permit to appeal that decision and request an evidentiary hearing.
LWC also argues that as part of a mandatory training on November 14, 2014, Mr. Mitchell, along with other blind vendors in Louisiana, were given the option of receiving the TAG Manual in print or on a DVD. The TAG Manual included appeal due process procedures, including the time limits for filing appeals. Each participant was required to sign an acknowledgment of receipt. While there is some dispute as to whether Mr. Mitchell signed the receipts, Mitchell testified that he attended the training and that he received a DVD, although he was not aware of what the DVD contained. However, even if it is established that Mr. Mitchell received a TAG Manual in an accessible format, LWC would not be relieved of its obligation under Section 1.B.7 of the BEP progressive discipline procedures to hand deliver to Mr. Mitchell the letter notifying him of the revocation of his permit and his removal from the facility and his appeal rights, including the time limits for filing any appeal.
Conclusion and Award
This panel finds that because Mr. Mitchell did not receive a hand delivered copy of his appeal rights and was subsequently notified of his appeal rights in a format not accessible to him and by email, Mr. Mitchell did not receive his notice of his right to appeal, including time limits for appeal, as mandated by Section 1B7 of the BEP progressive discipline procedures. Therefore Mr. Mitchell’s request for an evidentiary hearing on the revocation of his permit as requested by his letter of April 1, 2016 is granted.

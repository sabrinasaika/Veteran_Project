 Aiden is a 28 year old with Autism. He is serious minded, enjoys structure, and likes to stay busy. He 
possesses many innate qualities that make him a strong employee. Occasionally Aiden initiates conver-
sation with others, though he frequently talks to himself quietly, repeating words, phrases, or songs. He 
did not have many social experiences, and required a lot of support in this area. 
 In Aiden’s last year of high school he participated in an internship program at a local hospital.  He had 
three unique internship experiences working in the 1) Radiology Department, 2) Emergency Room, 
and 3) Infection Control. The challenges which he faced at work, and where his job coach provided the 
most support was in regards to his social behaviors. Aiden is tactilely stimulated and he had a tendency 
to invade personal space and rub or touch the person with whom he was interacting. The employment 
specialist used visual aids and role playing scenarios to clearly define the types of contact and space 
that are appropriate. Despite this and other social challenges that Aiden faced he demonstrated 
his skills as a dedicated worker and was ultimately hired  working in the hospital’s Infection Control 
Department. He works 20 hours a week, earns $12.50 per hour, and takes specialized transportation 
to and from work.  The employment specialist continues to provide regular monthly checks with Aiden, 
the supervisor, and coworkers.  Ongoing reminders about space, short role playing sessions, and family 
support have helped Aiden cope with his challenges, and to be a highly valued, “professional.”

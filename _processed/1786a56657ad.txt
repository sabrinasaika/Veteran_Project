Section 111(a)(2)(C) of the Rehabilitation Act authorizes RSA to grant a waiver or modification 
of the MOE requirement if it “is an equitable response to exceptional or uncontrollable 
circumstances affecting the State.” Under the VR program regulations at 34 C.F.R. § 
361.62(d)(1), the Secretary may grant a waiver or modification of the MOE requirement if it is 
necessary to permit the State to respond to exceptional or uncontrollable circumstances that 
cause significant unanticipated expenditures or reduction in revenues that result in a general 
reduction of programs within the State; or require the State to make substantial expenditures in 
the VR program for long-term purposes due to the one-time costs associated with the 
construction of a facility for community rehabilitation program (CRP) purposes, the 
establishment of a facility for CRP purposes, or the acquisition of equipment. To be eligible for a 
MOE waiver, the State must satisfy all relevant criteria set forth at Section 111(a)(2)(C) of the 
Rehabilitation Act and 34 C.F.R. § 361.62(d)(1). 
 
In its August 13, 2024, request for a MOE waiver, BSBP explained it incurred one-time non-
Federal costs for the long-term improvement and modernization of its Information Technology 
(IT) systems and capabilities in FFY 2020 totaling $326,700. In a follow-up email dated 
September 9, 2024, BSBP explained the expenditures were necessary because the BSBP IT 
system was more than 20 years old, and the agency had been put on notice that its “Electronic 
Case Management System (ECMS) was to be deprecated due to a business venture….”  BSBP 
used non-Federal expenditures to acquire new software and pay various migration and hosting 
fees for the updates and upgrades to the IT system. Of the $326,700 in costs incurred for 
updating and upgrading its IT systems and capabilities, BSBP used $245,700 of the FFY 2020 
non-Federal funds to engage in a separate contract with a software company to train BSBP staff 
to self-support and maintain the IT system. According to information provided by the agency, 
BSBP contracted with the company to teach the BSBP staff the nuances of the systems 
programming, including coding, form modification, form creation, software framework, 
workflow, troubleshooting, debugging, building and deploying system changes, and testing.   
 
As noted above, in pertinent part for the type of waiver requested by BSBP, RSA may waive or 
modify the MOE requirement for a State if it determines that such a waiver or modification is 
necessary to permit the State to respond to exceptional or uncontrollable circumstances that 
require the State to make substantial expenditures in the VR program for long-term purposes due 
to the one-time costs associated with the acquisition of equipment

upgrade or replace software whether it is the result of changing requirements, the availability of a

incurred in FFY 2020 to modernize, update, and upgrade its IT systems and capabilities. 
 
For the foregoing reasons, RSA will reduce BSBP’s FFY 2025 Federal VR grant award 
(H126A250100) by $184,454 and MRS’ FFY 2025 Federal VR grant award (H126A250099) by 
$221,993, in accordance with the requirement of 34 C.F.R. § 361.62(c)(2). If you have any 
questions, please contact David Steele, Fiscal Unit Chief, at 202-245-6358 or

Introduction 
The Rehabilitation Services Administration (RSA), a component of the U.S. Department 
of Education (Department), Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services 
(OSERS), provides this report evaluating activities under the Randolph-Sheppard Act.1 
The report describes the purpose, funding, and performance of the vending facility 
program for individuals who are blind established under the Randolph-Sheppard Act 
covering fiscal year (FY) 2023. In addition, the report highlights the technical assistance 
RSA provided during this period to support and improve the vending facility program in 
the States. 
The vending facility program provides individuals who are blind with remunerative 
employment and self-support by providing a priority to operate vending facilities on 
Federal property. The program recruits qualified individuals who are blind, trains them 
on managing and operating small business enterprises, and then licenses qualified blind 
vendors to operate the facilities on Federal and other property. 
At the outset, the program placed sundry stands in the lobbies of Federal office 
buildings and post offices, selling items such as newspapers, magazines, candies, and 
tobacco products. Through the years, the program has grown and broadened from 
Federal locations to include State, county, municipal, and private installations, as well as 
interstate highway rest areas. In addition, vending facility operations expanded to 
include military mess halls, cafeterias, snack bars, cart services, miscellaneous shops, 
and vending machines. 
State vocational rehabilitation (VR) agencies that provide services to individuals who 
are blind may also serve as State licensing agencies (SLAs) under the Randolph-
Sheppard Act. Currently, 51 SLAs are responsible for the management and operation of 
the vending facility programs in 49 States, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico. 
The only State not participating in the program is Wyoming.

by blind vendors, is supported by a combination of Federal VR program funds, State 
appropriations, set-aside funds, which include unassigned Federal vending machine 
income and funds accruing to the SLAs from assessments against the net proceeds of 
blind vendors, and other funds. In FY 2023, a total of $80,084,760 was expended from

pension, health care, and paid vacation/sick leave for individuals participating in the

set-aside assessment funds to increase as locations reopened after the COVID 
pandemic. In FY 2023, approximately $1.66 million more in set-aside assessment funds 
were used to support the vending facility program as compared to FY 2022. It is 
important to note that these set-aside assessment funds, if spent on expenditures that 
are also allowable under Section 103(b)(1) of the Rehabilitation Act for purposes of the 
VR program, may be used by States to meet their Federal match for the VR program; 
however, these expenditures must be calculated as part of the State’s maintenance of 
effort requirement under the VR program because they constitute part of the total 
allowable costs incurred under that program.

Blind Vendor Training and Placement 
In order to determine the effectiveness of the SLA’s training programs (including 
vocational, on-the-job, upward mobility, and post-employment training provided), that 
may help the blind vendors to achieve their maximum employment potential, as set forth 
in 34 C.F.R. § 395.11, the RSA-15 report requests States to provide information on the 
amount of training provided to blind vendors during the fiscal year in three major 
categories. The RSA-15 instructions describe in-service training as those activities that 
are designed to maintain vendor skill levels and improve current operations. This 
training would generally be of short duration, such as the annual vendor’s meeting 
during which some generic training is provided to all in attendance. During FY 2022, 
888 blind vendors received in-service training, representing 57.3 percent nationwide, 
while in FY 2023, 746 individuals received in-service training, representing 52.2 percent 
of blind vendors nationwide.

Upward mobility training are those activities that are designed to enable a vendor to 
achieve increased skill levels necessary for transfer and promotion to more complex 
facilities. This does not include training on rules and regulations, or generic training 
provided to all blind vendors. National consumer conferences sponsored by the National 
Association of Blind Vendors (NABM), and the Randolph-Sheppard Vendors of America 
(RSVA) are examples of upward mobility training. In FY 2022, 764 (49.3 percent) blind 
vendors received upward mobility training with 328 (21.1 percent) of blind vendors 
receiving their training at conferences held by national consumer organizations. In 
FY 2023, 838 (58.7 percent) blind vendors received upward mobility training, with 275 
(19.3 percent) of blind vendors receiving their training at conferences held by national 
consumer organizations.  
Initial training is the collection of training activities which an individual must complete to 
be granted a license by the SLA before they are allowed to operate a vending facility. In 
FY 2022, 44 individuals received a license and began operating a vending facility, while 
in FY 2023, 41 individuals received a license and began operating a vending facility.  
Arbitrations 
Under the Randolph-Sheppard Act (20 U.S.C.§§ 107d-1 and 107d-2), the Department 
convenes arbitration panels to arbitrate disputes between either a blind vendor and 
SLA, or an SLA and Federal agency. The Department uses the interim Policies and 
Procedures for Convening an Arbitration Panel4 to guide it and the arbitration panels in 
carrying out the arbitration process under the Act.  
The Act provides that each party to the dispute appoints their own panel member, and 
the two panel members choose a third neutral panel member, who then serves as the 
Chair of the panel. After the Department convenes and pays the costs of the hearing, it 
has no further involvement with the process. The panel decision is considered a final 
agency decision of the Department for the purposes of any appeal and is  subject to 
judicial review in Federal district court under the Administrative Procedure Act. 
At the start of FY 2023, there were 20 open cases, and the Department received six 
new requests for arbitrations, resulting in a total of 26 open cases in various stages of 
the arbitration process — from requests for arbitration to awaiting final decisions from 
arbitration panels. During this fiscal year seven cases were dismissed, settled, or 
withdrawn; and there were six cases resolved through hearings and panel decisions.

There are many reasons a case may take considerable time to move through the 
arbitration process. For example, the parties may choose to work on settling the dispute 
on their own; they may have difficulties in finding a panel member to serve on their 
behalf, or the selected Chair may not agree to serve, requiring additional time to select 
another Chair. Once a case is fully appointed, the panel schedule is largely beyond the 
Department's control. 
An arbitration program specialist in RSA closely monitors requests and provides parties 
pursuing arbitration with acknowledgement and convening letters, generally on a first-in, 
first-out basis, unless the arbitration request presents novel legal issues or other 
extenuating circumstances. By the end of FY 2023, due to close monitoring, increased 
staffing, and support from OSERS leadership and the Department’s Office of the 
General Counsel, all cases are actively moving through the arbitration process. The 
total number within the process has declined from 16 cases in FY 2022 to 13 cases at 
the close of FY 2023. 
Conflict Resolution 
Given the significant resources expended by the Department and program stakeholders 
on arbitrations, RSA instituted a conflict resolution (CR) initiative in FY 2019, creating a 
means by which parties could receive neutral facilitation from RSA. Blind vendors, 
including their advocates, SLAs, and Federal agencies, who find themselves at an 
impasse regarding aspects, issues, and potential violations of the Randolph-Sheppard 
Act and the implementing regulations are given the opportunity for a CR meeting. 
The objectives of this effort are to encourage resolution of issues by the parties in the 
shortest time and with a minimum of resources, to develop best practices in dealing with 
issues before they disrupt the administration of the program, and to increase respect 
and collaboration between and among blind licensees, SLAs, and Federal agencies. 
In FY 2023, in seven circumstances, CR was initiated, including CR meetings in 
advance of a party filing a complaint. Five of those meetings were successful in 
reaching agreements that resulted in the mutual dismissal of the arbitration cases or 
prevented a complaint from being filed. One case remains open as parties continue to 
work to resolve the underlying disagreement. Lastly, one case resulted in the filing of an 
arbitration request.

U.S AbilityOne Commission  
The Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) Between the U.S. AbilityOne Commission 
(AbilityOne) and RSA was finalized on January 19, 2021, for the purpose of increasing 
communication and understanding between the agencies regarding which opportunities 
are appropriate for persons who are blind to operate vending facilities under the 
Randolph-Sheppard Act and which are appropriate for placement on the Procurement 
List under the Javits-Wagner-O’Day (JWOD) Act. One critical component of the MOU 
was a protocol that AbilityOne would follow to inform RSA when it was provided notice 
of a contract requirement that might implicate a Randolph-Sheppard opportunity. RSA 
would then refer the contract opportunity to the appropriate SLA to determine if it 
wanted to pursue the opportunity under the Randolph-Sheppard priority. The MOU and 
its protocol have significantly improved how stakeholders under both the Randolph-
Sheppard Act and the JWOD Act are notified of and pursue opportunities in their 
respective programs. In FY 2023, AbilityOne notified RSA of six vending opportunities, 
and, of those, two SLAs responded that they were interested in the offers and were 
awarded the facilities, and four SLAs declined the offers due to limited staffing or the 
facility not being financially viable. 
Technical Assistance 
• Webinar to review an equipment exception to benefit the Randolph-Sheppard 
program: On October 6, 2022, a notice was published in the Federal Register: 
Lowering the Per-Unit Acquisition Cost for Equipment Acquired by State 
Licensing Agencies for the Benefit of the Randolph-Sheppard Vending Facility 
Program. The Department implemented an exception, approved by the Office of 
Management and Budget, to lower the per-unit acquisition cost for equipment 
acquired by SLAs for the benefit of the Randolph-Sheppard Vending Facility 
Program. The per unit acquisition cost was lowered from $5,000 or the 
capitalization level established by the non-Federal entity for financial statement 
purposes to “equal or exceed the lesser of $1,000 or the capitalization level 
established by the non-Federal entity for financial statement purposes.” RSA held 
a webinar for stakeholders on October 26, 2022, to review the exception and to 
respond to comments and questions.

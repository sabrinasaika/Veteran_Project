RE: Revised Federal Fiscal Year 2022 Maintenance of Effort Deficit 
 
Dear Chris Wells: 
 
On July 2, 2024, the Rehabilitation Services Administration (RSA) issued a letter to the Georgia 
Vocational Rehabilitation Agency (GVRA) regarding its failure to satisfy the maintenance of 
effort (MOE) requirement under the State Vocational Rehabilitation Services (VR) program 
during Federal fiscal year (FFY) 2022 and, as a result, of the need to reduce your VR program 
grant for FFY 2025 by $562,151,
 the amount of the MOE shortfall. RSA calculated the MOE 
shortfall based on information contained in the Federal Financial Reports (SF-425/RSA-17) 
GVRA submitted for the VR program at the time the letter was issued. 
 
After receiving RSA’s letter, GVRA notified RSA of potential errors in GVRA’s FFY 2022 
RSA-17. The revised non-Federal share for FFY 2022 shows $18,672,393 ($18,563,692 in non-
Federal share of expenditures for 1st - 4th quarters, line 28, and $108,701 in non-Federal share of 
obligations reported on the 4th quarter report, line 29, but liquidated during the carryover year). 
GVRA submitted supporting documentation of the changes requested to RSA prior to any 
revisions being made to the affected financial reports. After RSA’s review of the supporting 
documentation, RSA determined that the agency’s accounting records supported the requested 
revisions. GVRA then entered the corrections to the financial reports noted above via the RSA 
Management Information System.  
 
Based upon the corrections made, GVRA’s FFY 2022 MOE deficit was increased by $108,701. 
Therefore, GVRA’s FFY 2025 VR award will be reduced by the corrected balance of the FFY 
2022 MOE deficit, $670,852. See attached table titled “Revised MOE Analysis for FFY 2022” 
for details. 
GVRA’s internal control processes failed to identify the reporting error until the agency was 
notified of the MOE deficit by RSA. Internal controls must provide for the “reliability of 
reporting for internal and external use” (34 C.F.R. § 361.12). In the VR services portion of the 
Unified or Combined State Plan, GVRA assured RSA that GVRA would employ methods of 
administration to ensure the proper and efficient administration of the VR program. These 
methods of administration (i.e., the agency’s internal controls) must include procedures to ensure

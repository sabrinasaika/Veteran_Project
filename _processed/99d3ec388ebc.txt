Article 2
Business Enterprises Program
8 AAC 98.300 – 8 AC 98.470
8 AAC 98.300 Scope of Regulations
8 AAC 98.300 – 8 AAC 98.480 apply to the Alaska Business Enterprises Program created by Alaska  PL 83-565 and authorized by the Randolph-Sheppard Act found at 20 U.S.C.107. The purpose of the program is to provide blind persons with remunerative employment, enlarging the economic opportunities of the blind and stimulating the blind to greater efforts in striving to make themselves self-supporting.
AAC 98.310 Vending Facility Program Definitions
(a) Accessible Format means a copy of a document in an alternative manner of the individual's choosing that gives that individual who is blind equal access to the information as feasibly and comfortably as a sighted person.
(b) Active Participation means a process through which the Committee of Blind Vendors is provided the opportunity to exert a major influence on program policies, standards, and procedures affecting the overall operation of the vending facility program, with the Commissioner having final responsibility for the administration of the program.
(c) Agency means the Division of Vocational Rehabilitation in the Department of Labor and Workforce Development. which has been designated as the State Licensing Agency.
(d) Blind Person means a person who, after examination by a physician skilled in diseases of the eye or by an optometrist, whichever such person shall select, has been determined to have:
Not more than 20/200 central visual acuity in the better eye with correcting lenses, or
An equally disabling loss of the visual field as evidenced by a limitation to the field of vision in the better eye to such a degree that its widest diameter subtends an angle of no greater than 20 degrees.
(e) Business Enterprises Program (Program) means the program within the Division of Vocational Rehabilitation that provides blind persons the opportunity to manage and operate vending facilities on government and other properties.
(f) Certified for Placement List means a list of blind persons who are not currently managing a vending facility but have met all requirements and are eligible to bid on vacant vending facilities.
(g) Commissioner means the Department of Labor and Workforce Development head.
(h) Committee of Blind Vendors (Committee) means an officially organized entity within State government that meets the requirements of 34 CFR 395.14.
(i)  Conflict of Interest means a situation where an individual and/or a family member is in position to potentially derive personal benefit from the actions or decisions made in their official capacity except that decisions made by the Committee that impact all Blind Managers does not constitute a conflict.
(j) Director means the Director of the Division of Vocational Rehabilitation of the Department of Labor and Workforce Development or the Director's designee.
(k) Disciplinary Probation means placing the Manager's license into disciplinary status up to thirty (30) days, during which time the Licensed Manager must correct deficiencies and/or improve performance or risk suspension or termination of the license. The Licensee may not bid on vacant vending facilities while on probation.
(l) Federal Property means any building, land, or other real property owned, leased, or occupied by any department, Agency, or instrumentality of the United States (including the Department of Defense and the U.S. Postal Service) or any other instrumentality wholly owned by the United States, or by any department or Agency of the District of Columbia or any territory or possession of the United States.
(m) License means a written instrument issued by the State Licensing Agency to a blind person authorizing such person to operate a vending facility on Federal or other property as defined by 34 CFR 395.1(i).
(n)  Licensee means a person licensed by the Division to operate a vending facility on public or other property in Alaska.
(o) Management Services means supervision, inspection, quality control, consultation, accounting, regulating, in-service training, and other related services provided on a systematic basis to support and improve vending facilities operated by blind vendors. Management services do not include those services or costs which pertain to the ongoing operation of an individual facility after the initial establishment period.
(p) Manager means a blind person who is currently assigned to a vending facility.
(q) Net Proceeds means the amount remaining from the sale of articles or services of vending facilities and any vending machine or other income accruing to blind vendors after deducting the cost of such sale and other expenses (excluding set-aside charges required to be paid by such blind vendors).
(r)  Non-Federal Property means property not owned or leased by the Federal government or Federal instrumentality, including property managed by state and local government entities as well as the private sector.
(s) Operating Agreement means the document signed by the Manager and the Agency that outlines the terms and conditions of a vending facility assignment.
(t) Performance Probation means the initial six-month period of a blind individual's first vending facility assignment, during which time the individual must successfully operate the vending facility as a condition for being issued a license to operate a vending facility in the State of Alaska.
(u)Permit means the official approval given to the Agency by a department, Agency, or instrumentality in control of the maintenance, operation, and protection of Federal property or person in control of other property, whereby the Agency is authorized to establish a vending facility.
(v) Person with a Significant  Disability means a person who has one or more physical or mental disabilities that seriously limit the person's functional capacities in terms of regular employment and whose vocational rehabilitation requires multiple vocational rehabilitation services over an extended period of time.
(w) Policy and Procedures Manual means the collection of policies and procedures developed jointly by the Agency and Committee, with the approval of the U.S. Secretary of Education, that govern the day-to-day administration of the Business Enterprises Program, including requirements for the operation of vending facilities.
(x) Program Coordinator means the individual with the Agency who is assigned oversight and supervisory responsibility for the Alaska Business Enterprises Program. If the Department reclassifies the position and changes the job title, the responsibilities and authority bestowed on the Program Coordinator in these rules will automatically transfer to the new title without the need to amend these rules.
(y) Public Property means real or personal property owned or leased by the State or Federal government or an agency of the State or Federal government.
(z) Satisfactory Site means an area fully accessible to vending facility patrons and having:
(1) A minimum of 250 square feet for vending and storage of articles necessary for the operation of a vending facility; and
(2) Sufficient electrical plumbing, heating, and ventilation outlets for the location and operation of a vending facility in accordance with applicable health laws and building codes.
(aa) Set-Aside Funds means funds which accrue to a State licensing agency from an assessment against the net proceeds of each vending facility in the State's vending facility program and any income from vending machines on Federal property which accrues to the State licensing agency.
(bb) Vending Facility means automatic vending machines, cafeterias, snack bars, cart service, shelters, counters, and such other appropriate auxiliary equipment which may be operated by Blind Licensees and which is necessary for the sale of newspapers, periodicals, confections, tobacco products, foods, beverages, and other articles or services dispensed automatically or manually and prepared on or off the premises in accordance with all applicable health laws, and including the vending or exchange of chances for any lottery authorized by State law and conducted by an agency of a State within such State.
(cc) Vending Machine means a coin or currency operated machine which dispenses articles or services, except those machines operated by the United States Postal Service for the sale of postage stamps or other postal products and services, machines providing services of a recreational nature, and telephones shall not be considered to be vending machines.
8 AAC 98.320 Entry-Level Training
Prerequisite Requirements
The Agency, with the active participation of the Committee of Blind Vendors, shall develop criteria for entry into training to become a Licensed Vending Facility Manager. Such criteria shall be incorporated into the Business Enterprises Program's Policies and Procedures Manual. The criteria shall include but are not limited to the following:
Independent living skills
Academic background
Computer skills
Aptitude to provide good customer service
Aptitude to be successful as a vending facility manager
Entry Level Curriculum
The Agency, with the active participation of the Committee of Blind Vendors, shall develop a training program for potential Vending Facility Managers. Training may be provided in a classroom setting or online. The curriculum shall be comprehensive enough to ensure that individuals who complete the training are qualified to manage a vending facility and have a reasonable chance of succeeding. Training shall include training on these rules and the Policies and Procedures Manual. The formal training shall include modules on the Randolph-Sheppard Act and accompanying Federal regulations, business management, operations, different types of vending facilities, health and sanitation, and more. The curriculum will be detailed and included in the Policy and Procedures Manual.
On-the-Job Training
Pursuant to 34 CFR.395.11, entry-level training shall include an on-the-job training component. At the discretion of the Program Coordinator, on-the-job training may be provided at either an existing vending facility operated by a Licensed Blind Vending Facility Manager or at a privately-owned food service business.
Waiver
The Agency, with the active participation of the Committee of Blind Vendors, may develop criteria to be incorporated into the Policies and Procedures Manual for waiving training requirements to be certified for placement into a vending facility. Such a waiver may only be granted to individuals who demonstrate training and/or experience sufficient to offer the individual a reasonable chance of being successful as a Licensed Vending Facility Manager.
Individuals who are granted such a waiver may be certified without going through the Vocational Rehabilitation Program. The Program Coordinator shall arrange for training on Alaska requirements (i.e., reporting, health codes, etc.).
(e) Post-Employment Training
Pursuant to 34 CFR 395.11, the Agency shall make post-employment services available to the individual. Once placed into a vending facility, additional Vocational Rehabilitation services may be required prior to the VR case being closed. The Vocational Rehabilitation Counselor, after collaborating with the Program Coordinator, will make such services available to maximize the chances of the individual succeeding. The individual may reapply for VR services if additional VR services are required after the case is closed.
AAC 98.330 Certification of Trained Managers
Certified for Placement List
Upon completion of all training requirements, an individual's name will be placed on the Certified for Placement List. Being placed on the Certified for Placement List confirms that the Agency has documented that an individual has met all requirements contained in these rules and the Policy and Procedures Manual to qualify to manage a vending facility in Alaska. Being placed on the Certified for Placement List allows for the individual to be placed as a Manager of a vacant vending facility in accordance with 8 AAC 98.370.
Initial Assignment
Individuals on the Certified for Placement List must compete for their initial permanent vending facility assignment pursuant to 8 AAC 98.370. This requirement does not preclude the Agency from assigning a vending facility on a temporary basis pursuant to 8 AAC 98.370(h).
Individuals who have had their training waived pursuant to 8 AAC 98.320(d) will be placed on the Certified for Placement List.
8 AAC 98.340 Licensing of Vending Facility Managers
(a)  Eligibility
The Agency shall issue licenses to only individuals who meet the following criteria:
Legally blind;
A citizen of the United States; and,
Determined by the Agency to be qualified to manage a vending facility operation.
A license shall be issued in the name of the Manager and shall not be transferable.
(b)  Performance Probation
In determining that an individual is qualified to manage a vending facility, the Agency shall require that each individual placed into their initial assignment off of the Certified for Placement List  successfully complete a six-month performance probationary period. They may meet this requirement either as a permanently or temporarily assigned Manager. If they are successful in managing their first assignment for six months, the Agency may issue a license. The Program Coordinator, at their discretion, may extend the probationary period beyond six months if not satisfied that the individual has demonstrated the requisite skills to be successful, and there is reason to believe that with additional time the individual will prove to be successful. During this initial six-month assignment, the Agency shall closely monitor the operation and provide management services to increase the likelihood of success.
(c)  Displaying of License
The Licensed Vending Facility Manager shall post/display the license or a copy of the license in a prominent view in their vending facility.
(d)  Termination of License
The Agency shall issue licenses for an indefinite period of time, but subject to termination if, after affording the Manager an opportunity for a full evidentiary hearing, the Agency finds that the vending facility is not being operated in accordance with:
The rules and regulations governing the program;
The terms and conditions of the permit or contract;
The Operating Agreement;
The Policies and Procedures Manual;
State law, the violation of which is, or reasonably may result in financial or physical harm to the customers of the facility or other persons, the Agency, or the Manager; and/or,
Regulations of other agencies of the State of Alaska which have regulatory authority directly related to the operation of a vending facility, including but not limited to the Departments of Labor & Workforce Development and Department of Health.
The Agency will give thirty (30 ) days written notice in an accessible format in advance of terminating Manager's license, and such advance notice shall be given only after the expiration of a thirty (30) day probationary period, except as provided in 8AAC 98.410(c) and 8AAC98.650(a)(4).  The notice shall advise the Manager of their right to appeal the decision in accordance with 8 AAC 98.460
(e)  Non-Disciplinary Termination of License
The Agency must revoke the Manager's license if:
The Manager resigns or retires from the program;
The Manager has an extended illness of six months or longer with a medically documented diagnosis of Manager's incapacity to operate a facility;
The Manager fails to meet the definition of blindness as set forth in 34 CFR § 395.1(c). At its discretion, the Agency may require the Manager to undergo an ophthalmologic examination to verify blindness. If an examination is required, the Agency will select the doctor and will pay for the office visit.
(f)  Operating Agreement
Execution
On the first day of business, the Agency and the Manager, or any individual from the Certified for Placement List being assigned to a vending facility, shall execute a document known as the "Operating Agreement," which shall be developed by the Agency with the active participation of the Committee and included in the Policy and Procedures Manual. The Operating Agreement designates the assignment as temporary or permanent and clearly defines the responsibilities of the Agency and those of the Manager to ensure the effective and efficient operation of the vending facility to which the Manager has been assigned. The Operating Agreement shall be issued for an indefinite period of time but subject to termination for cause.
Exhibits to the Operating Agreement
The following documents shall be attached to and made a part of the Operating Agreement:
Occupancy Permit or Contract (if available); and,
Equipment Inventory Record;
The Operating Agreement and all attachments must be provided to the Manager in an accessible format.
(g)  Inactive Managers
If a Blind Licensee has been inactive for a period of three (3) years or more, a refresher course may be required before that individual can compete for a permanent assignment to a vacant vending facility through the normal bid process. The Agency, after active participation by the Committee, shall have the discretion to determine the extent of training necessary to ensure the individual is capable of being successful. For purposes of this section, "inactive" means they have maintained their license by meeting the requirements of AAC 98.360 but have not managed a vending facility either as a permanent or temporary assignment in at least three years.
(h)  Reapplying for a License
If a Blind Licensee has their license terminated for any reason, the individual may apply for Vocational Rehabilitation services and, if determined eligible, seek re-entry into the program. If accepted,  the individual must meet all eligibility requirements outlined in this section, including successfully completing all training. There is no obligation to allow an individual back into the program and will consider the reason(s) for leaving when making such a determination. If Manager's license is terminated for cause, that individual will not be allowed back into the program unless all deficiencies have been addressed.  If the individual is left owing any monies to the Agency or any other state or Federal entity, the payment of the past due debt must be liquidated by the individual before being allowed to enter training. An individual who returns to the program pursuant to this section will not regain the previous seniority earned.
8 AAC 98.350 Emergency Removal of a Vending Facility Manager
The Agency shall remove a Manager from a vending facility if a situation develops that prevents a Manager from fulfilling their obligations or if there is reasonable evidence of a hazardous situation involving the Manager which poses an immediate threat to the safety of the Manager or others. This removal may be immediate if the circumstances require it. At the time of the removal, the Agency shall advise the Licensed Vending Facility Manager of their right to appeal the Agency's action. Prior to or within twenty-four (24) hours of the removal, the Agency shall contact the Chairperson of the Committee and inform them of the action.
In the event of Manager's removal under paragraphs (a) of this subsection, the Agency must investigate the circumstances surrounding the removal and, within fifteen (15) working days, do one or more of the following:
Return the Manager to the facility if it is determined the Manager was not at fault and the Agency believes there is still an opportunity for the Licensed Vending Facility Manager to be successful at that location;
Mandate retraining if, in the opinion of the Agency additional training will rectify the situation;
Terminate the Operating Agreement, but place Manager's name on the Certified for Placement List and allow the Manager to bid on any vacant vending facilities;
Initiate disciplinary action against Manager's license pursuant to AAC 38.400.
8 AAC 98.360 Continuing Education and Upward Mobility Training
Licensed Vending Facility Managers already participating in the Business Enterprise Program and operating facilities, as well as individuals on the Certified for Placement List, will be required to obtain additional training for the purpose of maintaining their licenses and eligibility to bid on vacant vending facilities. The intent is to set the expectation for Licensees to update their skills, gain new skills to assist them in qualifying for higher-level facilities and improve and refresh their current abilities.
Each Licensee will be required to accrue a minimum number of training credits over a two-calendar year period.
The Agency, with the active participation of the Committee, shall outline in the Policies and Procedures Manual how training credits can be obtained and the point value that can be accrued for each training activity.
It is incumbent upon the Agency to provide enough training or provide financial assistance that will enable the Licensed Vending Facility Managers and individuals on the Certified for Placement List to fulfill these requirements.
If a Manager or individual on the Certified for Placement List fails to meet the minimum training requirements at the conclusion of two years, the individual's license shall be placed in probationary status  pursuant to 8 AAC 98.400 (a) and afforded one final opportunity to get the training. Failure to do so shall result in termination of the Managers' license and/or having their name removed from the Certified for Placement List.
The Program Coordinator may grant exceptions to the training requirement if a Manager or individual on the Certified for Placement List experiences an extended illness of six months or longer that prevents them from participating in training.
8 AAC 98.370 Assignment of Vending Facility Managers
(a)	Eligibility to Bid
When new vending facilities are established and/or existing facilities become vacant, all current Licensed Vending Facility Managers and those individuals on the Certified for Placement List are eligible to bid provided:
Their license is not in probationary status, nor is their license currently suspended;
They are not delinquent in the filing of any report required by the State of Alaska;
They are not delinquent in the payment of any financial obligation owed to the State of Alaska;
There are no known past-due debts with any wholesalers, suppliers, or any other facility-related debts;
They have provided a certificate verifying proof of liability insurance;
They have been in their current assignment for a full six (6) months.
(b)	Notice
All Licensed Vending Facility Managers and individuals on the Certified for Placement List shall be notified of any vacant vending facilities in an accessible format. The Policies and Procedures Manual shall specify how the notification will be provided and may include mail, email, telephone, etc. The bid announcement shall, as a minimum, include the following:
The location of the vending facility;
Whether it is a primary or secondary site;
Whether it is a permanent or temporary assignment;
Description of the vending facility;
Estimated number of prospective patrons;
Operating hours of the facility;
A general idea of the items to be sold;
Condition of the facility;
Projected net sales for the facility and estimated net proceeds;
Any special circumstances that may exist; and,
Specific training that must have been completed;
The bid closing date.
The bid announcement shall include a space for the individual to sign indicating their interest in being considered as well as a statement whereby they agree to the release of pertinent information about their past performance to the interview panel.
(c)	Submitting a Bid
If a Blind Licensee or individual on the Certified for Placement List wishes to bid on the vending facility, they will fill out the Bid Application, sign it, and return it to the Program Coordinator on or before the closing date in the manner described in the Policy and Procedures Manual.   As each bid is received, it will be dated, and the time of receipt will be noted. Any bids received after the closing date will not be considered. Bids will remain open for ten (10) working days. Following the closing of bids, the Program Coordinator will make a list of all bids received on or before the closing date. The list will indicate the date and time the bid was received and will be kept on file in the Program Coordinator's office.
(d)	The Selection Panel
After the candidates have been determined eligible to bid, a selection panel shall interview each candidate. The panel shall consist of the following:
The Program Coordinator or their designee;
The Chairperson of the Committee or their designee; and,
One person is jointly determined by the other two panel members. The third-panel member may be an independent business person, a property management official, or another person familiar with the Business Enterprises Program.
the Agency and Chairperson of the Committee shall ensure that no one serving on a panel has a conflict of interest.
If no Manager is available or willing to participate as an interview panel member, the Program Coordinator shall have the discretion to appoint a third-panel member. If practical, interviews shall be conducted within fifteen (15) working days after the bid closing date.
(e)	Ranking of Candidates
The panel shall rank each candidate based on their record of performance for the preceding twelve (12) months, responses received to questions, the candidate's plans for the new operation, professional demeanor, and attitude. The panel shall ensure that when ranking candidates, no individual is discriminated against based upon sex, age, physical or mental impairment, creed, race, color, religion, national origin, or political affiliation and will comply with all applicable Federal, State, and local laws related to discrimination. Each panel member shall rank the candidates according to the following:
First Choice......................10 Points
Second Choice..................8 Points
Third Choice.....................6 Points
Fourth Choice...................4 points
Fifth Choice......................2 points
The ranking shall be done by ballot. The Chairperson of the panel, who shall be the Agency representative, shall collect the ballots and tabulate the ratings, which shall be verified by the other panel members. The individual with the highest score shall be awarded the vending facility. In the event there is a tie, the individual with the highest score and greatest amount of seniority shall be awarded the facility. The successful candidate shall be immediately offered the award. The chosen candidate shall have up to twenty-four (24) hours to accept or reject the award. If the award is refused, the individual shall be precluded from having any further bids considered until one year has elapsed from the date of the refusal. If the individual is scheduled for more than one interview as a result of their bids on a number of facilities contained in a single bid package, then they may wait until the conclusion of the last interview in which the individual participates to accept or reject the award of a facility.
It is understood that property management may impose additional selection criteria for Managers on their properties. This may include, but is not limited to, such things as security clearance, special training, and random drug testing if such additional conditions are also required of property management's contractors. If a Manager who is awarded a facility fails to meet the additional selection criteria, the Manager with the next highest score who meets the additional selection criteria will be awarded the facility.
(f)	Secondary Facilities
Some vending facilities will not produce enough income to provide a livelihood for a blind person. Expected income levels for primary and secondary facilities will be specified in the Policies and Procedures Manual. In such events, the sites may be treated as secondary facilities and added to an existing vending facility. If the goal is to permanently or temporarily attach a site as a secondary facility to an existing vending facility, the bidding process outlined above shall be followed. The bid announcement shall clearly indicate that the facility is a secondary facility and whether or not it is being offered as a permanent attachment or a temporary attachment. If it is temporary, the Agency shall ensure that the Manager who is selected will be allowed to operate the site for a minimum of twelve (12) months.
(g)	If only one individual bids on a vacant facility, that person shall be awarded the facility without an interview, provided they are otherwise eligible to be assigned to the facility.
(h)	Temporary Assignments
In the event that no other Manager or individual on the Certified for Placement List bids on a vacant vending facility, the Agency, in an effort to preserve the opportunity for a blind person in the future, may offer the assignment for a period of time to a person who is not blind on a temporary basis. Preference shall be given to an unemployed person with a significant disability who possesses the skills necessary to successfully manage the business. This temporary assignment shall not exceed twelve (12) months. At the conclusion of that twelve (12) month period, the vending facility shall be announced for bid to Blind Managers and individuals on the Certified for Placement List. If no blind person is interested, the facility may continue under temporary operation for an additional twelve (12) months. However, after the second twelve (12) month period has elapsed for a site on Federal property, the Agency must either assign the vending facility to a Blind Manager on a permanent or temporary basis or close the facility. This ensures compliance with Technical Assistance Circular 21-02.
Emergency Assignment or Temporary Assignments Due to Unique Circumstances
Unique circumstances may dictate that the Agency make an emergency assignment on a temporary basis without announcing the facility for bid. This may occur when:
A Manager resigns without notice;
The Agency is forced to remove the current Manager on an emergency basis;
The current Manager passes away;
No one bids on a vacant facility that has been announced as available through the standard bidding process, or,
Other unique circumstances.
In such instances, the Agency may make a temporary assignment. Such temporary assignments shall not exceed twelve (12) months pursuant to subsection (h) above.
In the event of the establishment of a new vending facility, there may not be historical sales figures available upon which a bid could be based. Under such circumstances, the facility may be awarded on a temporary basis until such time as sales can be established.
In considering temporary assignments, preference shall be granted in the following order:
Persons on the Certified for Placement list;
A Blind Manager who is currently managing another vending facility; and,
An unemployed person with a significant disability possesses the skills necessary to successfully manage the business.
Any other individual or company who is capable of operating the vending facility.
8 AAC 98.380 Responsibilities of the Vending Facility Manager
(a)	The responsibilities of the Manager are outlined in the Operating Agreement. By signing the Operating Agreement, the Manager agrees to:
Comply with the rules and regulations governing the Business Enterprises Program and the Policies and Procedures Manual, including meeting all performance standards as outlined in the Policies and Procedures Manual;
Comply with the requirements of other government entities that may have jurisdiction over the operation of vending facilities, such as the Department of Health and the Department of Revenue.
Abide by the Managers' Code of Conduct;
Be responsible for having the vending facility open during the hours specified in the permit/contract;
Be accountable to the Agency for the proceeds of the business of the vending facility and handle the proceeds, including payments to suppliers and deposits of funds;
Conduct all aspects of the business without discriminating on the basis of sex, age, physical or mental impairment, creed, race, color, religion, national origin, or political affiliation and will comply with all applicable Federal, State, and local laws related to discrimination.
Carry on the business of the vending facility in compliance with applicable health laws and regulations and in accordance with regulations of other government entities with jurisdiction over the business operation;
Maintain a neat and clean businesslike appearance while working at the vending facility and conduct business in an orderly, businesslike manner;
Take proper care of the equipment of the vending facility and make alterations or changes therein only with the written approval of the Agency;
Notify the Agency within a reasonable amount of time in advance of taking any voluntary leave from the vending facility and as soon as possible with respect to any involuntary leave. Such notification is only required for absences exceeding three (3) days;
Ensure that the facility is properly staffed during the absence of the Manager due to illness or vacation to ensure no interruption in service. The salary of such staff will be an allowable expense to the vending facility where the service is performed;
Keep such records and make such reports as the Agency will require;
Provide housekeeping, janitorial, and exterminating services required for the vending facility and not provided by the property Manager;
Maintain a business banking account separate from Manager's personal banking accounts;
Establish selling prices for the vending facility stock in trade that are comparable to market prices in the general vicinity of the vending facility;
Be responsible for safeguarding money, stock in trade, and supplies. The Manager will be responsible for absorbing any losses or reduction in the value of stock regardless of cause;
Report promptly to the Agency any complaints or criticism by the Property Manager and work with property management and the Agency to resolve any identified issues;
Report promptly any significant malfunctioning or damaged equipment, loss by fire, damage, theft, or any other cause to the Agency;
Report to the Agency the number of vending facility employees and the number who are known to be legally blind and/or otherwise disabled, so the Agency can accurately complete Federal reports, which must be submitted to the US Department of Education;
Pay the Agency the full value of any initial stock provided in accordance with the payment schedule developed by the Agency with the active participation of the Committee of Blind Vendors. Failure to make timely monthly payments will result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of license;
Operate all machines assigned to their facility. A Manager shall not be allowed to transfer machines to full service or allow a third party to place their machines in a vending facility, and the Manager simply receives a commission without the written approval of the Agency.
Comply with requirements of property management, including ensuring a drug-free workplace.
The Agency shall be responsible for providing training to ensure that the Manager understands and can carry out all of their responsibilities.
8 AAC 98.390 Responsibilities of the Agency
The Agency shall:
Administer the program in strict adherence to the Randolph-Sheppard Act and its implementing regulations, these rules and regulations, and the Policies and Procedures Manual;
Apply all rules and policies uniformly;
Provide management services to ensure adequate oversight and supervision of all vending facilities;
Ensure that in all aspects of administering the program that it does not discriminate on the basis of sex, age, physical or mental impairment, creed, race, color, religion, national origin, or political affiliation and will comply with all applicable Federal, State, and local laws related to discrimination.
Provide the necessary equipment for the vending facility to carry out the business required by the permit/ contract and to ensure quality customer service;
Maintain the equipment at the vending facility in good repair, and replace obsolete and worn-out equipment as necessary, subject to the availability of funds;
Furnish the Manager with a complete inventory listing in an accessible format of all equipment, initial stocks, and cash provided;
Provide the Manager with a copy of the permit/contract in an accessible format;
Ensure that Managers are afforded the opportunity to receive training and retraining, including  upward mobility training;
Receive and respond to grievances from Licensed Vending Facility Managers and ensure that such aggrieved Licensees are afforded the right to due process as prescribed in AAC 98.460.
With the active participation of the Committee, develop a Policies and Procedures Manual which shall provide detail on the application of these rules;
Provide all Licensed Vending Facility Managers and individuals on the Certified for Placement List with a copy of the program rules and Policy and Procedures Manual in an accessible format and provide training to ensure to the extent practicable that Managers understand them;
Ensure the ongoing active participation by the Committee of Blind Vendors in all major administrative decisions affecting the overall operation of the Business Enterprises Program;
Survey opportunities and develop new vending facility sites and locations to be operated by Licensed Vending Facility Managers; and,
Manage program resources, including revenue from all sources, in a responsible manner that supports the intent of the Randolph-Sheppard Act.
In order to accomplish 8 AAC 98.390(a) above, the Agency shall maintain the Business Enterprises Program Revolving Fund. This fund consists of the following:
Commissions received from vending machines operated by third-party vendors  in non-federal buildings throughout the State of Alaska where there are no Blind Managers assigned and as authorized in AS 23.15.130;
Vending machine income received from Federal entities pursuant to the Code of Federal Regulation 34 CFR 395.32;
Funds are received from a set-aside fund to which the Licensed Vending Facility Managers contribute from their net proceeds each month.
These three sources of funds shall be accounted for separately in order to comply with Federal spending guidelines on the various funds. Funds not spent in a given fiscal year shall be rolled over to the next fiscal year and be available for use for the benefit of the BEP.
8 AAC 98.400 Placing a License in Probationary Status
(a)	Probation
A Licensed Vending Facility Manager's license may be placed in probationary status when the Agency determines that the vending facility is not being operated in accordance with the following:
The rules and regulations governing the program;
The Policies and Procedures Manual;
The terms and conditions of the permit/contract;
The terms and conditions of the Operating Agreement;
State law, the violation of which is, or reasonably may result in financial or physical harm to the customers of the facility or other persons, the Agency, or the Manager; or
Regulations of other agencies of the State or local governments which have regulatory authority directly related to the operation of a vending facility, including but not limited to the Departments of Health.
(b)	Notice of Probationary Status
Notice of the license being placed in probationary status shall be provided to the Manager in an accessible format. The inception of probation shall be the date upon which the notice is received by the Manager. The notice of disciplinary probation shall contain the reason(s) the license is being placed in probationary status, steps to be taken, if any, to avoid termination, Manager's right to appeal the Agency's action, and information about contacting a member of the Committee to serve as an advocate. The probationary period shall be for a mandatory thirty (30) days. At the conclusion of the thirty (30) days, if the identified issues have not been adequately corrected, the Agency may extend the probation or continue with additional disciplinary action.
(c)	Right to Bid
Managers whose licenses are placed in probationary status pursuant to this Part will not be permitted to bid on vacant vending facilities while the license is in probationary status.
Repeated Probations
If Manager's license is placed in disciplinary probationary status for the same offense for the third time during a twelve (12) month period, Manager's license will be terminated upon conclusion of the third probationary period and after first affording the Manager with an opportunity for a full evidentiary hearing.
8 AAC 98.410 Set Aside Fees
(a)	Establishing a Set-Aside Fee
The set-aside fee is a charge levied against the net proceeds of each vending facility. The Manager is responsible for the payment to the Agency of this assessment each month. It represents a certain percentage of the net proceeds realized as a result of the facility's operation. Incentives and/or discounts may be offered to those Managers who meet established performance standards, hire legally blind employees, or meet other established requirements. Such incentives and/or discounts shall be detailed in the Policies and Procedures Manual.
In determining net proceeds, a Manager may deduct legitimate business expenses from net sales. Categories of allowed deductions are detailed in the Policies and Procedures Manual. The amount of set-aside assessment may not be deducted as an expense in computing net proceeds.
The percentage of net proceeds to be paid to the Agency by each Manager is predicated upon a schedule negotiated between the Agency and the Committee and incorporated into the Policies and Procedures Manual. The amount shall be determined to be sufficient for the program's operation while at the same time allowing for the retention of reasonable reserves by the Agency. The collection of set aside fees may be suspended if there are sufficient dollars to manage the Program and the Agency and Committee agrees on said suspension.
The set-aside fee to be charged requires the approval of the Secretary of the US Department of Education prior to implementation.
(b)	Purposes for Which Set Aside Fees can Be Used
Pursuant to the provisions of 34 CFR §395.9, funds derived from set-aside assessments can only be used for the following purposes:
The purchase of new equipment;
Maintenance and replacement of equipment;
Management services;
Assuring a fair minimum of returns to vendors; and
The establishment and maintenance of retirement or pension plan, health insurance contributions, and paid sick leave and vacation time, if it is so determined by a majority vote of Blind Managers licensed by the Agency after such Agency provides to each such Manager information on all matters relevant to such proposed purposes.
The Agency shall maintain sufficient records to assure the reasonableness of the use of set-aside assessments in the operation of the BEP and for those purposes designated.
(c)	Requirements for Financial Reporting
Each Vending Facility Manager must file with the Agency a monthly financial report of their business operation. The report and payment of set-aside assessments currently due the Agency shall be due on the 25th day of the month following the month in which the business was transacted. Reports shall be submitted in the format and method required by the Agency. The Agency must ensure that the required report is in an accessible format.
(d)	Failure to File Reports or Pay Set Aside Fees
If a Manager fails, without the showing of good cause, to file the monthly financial report and/or fails to make payment of set-aside assessments as required by these rules, Manager's license shall be placed in probationary status for a period of thirty (30) days for either one or both of these infractions. For each offense, the Agency shall assess the Manager a penalty of $25.00.
(e)	Falsification of Reports
If a Manager falsifies a report filed with the Agency, as validated by the Agency or other state entity, Manager's license will be terminated without placing the Manager on probation. The termination will be preceded by a thirty (30) day written notice by the Agency advising the Manager of such action and their right to a full evidentiary hearing.
AAC 98.420 Establishment of Vending Facilities on Non-Federal Properties
The person responsible for the maintenance and operation of any State controlled public property shall not allow the establishment of a vending facility without the approval of the Commissioner. If the person responsible for the maintenance and operation of any public property wishes to have vending facility services, the Commissioner shall be notified in writing. It is the responsibility of the public property manager to ensure that a satisfactory site is provided to establish a vending facility to be operated by a person who is blind, if feasible. The Commissioner, through the Program Coordinator, shall determine the type of vending facility to be established, which may include but is not limited to automated vending machines, snack bars, cafes, cafeterias, C-Stores, micromarkets, and gift shops. Vending facilities are not restricted to selling only food products.
Typically, a satisfactory site must have enough square footage to enable the BEP to establish a facility that can effectively provide the desired services. The minimum requirements for vending facilities on Federal property are outlined in 34 CFR 395.1. The actual site must have enough square footage to ensure that a quality service can be provided according to the type of facility the Agency intends to operate. The Commissioner may make exceptions to the square footage minimum in buildings where it only wants to provide a limited service.
The Commissioner, through the Program Coordinator, shall survey the public and non-federal properties to determine the feasibility of establishing a vending facility. If the determination is made that a vending facility is feasible, a permit or contract will be initiated between the entity and the Agency if the property is maintained and operated by the State or any agency of the State. The contract shall set forth the terms of the operation. The permit or contract shall ensure that quality food will be provided at reasonable prices. If the property is owned or leased by the Federal government, the Agency shall make an application for a permit except in the cases of cafeterias when a contract will be executed.
The Program Coordinator will also be responsible for surveying potential opportunities on Federal and non-federal properties and creating opportunities for blind persons to manage and operate vending facilities. This includes vending facilities in the private sector.
The Agency, with the active participation of the Committee, shall develop minimum sales criteria for opening new vending facilities. These criteria shall be outlined in the Policies and Procedures Manual. Nothing in this Part shall be construed to limit any one Manager in terms of sales.
8 AAC 98.430 Equipment, Fixtures, Stock, and Initial Operating Funds
(a)	Providing Equipment for Use in Vending Facilities
The Agency will furnish each vending facility with adequate, suitable equipment and fixtures for the establishment and satisfactory operation of such facility. Such equipment shall be provided at no cost to the Manager. All rights to, title to and interest in the equipment and fixtures remain vested with the Agency.
(b)	Managers Adding Equipment
The Manager may not acquire, either through purchase or lease, any equipment to be added to the vending facility without the prior written approval of the Agency. The Manager may not allow a third party to service State-owned equipment without the prior approval of the Agency.
(c)	Full-Service Vending
The Agency, with the active participation of the Committee, may elect not to purchase beverage machines and allow the Manager to arrange for the placement of machines by a bottling company. The arrangements made will be between the Manager and bottler and may include placing the machines on full-service with the Manager receiving a commission. Under no other circumstances will a Manager be allowed to outsource any portion of their business to a third party without the approval of the Agency.
(d) 	Maintenance and Replacement of Equipment
The Agency will maintain (or cause to be maintained) all vending facility equipment in good repair and in attractive condition and will replace (or cause to be replaced) worn out or obsolete equipment as required to assure the continued successful operation of the facility. Specific procedures on how these repairs will be performed will be outlined in the Policies and Procedures Manual. A repair and maintenance deductible or co-pay may be required of the Manager depending upon the availability of funds.
(e)	Initial Stock
The Vocational Rehabilitation Counselor shall provide initial stock and operating capital in sufficient amounts to ensure a Manager can successfully begin operations in their initial assignment. If the Manager accepts another assignment, it will be that Manager's responsibility to provide initial inventory at the amount provided by the VR Counselor. They may either take the merchandise with them to their new assignment or sell it to the incoming Manager and use the money to buy stock for the new assignment. If the new assignment requires more stock than that originally provided by the VR Counselor, the Agency shall provide the additional amount in accordance with the Policy and Procedures Manual. Under no circumstances shall a Manager in good standing be denied an opportunity due to inability to pay for initial inventory.
8 AAC 98.440 Use of Teaming Partners
(a)	When Teaming Partners Are Permissible
Some vending facility operations are very complex and require large capital investments and cash flow that most Managers do not enjoy. Cafeterias are the best example, especially military troop dining contracts. The Agency may require the use of a teaming partner or allow a Manager to utilize a teaming partner upon request. When feasible, the Manager shall be allowed to select a teaming partner with the approval of the Agency. If a teaming partner is required, the Agency shall provide the Manager with a list of potential teaming partners it has approved.
(b)	Criteria for a Teaming Partner
In selecting a teaming partner, the Manager must ensure that the following criteria are met:
The teaming partner has experience in the type of operations to be operated with the Manager.
The teaming partner has demonstrated that they have the financial resources to support the operation, including cash flow to pay employees while waiting on payment from the government.
The Agreement between the Manager and teaming partner shall be based on profit sharing, with the Manager receiving a minimum of 51% of the profit from the operation.
The Agreement between the Manager and teaming partner must show the training that will be provided to the Manager by the teaming partner. The Manager is required to be trained in all aspects of the operation.
The Manager must be engaged in the day-to-day operations of the business and an active participant in all major decisions.
(c)	Exceptions can be made, and the Agency may select the teaming partner in situations where current contracts bind the Agency to a teaming partner, or unusual circumstances exist that require the Agency to move forward with a teaming partner in order to submit a proposal in response to a solicitation. The latter shall only apply if it is not practical to select the Manager first through the bidding process. Any decision to make such an exception is subject to active participation by the Committee.
8 AAC 98.450 Collection and Distribution of Unassigned Income
(a)	Vending Machine Income from Federal Property
In the event the Agency receives income from vending machines on Federal property which may or may not be in direct competition with a Licensed Vending Facility Manager, the Agency will be guided by 34 CFR § 395.8 in distributing any such funds to that Manager. Any funds not distributed to a licensed Manager shall be used by the Agency in accordance with 34 CFR §395.8(c). Technical Assistance Circular 21-02 will guide the Agency on the management of vending on Federal property.
(b)	Vending Machines Income from Non-Federal Property
Vending machine income derived from vending machines on State or other public property shall accrue to the Agency. Such funds shall be deposited into the Agency's Small Business Enterprises Revolving Fund. At the conclusion of each fiscal year, the Agency, with the active participation of the Committee, shall determine the purposes for which the vending machine income may be allocated. The purposes for which vending machine income from non-federal property may be used but are not limited to the same as for setting aside funds.
8 AAC 98.460 Due Process
(a)	Administrative Review
A Blind Licensee who is dissatisfied with any Agency action arising from the administration and operation of the program is entitled to a full evidentiary hearing. However, the goal is to resolve such complaints in a less formal way, if possible. The Licensee or their representative must file a request within thirty (30) calendar days of the Agency action or notice of said action. The request must be in writing and must state the issues to be resolved.   The administrative review process is detailed in the Policies and Procedures Manual. Under no circumstances shall an administrative review be required as a condition for being afforded an evidentiary hearing.
(b)	Impartial Due Process Hearing
Any Blind Licensee who is dissatisfied with any action arising out of the administration or operation of the program may file a request for an evidentiary hearing within thirty (30) calendar days of any Agency action or notice of such action or within fifteen (15) calendar days of receipt of a decision in an administrative review. An administrative review is not a prerequisite for an evidentiary hearing. The evidentiary hearing shall be governed by the Administrative Procedures Act (AS 44.62.330 – AS 44.62.640). The process is detailed in the Policies and Procedures Manual.
(c)	Arbitration
Any Blind Licensee dissatisfied with any action taken or decision rendered as a result of an evidentiary hearing may file a request to the Secretary of the US Department of Education to convene an arbitration panel to hear the case pursuant to 34 CFR 395.13.
8 AAC 98.470 Committee of Blind Vendors
(a)	Composition
All Blind Licensees who are actively managing a vending facility under an Operating Agreement shall automatically be members of the Committee of Blind Vendors.
(b)	Officers
The Agency shall conduct a biennial election of officers. The members of the Committee shall elect a President, Vice President, and Secretary. The election shall take place at a meeting of all Managers for which at least two weeks advance notice is provided by the Agency. If an officer position becomes vacant for any reason, the Agency shall conduct an election to fill the vacancy. If the position of President becomes vacant for whatever reason, the Vice President shall assume the duties of President, and a special election will be held to elect a new Vice President.
(c)	Function of the Committee
Pursuant to 34 CFR 395.14, the Committee shall:
Actively participate with the Agency in major administrative decisions and policy and program development decisions affecting the overall administration of the Agency's Vending Facility Program.
Receive and transmit to the Agency grievances at the request of Blind Licensed Vendors and serve as advocates for such vendors in connection with such grievances.
Actively participate with the Agency in the development and administration of a system for the promotion and transfer of Blind Licensed Vendors.
Actively participate with the Agency in the development of training and retraining programs for Blind Licensed Vendors.
Sponsor, with the assistance of the Agency, meetings and instructional conferences for Blind Licensed Vendors within the State subject to the Agency having funds available for such meetings and/or conferences.
(d)	Active Participation
The intent of the active participation requirement is to ensure that the Committee is able to influence major decisions affecting the overall operation of the program. Technical Assistance Circular 21-01 will serve as guidance for ensuring the active participation of the Committee.
(e)	Committee By-Laws
The Committee shall establish by-laws to govern its operation. The by-laws must be approved by a majority vote of all Committee members attending a regularly scheduled meeting at which there is a quorum. Any proposed by-law changes shall be sent out to all Licensed Vending Facility Managers in an accessible format at least ten (10) days prior to any scheduled vote.
Access to Program Information
The Committee shall be provided access to all financial data of the State Licensing Agency relevant to the operation of the Business Enterprises Program, including quarterly and annual financial reports. Such data shall be made available in an accessible format. At the request of a blind vendor, the Agency shall arrange a convenient time to assist in the interpretation of such financial data. No confidential information about individual blind persons shall be shared. As a minimum, the reports shall show funds coming into the program, including set aside collections as well as Federal and State unassigned funds, state appropriations devoted to the program, and Federal Vocational Rehabilitation dollars that are made available. Additionally, the reports should disclose expenditures by categories, including the purchase of equipment, repair and maintenance, management services, Manager Benefits, and others. The RSA-15 report that the Agency submits to the Department of Education each year may serve as the annual report.

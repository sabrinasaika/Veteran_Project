Applicable Standards
Congress enacted the Randolph-Sheppard Act (RSA) in 1936, which has been amended through 1974. 20 U.S.C., § 107, et. seq.
The RSA and its implementing regulations establish the overall framework for the program involving the awarding of vending contracts to blind vending managers to operate vending facilities on government properties. 20 U.S.C. §§107 d-1(a), 107 d-2, and the implementation regulations at 34 C.F.R. § 395.13.
The RSA bifurcates responsibilities at the federal and state levels. At the federal level, responsibility is the Secretary of Education who interprets and enforces provisions of the RSA and designates state licensing agencies to administer the program at the state level.
The RSA and regulations provide for an arbitration process if the vending manager is dissatisfied with the outcome of any proceedings at the state level and can file a complaint with the United States Secretary of Education, who will then authorize an ad-hoc arbitration panel to hear and decide the dispute. § 107d-1.
Findings of Fact
In Missouri, the state licensing agency (SLA) authorized to administer the Randolph-Sheppard Program is the Missouri Department of Social Services, Rehabilitation Services for the Blind.
The Missouri Department of Social Services, Rehabilitation Services for the Blind (RSB or Respondent), is an executive department of the State of Missouri acting by and through its officers, employees, servants and agents. Among other things, Department of Social Services is responsible for overseeing the Blind Enterprise Program (BEP), for the State of Missouri, providing training for and contracting with blind vending managers to manage concessions, vending machines, and restaurants on state property. 34 CFR 361.50, 34 CFR 395, §§ 8.051, 8.700-8.745, RSMo.
The Lion’s Business Opportunities for Missouri Blind, Inc. (L-BOMB) acts as BEP’s agent in administering the BEP program.
The Executive Committee of Blind Vendors of Missouri is for, among other things, administering in concert with RSB, a system for selecting, transferring, and promoting managers within the program. 13 CSR 40-90.010, § 6.
The BEP established guidelines for administration and implementation of RSB’s Blind
vendor program. The rules define the obligations of BEP and L-BOMB. 13 CSR 40.91.010 (BEP Rules).
BEP Rules include:
Section 3 provides that the SLA and RSB shall administer the BEP without regard to race; color; religion; sex; national origin; veteran, secondary handicap or marital status; age or political beliefs of blind persons who are manager applicants, trainees, licensees or managers.
Section 4(B) provides that the RSB shall provide to each manager consultation and advice for developing sales techniques, merchandising and general operating of the vending facility, purchasing procedures, managerial methods or procedures to promote financial success, appearance and sanitation of the vending facility and required reporting procedures.
Section 5 provides the responsibilities of the Blind Vendors of Missouri and that all active blind managers in the BEP are members of the Blind Vendors of Missouri.
Section 6 describes the responsibilities of the Executive Committee of Blind Vendors of Missouri. Section 6(B) provides that the Executive Committee participate actively with RSB in the development and administration of a system for the selection, transfer and promotion of managers. Section 6(C) provides that the Executive Committee participate actively with RSB in developing training and retraining programs for managers. Section 6(D) provides that the Executive Committee receive and transmit grievances from managers to RSB.
Section 10 provides for the selection and appointment of a vending facility manager. Applicable procedures to this case:
(A) RSB shall send written notification of each vending facility management opening to all licensees. In order for RSB to consider an applicant, a written application must be postmarked within twelve (12) calendar days of the date of written notification of the opening. In order for the application of any manager to be considered, the manager must be current in submitting Manager’s Weekly Reports, must be current in payment of administrative fees and, if applicable, must be current in all payments due the nominee. The applicant must be appropriately certified or licensed and have current National Restaurant Association’s Applied Food Service Sanitation subsection Course certification as required in (11)(D).
(B) An applicant for the position of vending facility manager must sign a “Release of Information” form, allowing a review of the applicant’s records. The executive committee shall review and evaluate each application on the basis of the information the applicant provides in the application, on information RSB provides regarding the applicant’s performance as a trainee or manager and on the results of a personal interview that the executive committee conducts with each applicant. RSB retains the right to acquire any additional information, such as verification of payment to purveyors, considered relevant to an applicant’s qualifications for appointment as a manager. The executive committee shall give the deputy director the committee’s recommendation regarding appointment of a manager. The executive committee may recommend that none of the applicants be appointed;
(C) If the deputy director agrees with the executive committee, the deputy director shall appoint or reject each applicant, as recommended by the executive committee. If the deputy director does not agree with the recommendation of the executive committee, the deputy director, after consultation with the executive committee, may appoint another qualified applicant as manager of the vending facility;
(D) The deputy director shall send written notification of the results of the selection process to each applicant.
Section 17 provides that a licensee who is dissatisfied with any action regarding the operation or administration of the BEP has access to the following grievance procedures: Section C Administrative Review, Section D -Fair Hearing, and Section E -Arbitration Panel.
Section E provides if the licensee is dissatisfied with the decision from the fair hearing, the licensee may file a written complaint with the secretary of the United States Department of Education. The licensee shall include with the written complaint all available supporting documents, including a statement of the decision of the hearing officer or director of the SLA and the basis for the decision. The secretary of the United States Department of Education shall convene an ad hoc arbitration panel to conduct a hearing and render a decision regarding the manager’s complaint.

Complainant argues that BEP Manager, Larry Branson, 1) intentionally misinformed the deciding Executive Committee of the BEP about Complainant’s bid, 2) expressed personal bias against Complainant to the Committee, and 3) slandered Complainant by telling the Committee that Complainant made illegal employee payments. Conclusions of Law and Reason
Issue 1: Whether the Rehabilitation Services for the Blind’s Business Enterprise Program (BEP) and its agent, the Lion’s Business Opportunities for Missouri Blind, Inc., (L-BOMB) was arbitrary and capricious, and therefore, violated the RSA and its implementing regulations and state rules and regulations, by not selecting Complainant as vending manager for VF #2.
Standard of Review
Neither the RSA nor the implementation of federal or state rules and regulations establish a clear standard of review to apply in this case. The Arbitration Panel applies the well-established “Arbitrary and Capricious” Standard. The United States Supreme Court held that in order to find agency decisions arbitrary in informal adjudications, court must first “consider whether the decision was based on a consideration of the relevant factors and whether there has been a clear error of judgment.” Citizens to Preserve Overton Park v. Volpe (401 U.S. 402).
Arbitrary and Capricious
Complainant submitted a letter for VF #2 in accordance with Respondent’s bid notice dated February 28, 2014. Prior to Complainant’s interview, Branson was allowed to make disparaging comments regarding Complainant’s character and illegal payments to employees, while the interview Executive Committee members remained silent as to Branson’s false
statements. The three Executive Committee members who conducted the March 22, 2014 interview with Complainant testified that the reasons for recommending Mr. White over Complainant were generally, that Mr. White gave a good interview and his WORs were consistently good for the previous two years and Complainant’s WORs were inconsistent during that two year period. FH Decision, Pages 7-8.
While the Panel acknowledges the importance of WORs, it believes factors other than the WORs impacted their recommendation for a vending manager for VF #2. At one point during the Fair Hearing, Branson makes the following statement concerning WORs as it relates to Mr. White.
MR. BRANSON: “He was delinquent with his payments. We had the WORs, as far as that goes, which doesn’t matter because the important thing is the monthly statement at the end of the month, but in 2010, he had not paid his full debt, but when he come back as temp manager, he was making payments on that debt that he had left with the corporation.” FHT, Page 130, Lines 28 – 30; FHT, Page 131, Lines 1-2.
The Respondent acted in an arbitrary and capricious manner upon Mr. Branson’s decision to share with the Committee the comment about the “illegal payments.” There was no factual basis for making that allegation about the Complainant, and thus, by considering that factor, the Executive Committee acted inappropriately.
Respondent argued that Branson had learned of the alleged “illegal” payments during a conversation he had with the Complainant. The Complainant denies having such conversation. During questioning by Arbitrator Stuart, Branson acknowledged that such conversation did not occur. Rather, the information had originated on a “rumor” he had heard. He was not able to provide the Panel any information about the source of the rumor. He also made no attempt to verify or substantiate the basis for that information before presenting it to the Committee.
Section 10(B) of the BEP Rules provides:
“The executive committee shall review and evaluate each application on the basis of the information the applicant provides in the application, on information RSB provides regarding the applicant’s performance as a trainee or manager and on the results of a personal interview that the executive committee conducts with each applicant. RSB retains the right to acquire any additional information, such as verification of payment to purveyors, considered relevant to an applicant’s qualifications for appointment as a manager.”
This section makes clear that the Executive Committee’s decision should be based on actual data such as the information provided by the applicant, information provided by the RSB based on the applicant’s prior performance, and information gathered during the interview. To be sure, Section 10 allows the committee to acquire additional information which might be “relevant to an applicant’s qualifications for appointment as a manager.” The example the section provides – “such as verification of payment to purveyors” -suggests that the type of additional information the committee is authorized to consider is information that is factually based.
It is clear from both the testimony presented at this hearing and at the Executive Committee pre-interview of Complainant, that members of the Executive Committee found that information relevant. It is also clear that in their conversation before interviewing the Complainant, members of the Committee commented on this information. Thus, it is likely that the Committee’s deliberations were influenced by information that seems to fall outside of the type of information that Section 10(B) of the BEP Rules allows the Committee to use when awarding contracts for vending facilities.
Further, it is noted from the Administrative Hearing that Complainant asserts the following took place during the timeframe of Branson’s disparaging false statements, which was during the process of Complainant’s bid and interview for VF #2:
“… You did state that you asked the Executive Committee what they were looking for in a manager for VF #2. You stated that the committee’s response was “Bill Gates,” and expressed concern that neither you nor your question was taken seriously.” AR Decision, Pages 4, 5.
“Mrs. Henderson, you stated your opinion that the executive committee already knows who they’re going to select prior to the interviews taken place. Mrs. Henderson you stated that while you were at the March 22, 2014 interview, you witnessed an Executive Committee member outwardly express favoritism towards Applicant A for VF #2. You stated that while ushering in Applicant A for their interview the Executive Committee member stated, “Oh, you got this.” You then expressed that everyone knows who is awarded the facility before arriving home. You described receiving a phone call while on your trip home indicating that you were not awarded the facility VF #2 because Applicant A possessed more experience and made higher profits. You stated that you were not at liberty to state who made the phone call to you.” AR Decision, Page 5.
Branson stated during his Fair Hearing testimony, when interviewing and choosing bidding managers, the Committee often looks mostly at the most recent 2 or 3 years for an idea of mostly current behavior. FH Decision, Page 6.
However, Branson’s false statements to the Executive Committee about Complainant did not live up to the BEP standard for evaluating Complainant’s bid for a vending facility.
Thus, the Arbitration Panel believes that in sharing that information with the Executive Committee, Branson acted inappropriately, and that in considering that information, the Committee acted in an arbitrary and capricious manner. Therefore, the Arbitration Panel finds for the Complainant.
Issue 2: Whether the Rehabilitation Services for the Blind’s Business Enterprise Program (BEP) and its agent, the Lion’s Business Opportunities for Missouri Blind, Inc., (L-BOMB) was discriminatory, and therefore, violated the RSA and its implementing regulations and state rules and regulations, by not selecting Complainant as vending manager for VF #2.
Standard of Review
There is no case law interpreting the anti-discrimination provisions in the regulations. The Panel looks to the standard set forth in McDonnell Douglas v. Green, 411 U.S. 792 (1973).
Specifically, the Court set forth that proof depend on burden shifting analysis, where the party alleging discrimination must first establish a prima facie case of discrimination. Thereafter, the defendant has the burden of coming forward with evidence of a legitimate non-discriminatory reason for its actions. If defendant party succeeds with a non-discriminatory reason, then plaintiff is tasked with producing evidence to establish further inference of discrimination.
To support a prima facie case of discrimination; McDonnell Douglass Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792 (1973), Complainant can show the following:
She is a member of a protected class. (She is African American.)
She applied for the job when the job was opened. (VF #2 was opened for bid.)
She had the minimum qualifications. (She met the criteria set forth in Branson’s letter dated February 28, 2014, to certified and licensed managers.)
She was not hired.
The job was filled by a person of another race outside the protected class.
Therefore, based upon the aforementioned evidence presented in the record, Complainant makes a compelling argument of a lost bid based on racism and discrimination.
To meet its burden in selecting another candidate for VF #2, instead of Complainant, Respondent noted that it was not unusual to have situations where the same information on the data sheet was not available for every candidate. Respondent pointed out that White’s problems with payments occurred in 2008, while the information about the Complainant’s payment history involved a more recent time period (2013-14). Respondent also noted that Complainant was inconsistent with submitting her WORs whereas White was consistent.
However, as previously discussed, Complainant argues that Branson disparaged the Complainant, by telling the Committee that Complainant had told him that she had paid several employees in cash, a practice that would be illegal, unethical and contrary to the BEP Rules.
Sometime after Complainant’s unsuccessful bid for VF #2, Complainant becomes aware of the audio recording where Branson made false statements about Complainant just prior to her interview with the Executive Committee on March 22, 2014.
Complainant sends a letter to Executive Committer dated August 26, 2014, regarding two different items: 1) Respond to Branson’s letter that another vending facility was available for bid and she was timely expressing her interest to bid and 2) Let the Executive Committee know that the information on the tape is not true. Excerpts from Complainant’s letter to Branson are:
“I implore Mr. Branson to read my entire resume in its entirety to the Executive Committee to clear up all misinformation as well as miscommunication, given to the Executive Committee on March 22. After receiving the bid interview tape, I learned that Mr. Branson misinformed the board that I paid employees with cash. I have documented proof that I paid every employee from the Eagle’s Nest Checking account. Mr. Branson also misinformed the board that I avoided paying taxing entities. This too was fabricated and I have proof that states otherwise. President James Pinkney and Board member, James Hinton further insinuated I was paying cash to avoid paying child support. This misinformation was given prior to me entering the room. None of this information was made known to me during the interview, and none of this is true.”
“The BEP is a program designed for visually impaired or blind persons to become self-sufficient in society. It is based on moral character, dignity, pride and truth. All of which were exempt during my interview.” (Submitted by Respondent – Group Documents provided as Item # 4).
Complainant was treated differently in the interviewing process from similar situated parties in that Branson’s disparate and disparaging statements during the interview bid process, and thereby, resulted in Complainant losing the VF #2 facility.
Therefore, Respondent failed to meet its burden of showing non-discriminatory grounds for awarding VF #2 to White.
Branson violated Section 3 of the BEP, which provides that the SLA and RSB shall administer the BEP without regard to race; color; religion; sex; national origin; veteran, secondary handicap or marital status; age or political beliefs of blind persons who are manager applicants, trainees, licensees or managers.
Complainant was an experienced manager who qualified for the VF #2 facility. Complainant earned 100% on her final exam, never abandoned a program facility with or without notice, utilized her own personal earnings to re-establish the Eagle Nest Café after it was left in financial turmoil by White and endured unnecessary disparate treatment during the interview process.
Further, at Complainant’s interview for VF #2, each Executive Committee member stated the importance of the WORs in their recommendation of White over Complainant. However, the issue of the late WORs was not weighed in good faith against the financial turmoil of the Eagle’s Nest Café facility and Branson’s false statements to the Committee. Moreover, Branson and others refused to help when Complainant repeatedly requested assistance that resulted from loss contracts.
Therefore, the Arbitration Panel finds, based on the evidence and record, for the Complainant.
Attorney Fees
In Wisconsin v. U.S. Department of Education, 667 F. Supp. 2d 1007 (W.D. Wis. 2009), the Court stated the following:
“The Equal Access to Justice Act applies only to certain adjudicative proceedings conducted by federal agencies in which ‘the position of the United States is represented by counsel’ and is adverse to the party seeking attorney fees. 5 U.S.C. § 504(b)(1)(C). The Act does not apply in this case because the underlying arbitration was not such an adverse proceeding. Although a state agency was in a position adverse …, no federal party was involved in the dispute. The Department of Education merely facilitated the arbitration and was not adverse … the American Rule governs and all parties must pay their own fees.”
AWARD
The Respondent is directed to award the Complainant the next comparable Level 4 facility with a history of generating profitable income that becomes available and mutually agreed to by Complainant and Respondent.
The Panel is not authorized to award Attorney fees.
It is expected that the award to Complainant will be fulfilled within a reasonable timeframe. The Arbitration Panel retains jurisdiction to consider any issue that might arise in the implementation of the award.

Use of FRRP Funds
Explain briefly how the SLA will distribute the FRRP funds to blind vendors to offset losses incurred during the 2020 calendar year (e.g., proportional to losses incurred, based on losses due to vendor’s spoiled or expired stock, etc.).
SLA: The SLA will utilize the monthly business reports submitted by each vending facility manager for the 12 months in calendar year 2019 to make a comparison of losses in the 12 months period in calendar year 2020. Using the categories identified on the business report (refer to attached business report template) a total of losses experienced by vending facility managers can be determined on a case specific basis. In addition to the business reports, the SLA will also factor in documented losses of perishable inventory in the calculation of loss for vending facility managers. No vending facility manager will receive more FRRP funds than what can be documented as their actual losses in 2020. The final determination of actual losses can only be done after CT-B is informed by RSA if the categories that the Statewide Committee of Blind Vendors has proposed for disallowance in the loss calculations have been approved, modified, or rejected. Once RSA affirms what categories from section B4 below are acceptable, a final calculation of all losses for all of the vending facility managers will be completed.  Should it be found that the FRRP funds are not sufficient to cover all losses at 100 percent, a straight pro-rata percentage will be applied equally so that every vending facility manager would receive the same percentage of compensation for their losses.

How will the SLA ensure blind vendor losses were not previously compensated (e.g., attestation, financial reports, inventory reports, documentation of losses due to spoilage, etc.)?
SLA:  Monthly business reports are already attested to by each vending facility manager at the time of submission. SLA staff will obtain copies of invoices for inventory that is to be counted as perished to attest to the accuracy of the losses claimed. In addition, an Attestation Form will be utilized that list specific examples of funding sources that are counted as other compensation and the vending facility manager will be required to attest to each item listed as having received or not received compensation in that category. The Statewide Committee of Blind Vendors at their meeting on March 30, 2021 made the following decisions as to what should and what should not be considered as other forms of compensation that would offset the losses claimed for reimbursement utilizing FRRP funds:
The first $10,000 in Unemployment Insurance payments will be exempted from consideration of other compensation because that amount is not considered as taxable income. Unemployment Insurance payments beyond $10,000 in calendar year 2020 will be counted as other compensation and will reduce the amount of funding through the FRRP funds available to the vending facility manager by an equivalent amount. The IRS form 1099 will be obtained from each vending facility manager who indicates receipt of Unemployment Insurance payments in calendar year 2020 to confirm the amount that will be considered as other compensation.
Any funds received from the Vocational Rehabilitation Program to reimburse a vending facility manager for inventory that perished in calendar year 2020 will be deducted from the amount of perished inventory that is claimed for compensation with FRRP funds.
Paycheck Protection Program funds (PPP) will be excluded from consideration as other compensation since these funds are considered as non-taxable income; may need to be paid back as a loan at a future date depending on circumstances that cannot be known at this time; and these funds can be used to pay for a variety of things to fund the business upon reopening.
Vacation payments made to vending facility managers in lieu of SLA field representative coverage for one week of managerial coverage at vending facilities to allow the vending facility manager to have a one-week vacation in 2020 shall not be considered as other compensation. The vending facilities were closed and therefore no option existed to provide for a one-week vacation coverage in 2020. This payment for the equivalent value of one week of field representative coverage was deemed by the Statewide Committee of Blind Vendor to be an alternative form of compensation for a benefit that was provided for annually per state regulations.
Fair Minimum Return payments will not be counted as other compensation because this provision has existed long before COVID-19 and has been available to vending facility managers on an annual basis. The Statewide Committee of Blind Vendors did not feel that it was reasonable or fair to link these payments to any calculations used for determining losses for 2020.
Grants awards from the Connecticut Department of Economic and Community Development shall not be included as other compensation since these funds were not utilized in calendar year 2020. These funds will be used for current and future expenses in 2021 going forward.

RE: FFY 2023 Annual Review Finding Regarding Noncompliance with Pre-Employment

Dear Kevin Wright: 
 
This letter presents the Rehabilitation Services Administration’s (RSA) annual review and 
finding regarding the State’s failure to satisfy requirements governing the provision of pre-
employment transition services in Federal fiscal year (FFY) 2023, as determined by RSA in 
accordance with Section 107(a) of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (Rehabilitation Act), as 
amended by the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA).
 Pursuant to Section 
107(a)(1) of the Rehabilitation Act, RSA conducts an annual review of the Tennessee 
Department of Human Services (DHS), Division of Rehabilitation Services (DRS) to 
determine whether the State is substantially complying with the requirements of its Unified 
or Combined State Plan, including requirements governing the provision of pre-employment 
transition services to students with disabilities. The FFY 2023 final financial report submitted 
by DHS for DRS demonstrates the State failed to satisfy the requirements of Sections 
110(d)(1) and 113 of the Rehabilitation Act by not reserving and expending at least 
15 percent of its vocational rehabilitation (VR) grant amount on the provision of pre-
employment transition services for students with disabilities. For this reason, RSA is 
requiring DHS to submit corrective action plan (CAP) pursuant to Section 107(b)(2) of the 
Rehabilitation Act, as described further below. 
 
Annual Review of DHS’s FFY 2023 Fiscal Data 
 
Short Summary:   
 
RSA has determined, after conducting an annual review of DHS’ final FFY 2023 RSA-17 for 
DRS, pursuant to Section 107(a)(2) of the Rehabilitation Act, that the agency failed to satisfy 
the requirements of Sections 110(d)(1) and 113(a) of the Rehabilitation Act (i.e., the 
expenditure requirements) by not expending at least 15 percent of the State’s matched FFY 
2023 VR grant funds on the provision of pre-employment transition services for students

Annual Review Requirements:  
  
Section 107(a)(1) of the Rehabilitation Act requires RSA to conduct annual reviews and 
periodic onsite monitoring of the VR agencies to determine whether they are in substantial 
compliance with their approved State plan, including the VR services portion of that Plan. 
In conducting the annual reviews, RSA must consider, at a minimum, budget, and financial 
management data (Section 107(a)(2)(I) of the Rehabilitation Act). Pursuant to Section 
107(a)(4)(B) of the Rehabilitation Act, RSA must examine the provision of services, 
including pre-employment transition services.  
 
Analysis of FFY 2023 Financial Data:   
 
According to DHS’ final FFY 2023 RSA-17 submitted for DRS, the net Federal VR 
allotment for the State was $62,120,561, all of which DHS/DRS matched. Therefore, 
DHS/DRS was required to expend at least $9,318,084 (rounded) for the provision of pre-
employment transition services. The DHS reported on DRS’ final FFY 2023 RSA-17 
spending a total of $8,910,122 (14.3 percent of the State’s VR allotment) in Federal VR 
funds for pre-employment transition services and spent $407,964 (rounded) in funds required 
to be reserved for pre-employment transition services on other VR services. RSA was unable 
to determine if these VR services were necessary for the student to participate in pre-
employment transition services and included on an individualized plan for employment (IPE) 
in support of pre-employment transition services. Only those individualized VR services 
necessary for a VR-eligible student to participate in pre-employment transition services may 
be provided under an IPE and charged to the funds reserved for the provision of pre-
employment transition services. 
 
RSA’s Annual Review Finding:   
 
As demonstrated by their final RSA-17 for FFY 2023, DHS/DRS reserved and expended a 
total of 14.3 percent of their matched Federal VR funds, a decrease from the 17.3 percent of 
the agency’s expenditures for pre-employment transition services in FFY 2022.  
 
Noncompliance with Approved State Plan:   
 
Section 101(a)(25) of the Rehabilitation Act requires DHS and DRS to assure in their State 
Plan, with respect to students with disabilities, that the State has developed and implemented 
strategies to: 
 
• Address the needs identified in the assessments described in Section 101(a)(15) of the 
Rehabilitation Act; 
• Achieve the goals and priorities identified by the State in accordance with Section 
101(a)(15) of the Rehabilitation Act to improve and expand pre-employment 
transition services for students with disabilities on a statewide basis; and

• Provide pre-employment transition services. 
 
Pre-employment transition services, as defined at Section 7(30) of the Rehabilitation Act, 
“means services provided in accordance with section 113.” Section 113(a) of the 
Rehabilitation Act requires the State to ensure that DHS and DRS will use the funds reserved 
under Section 110(d)(1) of the Rehabilitation Act to provide, or arrange for the provision of, 
pre-employment transition services to students with disabilities. Section 110(d)(1) of the 
Rehabilitation Act establishes the 15 percent minimum reservation requirement for the State 
from its VR allotment. 
 
Together, Sections 7(30), 101(a)(25), 110(d)(1), and 113 of the Rehabilitation Act create the 
statutory basis for the State Plan provisions governing pre-employment transition services.

Corrective Action Plan Required:  
 
In accordance with Section 107(b)(2) of the Rehabilitation Act, the Commissioner requires 
that DHS and DRS take the following actions to help ensure the State satisfies the pre-
employment transition services reservation requirements again.  
 
RSA requires DHS and DRS to develop and implement a CAP that: 
 
• Contains a solid, workable plan for action; 
• Contains a timeline for implementation that would be completed within a reasonable 
specific time; 
• Can be supported by documentation (i.e., fiscal reports) to show the agencies have 
implemented the actions;  
• Demonstrates verifiable improvement toward meeting requirement on a quarterly 
basis;  
• Provides a methodology that allows DHS and DRS to review internally whether the 
corrective actions have resolved the root cause of the noncompliance with the 
reservation requirements and to review whether the actions are consistent with the 
progress needed to meet the key dates in the timeline; and 
• Ensures implementation of internal controls necessary for the agencies to maintain 
compliance with the requirements on an ongoing basis. 
 
RSA requires DHS and DRS to submit the above-described CAP to their RSA State liaison 
within 60 calendar days of receipt of this letter. 
 
Conclusion: 
 
RSA has found, after conducting an annual review of DHS’ final FFY 2023 RSA-17 for DRS 
pursuant to Section 107 of the Rehabilitation Act, that the State did not satisfy the pre-
employment transition services reserve and expend requirements that year. Specifically, the

This letter presents the Rehabilitation Services Administration’s (RSA) findings regarding 
the New Hampshire Bureau of Vocational Rehabilitation’s (BVR) failure to substantially 
comply with the requirements to determine eligibility and develop individualized plans for 
employment (IPEs) on a timely basis in Program Year (PY 2022
1 under the State Vocational 
Rehabilitation (VR) program in accordance with sections 102(a)(6) and 102(b)(3)(F) of the 
Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (Rehabilitation Act), respectively. RSA made its determination of 
BVR’s substantial non-compliance during its annual review, conducted in accordance with 
section 107(a) of the Rehabilitation Act, of Case Service Report (RSA-911)2 data submitted 
by BVR during PY 2022. This letter constitutes RSA’s report to BVR of the annual review of 
PY 2022 data in accordance with section 107(a)(5) of the Rehabilitation Act.

of the VR program to ensure each State is substantially complying with the requirements of 
its Unified or Combined State Plan. In conducting the annual reviews, RSA must consider, 
among other things, reports submitted by the VR agencies (section 107(a)(2)(H) of the 
Rehabilitation Act). In conducting the annual reviews, RSA must examine, among other 
areas, the eligibility process and the provision of VR services (section 107(a)(4)(A) and (B) 
of the Rehabilitation Act). As recipients of Federal funds, VR agencies must collect and

Section 102(a)(6) of the Rehabilitation Act requires BVR to make eligibility determinations, 
“within a reasonable period of time, not to exceed 60 days, after the individual has submitted 
an application” for VR services, unless: (1) both BVR and the applicant agree to a specific 
extension of time; or (2) BVR is providing a trial work experience for the applicant. 
According to BVR’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data, the VR agency made a total of 1,079 
determinations of eligibility or ineligibility. Of these, BVR made the determinations for 832 
applicants within the required 60-day period (or 77.1 percent of the total applicants) and 
made the determinations for 247 applicants beyond 60 days of application (or 22.9 percent of 
the total applicants).
3 Therefore, RSA’s annual review of BVR’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data 
demonstrate the VR agency failed to substantially comply with the requirement of section 
102(a)(6) of the Rehabilitation Act because 22.9 percent of its applicants that year did not 
receive a timely eligibility or ineligibility determination within the required 60-day period. 
 
Section 102(b)(3)(F) of the Rehabilitation Act requires BVR to develop the IPE for each 
individual determined eligible “as soon as possible, but not later than a deadline of 90 days 
after the date of the determination of eligibility,” unless BVR and the individual agree to an 
extension of a specific period of time. According to BVR’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data, BVR 
developed a total of 608 IPEs for eligible individuals with disabilities without extensions. Of 
these, BVR developed 437 IPEs within 90 days of the eligibility determination (or 71.9 
percent) and developed 171 IPEs beyond 90 days of the eligibility determination (or 18.1 
percent).
4  Therefore, RSA’s annual review of BVR’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data demonstrate 
the VR agency failed to substantially comply with the requirement of section 102(b)(3)(F) of 
the Rehabilitation Act because BVR did not develop IPEs for 19.1 percent of its eligible 
individuals that year within the required 90-day period.

Opportunity Act in order to receive VR funding. The State Plan must contain a VR services 
portion that satisfies the requirements of the VR program (34 C.F.R. § 361.10(a)(2)), 
including those pertaining to eligibility determinations and IPE development. The VR

services portion of the State Plan must contain an assurance that IPEs, meeting the 
requirements of section 102(b), will be developed and implemented in a timely manner for 
eligible individuals (section 101(a)(9) of the Rehabilitation Act). In addition to this specific 
assurance, among others, each VR agency submitting the VR services portion of the State 
Plan signs an assurance that the VR agency will satisfy all Federal requirements governing 
the VR program, which would include those pertaining to timelines for eligibility 
determinations and IPE development. Moreover, as a recipient of Federal funds, each VR 
agency, must develop internal controls to ensure the VR agency complies with all Federal 
requirements and implements corrective actions when it identifies instances of non-
compliance (2 C.F.R. § 200.303(a) and (d)). By signing the VR services portions of their 
State Plan and accepting VR grant funds, BVR certified that it would administer and operate 
the VR program in accordance with the State Plan, Rehabilitation Act, and applicable Federal 
regulations governing the VR program. As demonstrated by BVR’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data 
and described above, the VR agency fell short of satisfying Federal requirements governing 
timelines for eligibility determinations and IPE developments that year.  
 
In accordance with Section 107(b)(2) of the Rehabilitation Act, the Commissioner requires 
BVR to develop and implement a corrective action plan (CAP) that— 
• Addresses the root cause of the VR agency’s noncompliance with these 
requirements; 
• Contains a solid, workable plan for action; 
• Contains a timeline for implementation that would be completed by end of PY 2024 
(i.e., June 30, 2025); 
• Can be supported by RSA-911 quarterly reports to show the VR agency has 
maintained compliance for at least two consecutive quarters;  
• Provides a methodology that allows the VR agency to review internally whether the 
corrective actions have resolved the root cause of the noncompliance with the 
requirements and to review whether the actions are consistent with the progress 
needed to ensure compliance; and 
• Ensures implementation of internal controls necessary for the VR agency to maintain 
compliance with the requirements on an ongoing basis. 
 
BVR must submit the above-described CAP to their RSA State Liaison within 60 days of the 
date of issuance of this letter.

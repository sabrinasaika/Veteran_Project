Direct Negotiation for Contracts Clarification
While we appreciate the XXX seeking to consult with the Secretary over the award of the XXX contract, under 34 C.F.R. §395.33(d) of the implementing regulations for the Randolph-Sheppard Act, the Federal agency does not have to consult with the Secretary pursuant to 34 C.F.R. §§395.33(a) and (b), when a contract for the operation of a cafeteria results from the direct negotiation of the Federal agency with the SLA. Section 395.33(d) provides:
- Notwithstanding the requirements of paragraphs (a) and (b) of this section, Federal property managing departments, agencies and instrumentalities may afford priority in the operation of cafeterias by blind vendors on Federal property through direct negotiations with State licensing agencies whenever such department, agency, or instrumentality determines on an individual basis, that such operations can be provided at a reasonable cost, with food of a high quality comparable to that currently provided employees: Provided, however, That the provisions of paragraphs (a) and (b) of this section shall apply in the event that the negotiations authorized by this paragraph do not result in a contract.
In such a case, the Federal agency has determined through direct negotiation with the SLA that it will afford the priority for the operation of the cafeteria to the SLA, finding that a blind vendor can operate the cafeteria at a reasonable cost, with food of a high quality comparable to that currently provided to employees. Since the XXX engaged in direct negotiations with the SLA resulting in a contract with the SLA, no consultation is necessary, and the XXX may move forward with awarding the contract to the SLA.
We are, however, grateful that the XXX provided notice of its contract with the SLA, and we are pleased that such a result could be achieved by negotiating directly with the SLA.

FFY 2024 Report of Vending Facility Program (RSA-15)
State licensing agencies (SLAs) under the Randolph-Sheppard Act (Act) are required annually to complete the Report of Vending Facility Program (RSA-15) that reports activities for the prior Federal fiscal year (FFY). The intent of the RSA-15 is to collect data on all vending facilities managed and operated by the SLA under the Act.
For FFY 2024, the reporting period is October 1, 2023 through September 30, 2024, and the deadline to submit this report is December 29, 2024.
Reporting Instructions and the RSA-15 report are available.
The report itself has no changes, but some of the instructions have changed due to the OMB equipment exception (2022-21751 Federal Register Notice Published). Note the instructions have been updated as of 12/26/2023 at Section IV, line 3b: Acquisition of Facilities:
Only allowable sources of funding, including Federal VR funds and non-Federal funds used for match and MOE purposes under the VR program (e.g., State appropriated funds), may be used to finance the cost of acquiring facilities. However, set-aside funds may not be used for acquiring new facilities, as these are not allowable uses of such funds.
You may also contact the Randolph-Sheppard State Liaisons with questions or concerns regarding the RSA-15 report.

AbilityOne/RSA Memorandum of Understanding
RSA and the Committee for Purchase From People Who Are Blind or Severely Disabled (operating as the U.S. AbilityOne Commission) agree to work together to increase communication and understanding between the agencies regarding which opportunities are appropriate for persons who are blind to operate vending facilities under the Randolph-Sheppard Act and which are appropriate for placement on the Procurement List under the Javits-Wagner-O’Day Act. Furthermore, AbilityOne and RSA agree to work together to expand entrepreneurial and other employment-related opportunities for individuals who are blind or who have significant disabilities.
Employment outcomes and entrepreneurial opportunities for individuals who are blind or who have significant disabilities can be improved through a closer working relationship between AbilityOne and RSA. This Memorandum of Understanding (MOU), executed on January 19, 2021, establishes the framework for AbilityOne and RSA to work more closely together and to encourage their respective stakeholders to also work cooperatively together to promote improved services and entrepreneurial and employment opportunities to their mutual participants. The definitions, published on June 1, 2021 as an appendix, are pertinent to this agreement that will facilitate both clearer communication and adherence to the agreement.

Appeal of Arbitration Decision Letter
On November 23, 2020, RSA responded to Department of the Army’s appeal of the arbitration panel decision in Oklahoma Department of Rehabilitation Services v. Department of the Army, Fort Sill (Case No. R-S/18-9) via this letter. Under the Randolph-Sheppard Act, there is no right to appeal an adverse arbitration decision to the Secretary of Education. Judicial review in Federal district court is available to aggrieved persons as defined in the Administrative Procedure Act.

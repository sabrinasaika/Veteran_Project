Registered apprenticeships are an increasingly popular way for people to train for and retain high-quality, in-demand jobs.
Employers participating in apprenticeship programs have a custom-trained talent pool to hire from. Career-seekers get paid while they learn, get hands-on training, and come away with a nationally recognized credential.
93% of apprentices who complete a Registered Apprenticeship retain employment, with an average annual salary of $77,000.
Apprenticeships: A Different Way to Train
Unlike other educational experiences or training, apprenticeships are:
Industry driven to meet labor market demands
Paid jobs so apprentices can learn and earn at the same time
Hybrid training, combining classroom and hands-on experience
Reflective of the diverse communities where they are located
Safe, since apprenticeships work together with their supervisors
A great way to earn a nationally recognized credentials
Approved and validated by the U.S. Department of Labor or a State Apprenticeship Agency
Employer Benefits
Apprenticeship Programs Success
Employers reflecting a wide range of industries use apprenticeship programs to meet their business needs. Benefits include:
Building a skilled workforce: Recruit and develop a varied and highly-skilled workforce.
Improving productivity: Improve profitability and positive impact to your bottom line.
Reduced turnover: Minimize cost with reduced turnover and liability.
Customized training: Create flexible training options that ensure workers develop the right skills.
Retain workers: 93% of apprentices continue employment after completing an apprenticeship.

Registered Apprenticeship Programs are designed to reflect the communities in which they operate through strong hiring and recruitment practices to ensure access for all.
By including protections for apprenticeship candidates with disabilities, Registered Apprenticeship Programs allow employers access to a major untapped talent pool, whose worldview can contribute insight and depth to industries. The revisions also open up career paths previously unavailable to job-seekers with disabilities.

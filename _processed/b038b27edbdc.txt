Memorandum of Understanding Webinar
On August 26, 2021, the Rehabilitation Services Administration and the U.S. AbilityOne Commission held a webinar to provide an overview and details related to the Memorandum of Understanding. This document provides answers to questions asked during and after the webinar.
Background
The U.S. Department of Education’s Rehabilitation Services Administration (RSA) and the Committee for Purchase From People Who Are Blind or Severely Disabled (operating as the U.S. AbilityOne Commission) (hereinafter referred to as “AbilityOne” or “the Commission”) executed a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) on January 19, 2021, agreeing to work together to increase communication and understanding between the agencies regarding which opportunities are appropriate for individuals who are blind to operate vending facilities under the Randolph-Sheppard Act (R-S Act) (20 U.S.C. § 107 et seq.) and which are appropriate for placement on the Procurement List under the Javits-Wagner-O’Day Act (JWOD) Act (41 U.S.C. §§ 8501-8506). Furthermore, AbilityOne and RSA agreed to work together to expand entrepreneurial and other employment-related opportunities for individuals who are blind or who have significant disabilities through providing training and other resources to the constituencies of each agency: State licensing agencies (SLAs), Federal agencies, blind vendors, and nonprofit agencies (NPAs).
Q1. How will RSA assist SLAs throughout the process outlined in the MOU?
With this MOU, RSA and the Commission are committed to closer collaboration and better communication. In particular, the MOU establishes a new protocol regarding when an opportunity is appropriate under the R-S Act and when it is appropriate for placement on the Procurement List under the JWOD Act.
RSA will provide broad support to SLAs to help them understand the protocol and the timeframes for moving through the process. The protocol will also serve as a technical assistance resource to all the Federal agencies. RSA will work with the SLAs to make sure that SLA determinations about whether to pursue opportunities are communicated with the Commission, as outlined in the MOU.
RSA and the Commission have established a workgroup to carry out the terms of the MOU and to collaborate on a variety of activities. If issues arise that need to be addressed in the protocol, RSA can raise them in this workgroup. The MOU provides a mechanism for modifications if necessary.
From the MOU:
If RSA believes that an opportunity referred by the Commission merits additional review by the appropriate SLA, it will forward its determination and all documentation to the SLA. Once RSA makes the determination to forward the opportunity for additional review to the appropriate SLA, RSA will notify the Commission of that determination. The Commission will inform the Federal agency that RSA has referred the opportunity to the appropriate SLA to decide whether to exercise the priority to provide a licensed blind vendor to operate a vending facility to which the R-S Act applies, and to expect communication from the SLA.
The notification RSA sends to the SLA will include everything the Commission sent to RSA, which under the MOU should include the service location, the service requirement, the responsible Federal Contracting Activity (including the contact information for the Federal agency contracting officer), and all documentation originally provided by the Federal agency. RSA will also include the permitting information and any contract documents, including the performance work statement, if obtained from the Commission or the Federal agency.
Once RSA has referred the opportunity to the appropriate SLA for additional review, the negotiation of the opportunity is between the SLA and the Federal agency until and unless the SLA notifies the Federal agency that it has decided not to pursue the opportunity as a vending facility to which the R-S Act applies.
RSA will maintain contact with the SLA during the determination process to monitor progress and RSA will request notification and documentation when the SLA makes its determination whether or not it intends to pursue the opportunity. If an SLA determines it will not exercise its priority at a given time, the Commission may add the contract to the Procurement List.  However, the opportunity will be assessed as a Randolph-Sheppard opportunity under the MOU protocol when the contract expires, which is usually five years. RSA will continue to communicate with the SLAs about the opportunities each time the opportunities arise.
RSA plans to provide broad support to SLAs to help them understand the process and the timeframes for moving through the process. It will also serve as a technical assistance resource to the Federal agencies. RSA will work with the SLAs to make sure that the SLA determination about whether to pursue an opportunity is communicated with the Commission.
Q2. What other activities are covered by the MOU?
In addition to the protocol discussed in the response to question one (Q1), the MOU provides for additional training to be provided by RSA and the Commission to each other and jointly to stakeholders. This training will help each agency to expand its knowledge base about the respective programs and the opportunities that exist for collaboration.
For example, products and services produced and provided by National Industries for the Blind (NIB) and SourceAmerica may be able to be procured by Randolph-Sheppard blind vendors for use in vending facilities. Further, some individuals employed by NPAs participating in NIB may wish to become blind vendors under the Randolph-Sheppard Vending Facility Program (RSVFP). Enhanced training on such programs may help to identify new and existing opportunities to benefit stakeholders.
Finally, since the Commission works with a broad and varied group of Federal agencies, it provides an opportunity for the RSVFP to expand its network. As the Commission conducts training on its AbilityOne program, discussion of the different R-S Act requirements and the MOU will educate Federal contracting officers about the RSVFP and how it may interact with the AbilityOne program.
From the MOU:
RSA will provide training to the Commission staff and Commissioners on the R-S Act and the associated regulations and policies, and the Commission staff will provide training to RSA and other Departmental staff, as appropriate, on the JWOD Act, and the associated regulations and policies. Such training may also include the operations of each program and potential opportunities each program may be able to develop to encourage more business and employment opportunities for stakeholders.
RSA and the Commission may participate in appropriate national meetings, trainings, and conferences to become familiar with the requirements, best practices, and issues of each program and to discuss collaboration with stakeholders.
RSA and the Commission agreed to consider other strategies for increased communication and collaboration, which may include, but are not limited to the following:
Assign staff to participate in workgroups on topics or issues of mutual interest;
Explore information exchange and training opportunities for stakeholders, such as Federal agencies, SLAs, central nonprofit agencies, nonprofit agencies, and blind vendors regarding the operations of each program and potential business opportunities each may be able to develop with the other; and
Explore possibilities for internships and opportunities for employees working with RSA and the Commission and stakeholders of the R-S Act and JWOD Act programs to work with the other agency.
Q3. If a vending facility, such as a commercial laundry facility, exists as part of a State’s RSVFP, would the vending facility be usable by AbilityOne NPAs?

One of the objectives of the MOU is to identify ways in which the RSVFP and the AbilityOne program can work together. The Commission will explore ways to facilitate communication regarding future business opportunities and the exchange of capabilities aimed at expanding competitive integrated employment opportunities for individuals with disabilities. Inquiries about AbilityOne partnering opportunities may be sent to info@abilityone.gov.
Q4. May vendors under the RSVFP purchase products from AbilityOne NPAs, and how may those purchases be made?
Yes. Licensed vendors may purchase products, such as paper cups, paper plates, mess trays, napkins, paper towels, and plastic flatware, from AbilityOne NPAs.

Q5. Can anyone access the current Procurement List to identify opportunities that may not be available to SLAs?
Yes. A searchable list of Procurement List services is posted on the AbilityOne.gov website at: https://pl.abilityone.gov/procurement_search/procurement_service_search.aspx. More generally, information about the AbilityOne program can be found at www.abilityone.gov.
Q6. Will services currently on the Procurement List be offered to RSA when they come up for renewal?
No. Services currently on the AbilityOne Program Procurement List will not be offered back to RSA for consideration as an opportunity under the RSVFP. However, for prospective or new opportunities covered by the MOU that are offered to but declined by SLAs and added to the Procurement List, the MOU provides that those opportunities will be assessed by RSA as R-S Act opportunities under the MOU protocol when the contract expires.
Q7. When was the last time a food service contract was removed from the Procurement List, other than as a result of a lawsuit?
Between 2019 and 2020, the Commission deleted 12 inactive requirements for food service, food service attendants, or dining facility attendant services from the Procurement List. The deletions were part of a periodic process to update the Procurement List and remove services for which there is no longer a Government requirement or that are no longer economically feasible for a qualified NPA to provide.
Q8. Does the AbilityOne Program hire individuals with disabilities as Federal employees, or are they hired by the NPAs?
When individuals who are blind or who have significant disabilities are hired to work on AbilityOne contracts, they work as employees of qualified NPAs. Some individuals do move from AbilityOne contract employment to employment with the Federal Government.
Q9. Do AbilityOne NPAs use 14(c) certificates, under the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938, on food service contracts?
The Commission does not currently collect this data. However, on November 12, 2021, the Commission published a proposed rule in the Federal Register (86 FR 56679) that would require the prohibition of the payment of subminimum wages under 14(c) certificates as a qualification for participation as an NPA under the AbilityOne Program: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2021-11-12/pdf/2021-24714.pdf.
Additionally, as all AbilityOne food-related service contracts are subject to the McNamara-O’Hara Service Contract Act, beginning in January 2022, Executive Order 14026 (86 FR 22835, April 30, 2021) sets the minimum wage on those contracts at an hourly wage of at least $15.00 as those contracts are renewed or extended.
Q10. May vendors under the RSVFP hire AbilityOne employees?
Yes. Licensed vendors may hire individuals who are blind or who have significant disabilities who currently work or previously worked on AbilityOne contracts. In addition, SLAs that are the prime contractors on cafeteria contracts may subcontract with NPAs who employ individuals who are blind or who have significant disabilities. The Commission supports the development of local relationships between licensed vendors and AbilityOne NPAs so that employment candidates may be identified. Under the MOU framework, the Commission and RSA will explore further ways to facilitate communication and the exchange of capabilities.

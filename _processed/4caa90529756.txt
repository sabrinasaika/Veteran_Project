Introduction 
The Rehabilitation Services Administration (RSA), a component of the Office of Special 
Education and Rehabilitative Services (OSERS), is providing this report to the President 
and Congress pursuant to Section 107a(6)(A) of the Randolph-Sheppard Act.1 The 
report describes the purpose, funding, and performance of the vending facility program 
for individuals who are blind established under the Randolph-Sheppard Act for fiscal 
years 2021 through 2022 (October 1, 2020, through September 30, 2022). In addition, 
the report highlights the technical assistance RSA provided during this period to support 
and improve the vending facility program in the States. 
The vending facility program provides individuals who are blind with remunerative 
employment and self-support by providing a priority to operate vending facilities on 
Federal and other property. The program recruits qualified individuals who are blind, trains 
them on managing and operating small business enterprises, and then licenses qualified 
blind vendors to operate the facilities. 
At the outset, the program placed sundry stands in the lobbies of Federal office 
buildings and post offices, selling items such as newspapers, magazines, candies, and 
tobacco products. Through the years, the program has grown and broadened from 
Federal locations to include State, county, municipal, and private installations, as well as 
interstate highway rest areas. In addition, vending facility operations expanded to 
include military mess halls, cafeterias, snack bars, cart services, miscellaneous shops, 
and vending machines. 
State vocational rehabilitation (VR) agencies that provide services to individuals who 
are blind may also serve as State licensing agencies (SLA) under the Randolph-
Sheppard Act. Currently, 51 SLAs are responsible for the management and operation of 
the vending facility programs in 49 States, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico.  
The only State not participating in the program is Wyoming. 
Funding 
The vending facility program, including the licensing and operation of vending facilities by 
blind vendors, is supported by a combination of Federal VR program funds, State 
appropriations, unassigned Federal vending machine income, and set-aside funds, which 
accrue to the SLA from assessments against the net proceeds of vendors.

Section 103(b)(1) of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (Rehabilitation Act) authorizes VR 
agencies to provide services for the benefit of groups of individuals with disabilities. 
These services may include management and supervision services, the acquisition of 
vending facilities or other equipment, initial stocks and supplies, and initial operating 
expenses to improve businesses operated by individuals with significant disabilities (see 
also 34 C.F.R. § 361.49(a)(5)). Under this authority, VR agencies support the 
management and operation in the States of the vending facility program for individuals 
who are blind established pursuant to the Randolph-Sheppard Act.  
SLAs may use Federal VR program funds to purchase, maintain, and replace 
equipment in vending facilities, provide management services, such as accounting, 
inspection, and quality control, and provide crucial in-service training and related 
services on a systematic basis to support and improve the vending facilities operated by

The blind vendors support their BEPs under the Randolph-Sheppard Act through 
payment of assessments by some States against the net proceeds of each vending 
facility to create set-aside funds. Like Federal VR program funds, set-aside funds may 
be used to purchase, maintain, and replace equipment in vending facilities and provide 
management services. In addition, set-aside funds may be used to provide for a fair 
minimum return to blind vendors and establish retirement, pension, health care, and 
paid vacation/sick leave for individuals participating in the program. Table 2 shows the 
amounts and percentages of set-aside funds in FYs 2020 through 2022 used by SLAs 
to support the BEPs under the Randolph-Sheppard Act.  
The percentage of total Randolph-Sheppard Act BEP expenditures paid for by set-aside 
funds was 14.1 percent in FY 2021 and 12 percent in FY 2022. Expenditures from set-
aside funds decreased by approximately $6.3 million between FY 2020 and FY 2022. As 
a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, many Randolph-Sheppard vending facilities 
remained closed through FY 2021, leading to lower set-aside assessments paid by blind 
vendors. However, as facilities reopened in FY 2022, vendor earnings increased and, 
although the amount of set-aside funds was reduced in FY 2022, the U.S. Department 
of Education (ED) expects this amount to increase in future fiscal years. It is important 
to note that these set-aside funds, if spent on expenditures that are also allowable 
under Section 103(b)(1) of the Rehabilitation Act for purposes of the VR program, may 
be used by States to meet their Federal match and maintenance of effort requirements 
for the VR program.

Performance 
Table 3 compares income and earnings data from FYs 2020 through 2022 and the 
number of vendors and vending facilities operating during that time period, and shows: 
• The total gross income for the program was $564.9 million in FY 2020, compared 
to $544.5 million in FY 2021 and $634.7 million in FY 2022;

• The total earnings of all vendors were $95 million in FY 2020 and $120.9 million 
in FY 2021. (Note that this amount is artificially inflated because of the Federal 
Relief and Restoration Payment (FRRP) appropriation described later in this 
report.). While the number of vending locations decreased during FY 2022, 
vendor earnings increased to $130.5 million as remaining vending facilities 
began to reopen; however, this increase may be the result of SLAs continuing to 
obligate the FRRP funds to the vendors in FY 2022; 
• The national average annual net earnings of vendors were $55,958.12 in 
FY 2020, $75,818.52 in FY 2021, and $84,213.40 in FY 2022; 
• The number of vendors at the end of FY 2020 was 1,697, compared to 1,595 in 
FY 2021, and 1,550 in FY 2022; and 
• The total number of vending facilities at the end of FY 2020 was 2,183, 
compared to 2,095 in FY 2021, with a further reduction to 1,887 in FY 2022. 
Many State programs have consolidated vending locations due to shifting 
building populations in Federal and State buildings.

Randolph-Sheppard Financial Relief and Restoration Payments (FRRP) Appropriation  
Recognizing the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on Randolph-Sheppard vendors,  
in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021, Division H, Title III, Section 318 (Section 
318), Congress directed ED to make $20 million available for the purpose of making 
grants to SLAs designated to administer the Randolph-Sheppard Act. Section 318 
directed ED to make grants to SLAs in the same proportion as the number of blind 
vendors operating a vending facility in a State as compared to the number of blind 
vendors operating a vending facility in all the States on September 30, 2019. By 
September 30, 2022, the SLAs used these grants: 
 
• to make financial relief and restoration payments to offset losses of blind vendors 
that occurred during calendar year 2020 and which were not otherwise 
compensated; and 
• for other purposes authorized under 34 C.F.R. § 395.9, but only to the extent any 
funds remained after the SLA made financial relief and restoration payments to 
blind vendors to cover their losses incurred in calendar year 2020. 
 
According to OMB control No. 1820-0699, Randolph-Sheppard FRRP Appropriation 
Final Performance Report, submitted by SLAs,1,309 blind vendors received 
$18,827,888 of the appropriated funds to help offset losses incurred due to the COVID-
19 pandemic. After the blind vendors were fully compensated, an additional $1,101,216 
helped support vending programs by being retained for set-aside fund purposes. In 
total, approximately 99.6 percent of appropriated funds were used to provide relief to 
vendors directly or to support program recovery from the pandemic. Eight States used 
$69,280 of the appropriated funds to cover administrative costs of the FRRP grants. 
Lastly, $1,616 was returned to the U.S. Department of Treasury as unexpended funds. 
 
Arbitrations 
 
Under sections 107d-1, and 107d-2 of the Act, ED convenes arbitration panels to 
arbitrate disputes between either a blind vendor and SLA, or an SLA and Federal 
agency. ED uses the interim Policies and Procedures for Convening an Arbitration 
Panel3 to guide its and the arbitration panels in carrying out the arbitration process 
under the Act.   
 
 
3 ED’s interim policies and procedures for carrying out its responsibilities to convene arbitration panels 
under the Randolph-Sheppard Act can be found at the following website:  
https://rsa.ed.gov/about/programs/randolph-sheppard-vending-facility-program/decisions-of-arbitration-
panels

The Act provides that each party to the dispute appoints their own panel member, and 
the two panel members choose a third neutral panel member, who then serves as the 
Chair of the panel. After ED convenes and pays the costs of the hearing, it has no 
further involvement with the process. The panel decision is considered a final agency 
decision of ED for the purposes of any appeal and is  subject to judicial review in Federal 
district court under the Administrative Procedure Act. 
In FY 2021, ED received three new requests for arbitrations, and there was a total 
of 29 open cases in various stages of the arbitration process at that time— from 
requests for arbitration to awaiting final decisions from arbitration panels. By the 
end of FY 2021, there were 22 open cases as a result of the closure or settlement 
of seven cases. In FY 2022, ED received nine new requests for arbitrations, and by 
the end of FY 2022, there were 20 open cases given the closure or settlement of 
11 cases. 
 
There are many reasons why a case may take considerable time moving through the 
arbitration process. For example, the parties may choose to work on settling the dispute 
on their own; they may have difficulties in finding a panel member to serve on their 
behalf, or the Chair they choose may not agree to serve, requiring additional time to 
select another Chair. Once a case is fully appointed, the panel schedule is largely 
beyond control of ED. 
 
An arbitration program specialist in RSA closely monitors requests and provides parties 
pursuing arbitration with acknowledgement and convening letters, generally on a first in, 
first out basis, unless the arbitration request presents novel legal issues or other 
extenuating circumstances. By the end of FY 2022, due to close monitoring, increased 
staffing, and support from OSERS leadership and ED’s Office of the General Counsel, 
all cases are actively moving through the arbitration process. 
Conflict Resolution 
Given the significant resources expended by ED and program stakeholders on 
arbitrations, RSA instituted a conflict resolution (CR) initiative in FY 2019, creating a 
means by which parties could receive neutral facilitation from RSA. Blind vendors, 
including their advocates, SLAs, and Federal agencies, who find themselves at an 
impasse regarding aspects, issues, and potential violations of the Randolph-Sheppard 
Act and the implementing regulations are given the opportunity for a CR meeting. 
 
The objectives of this effort are to encourage resolution of issues by the parties in the 
shortest time and with a minimum of resources, to develop best practices in dealing 
with issues before they disrupt the administration of the program, and to increase 
respect and collaboration between and among blind licensees, SLAs, and Federal 
agencies.

In FYs 2019 through 2022, in eight circumstances, CR was initiated, including CR 
meetings in advance of a party filing a complaint. Seven of those meetings were 
successful in reaching agreements that resulted in the mutual dismissal of the 
arbitration cases or prevented a complaint from being filed. One case was referred to 
RSA’s State Monitoring and Program Improvement Division for technical assistance 
related to a fiscal issue. 
 
Review of SLA State Rules and Regulations 
 
The Act and its implementing regulations require SLAs to provide their rules, 
regulations, policies, procedures, and related documents to RSA for review and 
approval before they can be used in the State’s vending facility program. These rules, 
regulations, policies, procedures and related documents include: 
• The application for designation as an SLA (34 C.F.R. §§ 395.2 and 395.3); 
• The State rules and regulations affecting the administration and operation of the 
State’s vending facility program, as well as revisions to the State rules and 
regulations (34 C.F.R. § 395.4); 
• A prior determination by the Secretary that the percentage to be assessed 
against vendor net proceeds to be used as set-aside funds is reasonable, if an 
SLA wishes to require a set-aside assessment from blind vendors, (34 C.F.R. § 
395.9(a)); 
• Changes to State policies on the setting aside of funds (34 C.F.R. § 
395.3(a)(11)(iii)); 
• Changes in the legal authority of the SLA (34 C.F.R. § 395.3(a)(11)(iii); 
• Changes to blind vendor operating agreements (34 C.F.R. § 395.3(a)(11)(iii)); 
• Changes to arrangements for carrying general liability and product liability 
insurance (34 C.F.R. § 395.3(a)(11)(iii); and  
• Changes to contractual arrangements for the furnishing of services by a nominee 
(34 C.F.R. § 395.3(a)(11)(iii). 
 
ED received new submissions of State rules and regulations, vendor operating 
agreements, and an expedited policy request for review during FYs 2021 and 2022.  
The status of those reviews follows: 
• ED returned three State rules’ packages with letters of approval; 
• ED returned two operating agreements with letters of approval; and 
• ED approved a policy change in operation due to the COVID-19 pandemic. 
 
During this time period, ED also returned six rules’ packages with comments, informing 
the submitters that further information was needed for approval, and by the end of FY

2022, those packages had not been re-submitted. 
U.S AbilityOne Commission 
 
The Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) Between the U.S. AbilityOne Commission 
(AbilityOne) and RSA was finalized on January 19, 20214, for the purpose of increasing 
communication and understanding between the agencies regarding which opportunities 
are appropriate for persons who are blind to operate vending facilities under the 
Randolph-Sheppard Act and which are appropriate for placement on the Procurement 
List under the Javits-Wagner-O’Day (JWOD) Act. AbilityOne and RSA met regularly to 
develop definitions under the MOU and held a training that involved both AbilityOne and 
RSA stakeholders on August 26, 2021, to provide an overview of the Randolph-
Sheppard Vending Facility and AbilityOne programs and details related to the MOU. 
One critical component of the MOU was a protocol that AbilityOne would follow to 
inform RSA when it was provided notice of a contract requirement that might implicate a 
Randolph-Sheppard opportunity. RSA would then refer the contract opportunity to the 
appropriate SLA to determine if it wanted to pursue the opportunity under the Randolph-
Sheppard priority. The MOU and its protocol have significantly improved how 
stakeholders under both the Randolph-Sheppard Act and the JWOD Act are notified of 
and pursue opportunities in their respective programs.   
Technical Assistance 
Technical Assistance Circulars 
RSA routinely issues sub-regulatory guidance and provides technical assistance to blind 
vendors, SLAs, Federal agencies, and other stakeholders to support the management 
and operation of the vending facility program. During FYs 2021 and 2022, RSA 
published three technical assistance circulars (TACs)5 related to Randolph-Sheppard 
Act activities: 
 
• TAC 21-01 (December 15, 2020). Active Participation of Elected Committees of 
Blind Vendors with State licensing agencies in the Randolph-Sheppard Vending 
Facility Program: The Randolph-Sheppard Act requires SLAs to actively 
participate in major administrative decisions affecting the State’s program.

However, the Randolph-Sheppard Act and its implementing regulations do not 
define active participation. In order to promote consistency and uniformity, RSA 
issued this circular to provide guidance on the types of activities subject to active 
participation.  
• TAC 21-02 (June 7, 2021). The Application of the Randolph-Sheppard Act Priority 
for Blind Vendors to the Operation of Vending Machines on Federal Property and 
the Use of Third-Party Vendors on that Property: The guidance in this TAC 
describes the requirements in the Randolph-Sheppard Act and its implementing 
regulations that provide a priority for blind licensees to operate vending facilities, 
including vending machines, on Federal property. It further explains that the 
Federal agency may choose to contract with a third-party vendor to operate the 
facility if a licensed blind vendor is not available to operate the vending facility, 
but the Federal agency is obligated to share the vending machine income with 
the SLA.  
• TAC 22-01 (December 3, 2021). RSA Approval of State Rules: The guidance in 
this TAC describes how the requirements in the Randolph-Sheppard Act and its 
implementing regulations require SLAs to issue State rules and regulations 
affecting the operation of their BEPs in their States and require RSA to review 
and approve those rules. The TAC further provides examples of recurring issues 
that RSA has experienced in its review of State rules so that States can consider 
how to address these issues before submitting their rules for review. 
Other Technical Assistance 
• RSA-15 data collection training: ED uses the RSA-15 data collection instrument 
to gather annual data on the vending facilities program. In recent years, common 
errors have been made during the submission of these reports; therefore, RSA 
provided training on commonly identified errors to help streamline the submission 
and approval of these reports. 
• S LA/Elected Committee of Blind Vendors teleconferences: RSA established 
monthly or bi-monthly calls beginning in FY 2020 through FY 2022 with all 51 
SLAs by holding calls with representatives of three or four SLAs at a time and 
their respective Elected Committee of Blind Vendors on separate calls. These 
calls provided RSA with an opportunity to provide technical assistance to 
program stakeholders and allowed for collaboration among different States 
related to issues, concerns, and strategies for improving the operation of vending 
facilities. These calls laid the groundwork for the Randolph-Sheppard Act 
innovative practices regional calls being conducted by RSA in FY 2023.  
• Presentations to Stakeholders: RSA has been active in participating in Randolph-
Sheppard Act stakeholder meetings and conferences. Even during the pandemic 
RSA was able to use virtual options to participate in conferences held by the 
National Association of Blind Merchants (NABM), Randolph-Sheppard Vendors of 
America, and the National Council of State Agencies for the Blind. These 
conferences provided an opportunity to address the concerns of program 
stakeholders as well as to discuss the TACs mentioned above in greater detail.

• General Services Administration (GSA) and the Randolph-Sheppard Act 
Workgroup: In FY 2021, in collaboration with GSA, RSA convened a workgroup 
consisting of three SLA directors, representatives of NABM and NCSAB to 
collaborate on addressing issues of concern with vending facility operation at 
GSA locations. The workgroup developed a permit template to be used by GSA 
when new vending facilities are established, and it created a protocol to increase 
communication between GSA and SLAs when issues arise concerning vending 
facilities.

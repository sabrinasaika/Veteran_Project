Thank you for your letter dated March 31, 2016, to Secretary King at the U.S. Department of Education (ED). In your letter, you requested guidance from ED, as the Federal agency responsible with interpreting the Randolph-Sheppard Act (R-S Act) and administering the program nationwide, to help resolve an apparent national dispute between licensed blind vendors, who are part of the Randolph-Sheppard Program, and the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA). Specifically, you have expressed some concerns regarding how the R-S Act applies to VA properties and the operation of the Veterans' Canteen Service (VCS).
As a general matter, we can respond to the questions in terms of the R-S Act, but, given that the VA administers the VCS, we are not in the position to interpret that statutory authority. The court in Minnesota v. Riley, 18 F. 3d (8th. Cir 1994), however, provides a clear analysis of the relationship between the R-S Act and the VCS authorizing statute as follows:
Contrary to the VCS's claim, it is entirely possible for the Randolph-Sheppard Act and the Veterans' Canteen Act to co-exist in harmony. Although the Veterans' Canteen Act empowers the VCS to operate canteens on VA property, nothing in the Act authorizes the VCS to exercise this statutory control over Randolph Sheppard vendors who also operate on VA property. Because blind vendors operate vending facilities under the Randolph-Sheppard Act and the DOE regulations, the blind vendor's operation is neither a VCS canteen nor subject to the Veterans' Canteen Act and the VCS's regulations. Indeed, to allow the VCS to operate independently of the Randolph-Sheppard Act, Congress exempted the VCS's vending machines from the income share provisions of the Randolph­ Sheppard Act.
Thus, given this language, ED has always taken the position that there is nothing in the VCS statute that would supersede the priority in the R-S Act. Our responses to your specific questions area as follows:

I. Does the Randolph-Sheppard priority apply to properties owned or controlled by the Department of Veterans Affairs?
Section 107(b) of the R-S Act contains the priority for blind vendors on Federal property. There is nothing in this provision or elsewhere in the R-S Act that exempts properties owned or controlled by the VA from complying with this priority.
Does the Department of Veterans Affairs have a legal obligation to provide a satisfactory site in all buildings constructed or renovated after January I, 1975, as required by 34 CFR 395.31?
The R-S Act states:
Since January I, 1975, section 107a(d)(l) of the R-S Act and 34 CFR 395.31 have required all Federal agencies to acquire or otherwise occupy buildings that have "a satisfactory site or sites" to locate a vending facility for a blind vendor. Therefore, the VA does have a legal obligation to provide a satisfactory site in all buildings constructed, purchased, or renovated after January I, 1975.
If a new facility is being constructed and the Veterans Canteen Service elects to provide vending facility services, does that relieve the VA of its responsibility to provide a satisfactory site for a blind vendor?
There is nothing in the R-S Act that relieves the VA of its legal obligation to provide a satisfactory site or sites for a vending facility for a blind vendor where the VA has chosen to also have services provided by the Veterans Canteen Services.
If a "satisfactory site" is being provided to the SLA for a licensed blind vendor and the Veterans Canteen Service plans to operate on the same premises, does the Canteen Service get first choice of location and type of service it wishes to provide and then dictate to the SLA what type of service it will be allowed to provide?
According to section 107a(c) of the R-S Act, SLAs "select a location for such facility and the type of facility to be provided" with the approval of the Federal property manager.
Therefore, although the VA must approve of the SLA's determination of location and type of facility, it does not dictate location and type of service that will be allowed.
If a Federal entity occupies a building with fewer than I 00 employees and/or 15,000 square feet and it wants vending facility services, does the Randolph-Sheppard priority apply?
The Randolph-Sheppard priority applies to all Federal property. The satisfactory site provision in section 395.31 of the regulations, to which the question refers, does not control when the priority applies but rather under what conditions a Federal agency must provide a satisfactory site for a vending facility. Satisfactory site is defined in section 395.1 to impose certain minimum requirements, including the size of the space that must be made available to an SLA to provide vending and the provision of sufficient utilities to operate the facility. Thus, if a Federal property does not meet the minimum requirements in section 395.31, the property manager is under no obligation to meet the satisfactory site provisions. If an SLA, however, wishes to submit a permit to operate a vending facility on a property that is not required to meet the satisfactory site provisions, the SLA may do so but cannot require the agency to provide a minimum amount of space or to ensure that adequate utilities are available. Thus, in answer to your concern that the VA maintains that it "can do what it wants" just because there are fewer than 100 employees or 15,000 square feet, there is no basis in the R-S Act for this position.
If the Canteen Service contracts with a third party to provide vending service, does the exemption to share income found at 20 U.S.C. 107d-3(d) and 34 CFR 395.32(i) still apply?
Given that a third-party contractor carries out functions that a contracting agency is already authorized to perform, we do not interpret the R-S Act as excluding third-party VCS contracts from the income sharing exemption under the R-S Act. We believe, however, that before a final determination on this issue can be reached, the VCS statute should be reviewed to determine whether there is anything in that statute that has a bearing on this issue, such as whether that statute addresses the authority of the VCS to contract out vending services. Again, given that we do not administer the VCS, it would be best for the VA to make this determination.
If an SLA is unable to resolve a disagreement with the VA and files for arbitration, is the decision of the panel binding on the VA, or does it have the option of ignoring the decision if it disagrees with it?
Section 107d-2(b)(2)(c) of the R- S Act states that when an arbitration panel finds that a Federal agency has violated a requirement in the R-S Act or regulations, the agency "shall cause such acts or practices to be terminated promptly and shall take such other action as may be necessary to carry out the decision of the panel."
Therefore, while a Federal agency may have some discretion as to what action is necessary to carry out a panel's decision, it clearly is required to stop the acts or practices that gave rise to the situation and implement corrective action. Based on the foregoing, it's clear that the VA does not
have an option to ignore an arbitration panel's decision that there is a violation of the R-S Act if it disagrees with that determination.
Can you tell me if your Department was consulted prior to implementation of the VA's recently revised policy directive in regards to the Randolph-Sheppard priority on VA properties?
No. To my knowledge, the Department of Education was not consulted.
Randolph-Sheppard advocates have asked that the Department of Veterans Affairs and the Department of Education develop a memorandum of understanding as to the implementation of the Randolph-Sheppard Act on VA controlled properties. Would your Department see any benefit to such an MOU? Are there other remedies that you would suggest so that we can eliminate the need for expensive litigation and/or further Congressional involvement?
ED would welcome the opportunity to work with the VA to increase opportunities for blind individuals as required by the R-S Act. We are happy to explore any options that will help ensure that the VA is in compliance with the R-S Act.

About this Event:
This training will highlight findings from a mixed methods study of state vocational rehabilitation (SVR) directors (n=40) who completed an online survey that examined recruitment and retention problems of SVR counselors. A follow-up semi-structured interview with about half of this sample (n=19) was also conducted to further elucidate these problems and identify strategies that individual states have implemented to address them. The presentation will focus more on 12 strategies recommended to facilitate retention of SVR counselors. Attendees will have opportunity to provide reaction and comment on these strategies.
Learning Objectives:
Examine the major barriers confronting the public vocational rehabilitation program in recruiting and retaining qualified rehabilitation counselors.
Evaluate the usefulness of 12 recommended strategies to facilitate retention of state vocational rehabilitation counselors.

Meet Our Presenter!
James T. Herbert
,
Ph.D.
Professor of Rehabilitation and Human Services
The Pennsylvania State University
Dr. James T. Herbert has worked and studied in the rehabilitation field for nearly 50 years, primarily as an academician and, in that capacity, as Professor-in-charge of the Rehabilitation and Human Services Program for most of his 36 years at the Pennsylvania State University. He is the principal or co-author of over 80 juried articles and book chapters and about 100 juried professional presentations. His main research areas have examined recruitment, retention and clinical supervision practices of state vocational rehabilitation counselors and interventions to improve retention of college students with disabilities. Dr. Herbert has received several national awards for his research contributions from the American Rehabilitation Counseling Association, the National Council on Rehabilitation Education and the National Institute on Disability, Independent Living, and Rehabilitation Research (NIDILRR). He currently serves as Principal Investigator of a NIDILRR research grant, “Evaluating the Effectiveness of the Rehabilitation Services Administration (RSA) Scholarship Program” which partially provided funds to support this study.

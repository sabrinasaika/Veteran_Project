RE:  Revised Federal Fiscal Year 2021 Maintenance of Effort Deficit 
 
Dear Kathryn Hayfield and R. L. Mitchell: 
On July 24, 2023, the Rehabilitation Services Administration (RSA) issued a letter to the State of 
Virginia (Virginia) regarding its failure to satisfy the maintenance of effort (MOE) requirement 
under the State Vocational Rehabilitation Services (VR) program during Federal fiscal year 
(FFY) 2021 and, as a result, of the need to reduce the Virginia Department for the Blind and 
Vision Impaired’s (DBVI) grant for FFY 2024 by $2,134,313.00, the amount of the MOE 
shortfall. RSA calculated the MOE shortfall based on information contained in the Federal 
Financial Reports (SF-425/RSA-17) submitted by DBVI and the Virginia Department for Aging 
and Rehabilitative Services (DARS) at the time the letter was issued. 
 
After receiving RSA’s letter, DBVI notified RSA of potential errors in DBVI’s FFY 2019 SF-
425 report calculations and construction expenditures assigned to the grant award. DBVI 
submitted supporting documentation of the changes requested to RSA prior to any revisions 
being made to the affected financial reports. After reviewing the supporting documentation, RSA 
determined that the State’s and agency’s accounting records supported the requested revisions. 
DBVI then entered the corrections to the financial reports noted above via the RSA Management 
Information System.  
 
Based on the corrections made, DBVI did not incur an FFY 2021 MOE deficit. Therefore, there 
will be no reduction to DBVI’s FFY 2024 VR award to address a MOE shortfall. See attached 
table titled “Revised MOE Analysis for FFY 2021” for details.

Page 2 – Hayfield and Mitchell 
 
DBVI’s internal control processes failed to identify the reporting error until the agency was 
notified of the MOE deficit by RSA. Internal controls must provide for the “reliability of 
reporting for internal and external use” (34 C.F.R. § 361.12). In the VR services portion of the 
Unified or Combined State Plan, DBVI assured RSA that it would employ methods of 
administration to ensure the proper and efficient administration of the VR program. These 
methods of administration (i.e., the agency’s internal controls) must include procedures to ensure 
accurate data collection and financial accountability. Repeated submission of inaccurate 
reporting may lead to enforcement actions.

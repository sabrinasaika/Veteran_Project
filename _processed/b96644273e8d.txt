INTRODUCTION
This matter is being brought before this tripartite panel pursuant to Randolph-Sheppard Act, 20 U.S.C. 107d-l(a) and 107d-2 and the implementing regulations at 34 CFR 395.13.
Calvette Brown (hereinafter referred to as "Petitioner") and the Illinois Department of Human Services, Division of Rehabilitation Services (hereinafter referred to as "Respondent") appeared at a hearing on November 16, 2015, at the offices of Erbacci & Cerone, Ltd., located at 770 Lee Street, Des Plaines, Illinois 60016, commencing at 9:00 a.m. At the hearing both parties acknowledged that they were given a full and fair opportunity to present their respective positions. Illinois Department of Human Services, Division of Rehabilitation Services was represented by Counsel, Calvette Brown was Pro Se.
ISSUE
Whether 1) the hearing officer on the first bid overstepped his authority by ordering a rebid; 2) the panelists on the second bid process were biased against Petitioner; and 3) the Department erred in terminating Petitioner's "contract" for the Palatine Vending Facility.
RELEVANT LAW
1) Randolph-Sheppard Act, 20 U.S.C. Sections 107d-l(a) and 107d-2;
2) The Regulations of the Act, 34 C.F.R. 395.13;
3) 89 Ill. Administrative Code Section 650.90
4) American Arbitration Association, Rule 16
STATEMENT OF FACTS
Mrs. Calvette Brown, Petitioner has been operating a facility in Carol Stream for many years. She is a business owner and participant in the Business Enterprise Program for the Blind Department of Human Services, ("BEPB").
On December 18, 2008, BEPB issued a bid announcement for the property in question here at the Palatine Facilities #483 and 496, which were being merged.
After the bid closed Mrs. Brown was awarded the combined Facility #496 because she was the most successful bidder, i.e., she received the highest cumulative scores as tallied by the four panel members of 86.25 on February 10, 2009. She was awarded the contract for Palatine Facility.
She had been operating Facility #496 on a service agreement until the bid process could be completed.
On February 20, 2009, one of the unsuccessful bidders, Marco Giannotti who scored 80 and came in second filed a grievance stating that the selection process was not impartial or biased.
A final decision was made on June 4, 2009, by Impartial Hearing Officer, Michael W. Becher that the first selection process was not impartial or objective and ordered that the scoring of June 28, 2009, was invalidated and shall be redone through another interview process. He set the Selection Committee as the three original members but stated that Ms. Shanks would not sit in on the re-bid. Another Vocational Rehabilitation ("VR") Counselor should be appointed.
On June 9, 2009, Mrs. Brown filed an appeal.
On July 2, 2009, there was a new interview for the re-bid and on July 17, 2009, Giannotti was awarded the Palatine Facility because he scored highest 83.75, Grievant scored 82 and a third bidder scored 77.
All the parties know all of the facts and time tables regarding this case from December 18, 2008 through January 17, 2014. I see no need to restate the occurrences and their respective dates since none of these facts seem to be in dispute.
PETITIONER'S ARGUMENT
State Regulations 89 Ill. Administrative Code Section 650.90 and Federal Regulations 34 CFR Rules governing Randolph-Sheppard Act for the blind clearly provide only one provision for rebidding facilities, if no one scores above 60%. Petitioner did score well over 60% on the first bid.
State and Federal Regulations guiding the Randolph-Sheppard Act orders no panel member shall serve with conflicts of interest.
Donald Anderson wrote a passionate article which showed bias. The hearing officer clearly stated that anyone with a conflict of interest should recuse themselves from the second bid process. Anderson didn't recuse himself because he said he was not asked to do so.
When Petitioner was the successful bidder on the first bid she received a contract from the combined location Facility #496. Giannotti filed a grievance with the help of one panel member Mr. Anderson. Mr. Raven called Petitioner and told her they were terminating her contract and rebidding the site because Giannotti accused one of the panel members of spiking the score on the first bid. Petitioner did not grieve Giannotti 's grievance, but grieved the termination of her contract which had been awarded to her.
At the hearing, Mr. Pulliam testified there was a "glitch" in the process. (Tr. Pg. 237) Pulliam testified to his knowledge no other Vocational Rehabilitation Counselor panel member had ever been removed just this one time. (Tr. Pg. 220) In 20 years this was the only glitch in the process.
Mr. Anderson wrote his article (Petitioner Exhibit 8) after he learned Petitioner filed her grievance and letter testified to writing the article (Respondent Exhibit 21).
After writing that article Mr. Anderson should have not have been allowed to serve on a bid process.
Mr. Cunningham testified that he did ask Ms. Shanks if there were any conflicts of interest, but not Mr. Anderson or Mr. Dwyer.
Respondent Exhibit 8, the Federal Randolph Sheppard Act states that a location is temporary until it is bided out and a permanent agreement is signed. Petitioner did nothing to violate any of the rules before, during or after the bid process.
Raven Pulliam first gave testimony that Petitioner was not inventoried in but later said she was. (Tr. Pgs. 200-201)
Petitioner is asking that this matter be reviewed under the Randolph-Sheppard Act under Regulations 34 CFR Part 395 all before 2010 since this action occurred in 2008 and 2009.
Petitioner is seeking damages from 2008-2012 (4) four years at $40,000 per year, plus 2012-2015 three (3) years at $80,000 per year, plus attorney's fees of $30,000 plus punitive damages. Petitioner also seeks the second bid be voided and the results of the first bid be reinstated.
Thus the main arguments of Petitioner are as follows:
1) The hearing officer on the first bid overstepped his authority by ordering a rebid;
2) The panelists on the second bid process were biased against her;
3) The Department erred in terminating her "contract" for the Palatine Facility.
RESPONDENT'S ARGUMENT
It is undisputed that the scoring on the first bid was not objective, and therefore a new bid process was required under Illinois Law. One of the members of the original panel, Gina Shanks, scored the petitioner as the only qualified applicant with a score of 88 while scoring the other three bidders 55, 58 and 57. (Respondent Exhibit 1, Pg. 6) These are under the 60% threshold to win a bid. 89 Ill. Admin. Code Section 650.90( e) (2009). (Respondent Exhibit 18) The other three panelists' scoring of all applicants, above the 60%.
This scoring is what prompted the hearing officer to conclude that the bid process was not objective on its face and order a re-bid. Matt Cunningham testified that such a wide range difference in scoring was "abnormal". (Tr. Pg. 13 7) In 2009 he stated that a 10 to 15 point variance in scores would be acceptable. (Tr. Pg. 140) (Respondent Exhibit 12, Pg. 6)
The hearing officer ordered a new bid process without Ms. Shanks, therefore, Petitioner failed to demonstrate that the hearing officer erred in ordering new bid process.
The Petitioner misinterpreted the Administrative Code when she says "no new bids" may occur when there is a successful bidder over 60%. That reading is contrary to the Code and creates a rule that was not promulgated by the Department and the legislative Joint Committee on Administrative Rules ("JCAR") as was 89 Ill. Administrative Code 650.90.
It is also contrary to Illinois Law which requires the bid process to be objective. The lack of objectivity on the first bid was a proper and lawful reason for a re-bid.
The second bid process did not contain any reversible error or violations of Petitioners due process rights. Petitioner failed to demonstrate any bias by the panelists, Janet Sherburne and Matt Cunningham who scored Mrs. Brown higher than the other panelists.
The requirement for an impartial juror is met when the prospective juror has given unequivocal assurances deemed credible by the judge that he or she can remain impartial. US. v. Taylor 777 F. 3d 434, 441 (?111 Circuit 2015).
Mr. Anderson in fact raised his score for Mrs. Brown by three (3) points on the second bid. (Respondent Exhibit 16) Mr. Anderson's letter was not admitted into evidence at the hearing and should not be considered now.
Ms. Sherburne was an appropriate Panel member because she was qualified having served on panels before and having over forty years experience with the IDHS Bureau of Blind Services. In fact she scored Petitioner higher than the other two applicants.
Petitioner has been afforded her due process since the Department has allowed her to file multiple appeals and granting her hearings on each one. (Respondent Exhibits 5 and 20; Respondent Exhibit 6)
The cancellation of Petitioners service agreement was proper because she only had a temporary service agreement for a portion of the facility. Even though Petitioner was awarded the entire facility as a result of the first bid and she was sent an agreement signed by the Director of Vocational Rehabilitation Services she never took over the entire facility. She was never inventoried out of Carol Stream Facility and inventoried into Palatine Facility. Even if the February 3, 2009, Vendor agreement is the controlling agreement DHS had a right to terminate the agreement because Petitioner failed to comply with Section A.2 as she did not pay back the working capital within 12 months and the first bid awarding the contract was not objective as required by Illinois Law.
Respondent also argued that one of the arbitrators, Mr. Brown should have been disqualified as an Arbitrator under Rule 16 of the American Arbitration Association. He is Petitioner's husband and has a pecuniary interest in this case and has the appearance of impropriety.
DECISION
The issues in this case are:
1) Whether the hearing officer in Case 09 VFPB 001 ("Marco Giannotti") exceeded his authority in ordering a new selection process for the bidding of the Palatine Facility resulting in the denial of Calvetter Brown's due process rights;
2) Whether the Department erred in awarding the facility to another applicant due to the re-bid process and;
3) Whether the removal of Calvette Brown from the Palatine Facility was proper.
On October 21, 2008, Petitioner entered into a BEPB Service Agreement with the DHS/DRS to operate Facility #496. This Service Agreement was a month to month agreement.
She operated this Facility all through the bid process until November 30, 2012, when she was removed and her Service Agreement was terminated. Mr. Giannotti was then awarded the combined Facilities #483 and #496.
On November 15, 2012, Petitioner requested a hearing challenging her removal from Facility #496. Her case was heard and dismissed and she has filed for arbitration on all these issues before us.
In DHS Exhibit 18, Procedure for Selection of Vendors (DRS/BBS/VFP) dated February 9, 1991, Pg. 15 and 16( c) there is no minimum score other than 1 point minimum and a maximum of 100 points.
In the 89 Ill. Administrative Code, Chapter IV, Section 650.90, Subchapter C, there is a list of criteria by which the Selection Committee shall assess each applicant during the bidding process.
There is no mention of acceptable range to qualify other than to state that "the facility shall be awarded to the applicant who is most qualified for that specific facility with the highest rating above 60% based upon the selection process as described in Subsection (c)(2) of this Section." Section i states that a dissatisfied bidder may appeal the decision per 89 Ill. Administrative Code, Section 510 which Marco Giannotti did.
The hearing officer on May 14, 2009, found that Mr. Giannotti met the burden of proof and ordered a re-bid because the selection process was not impartial or objective. Mr. Giannotti was assisted in filing his appeal by one of the selection committee members who also sat on the re-bid process.
Although a re-bid may have been justified the hearing officer erred in only replacing Ms. Shanks. In viewing the scores of other panelists there are variances of 25 and 26 points from bidder to bidder. Also one of the panelists assisted Mr. Giannotti in preparing his appeal. That fact alone should have disqualified that panelist from sitting on the re-bid. To insure there was no perception of impropriety or bias every panelist should have been removed and a complete new re-bid procedure should have taken place. The Department should also review the appeal process and Arbitration Clause because the entire procedure takes way to long. An undue burden and injustice is being given to all parties involved including the department. From 2008 until 2016 is ludicrous.
Finally, I ruled at the hearing that if there was an issue as to whether Gregory Brown should have been allowed to act as an Arbitrator in this case it should have been raised by the Department as soon as it became aware of who Mrs. Brown had selected. They waived any right to remove him by waiting until the hearing date.
Also the Arbitrators did not take into consideration at all Mr. Anderson's article since it was not allowed into evidence.
This case, a first of a kind in a few respects gave this Arbitrator much consternation. Hopefully a re-bid with an entirely new panel will set a precedent if this issue arises again.
The IDHS/RS knew that Mrs. Brown had filed for arbitration on this matter and should not have entered into a contract with Mr. Giannotti, but rather the facility should have continued to operate as it did before the first bid and after as a Service Agreement.
IT IS THEREFORE ORDERED:
There shall be a new bid process open to all eligible bidders to be screened as per the Randolph-Sheppard Regulations and Code with a selection Committee of four different panelists none of whom sat on the first or second bid process.
The procedure shall commence immediately after the parties receive this decision and shall be completed within sixty (60) days thereof.
This panel of arbitrators shall retain jurisdiction over this case until the process for the bid has been completed and the successful bidder has been presented with his or her contract.

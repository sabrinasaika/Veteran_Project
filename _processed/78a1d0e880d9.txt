This letter presents the Rehabilitation Services Administration’s (RSA) findings regarding 
the South Dakota Service to the Blind and Visually Impaired (SBVI) failure to substantially 
comply with the requirement to develop individualized plans for employment (IPEs) on a 
timely basis in Program Year (PY) 2022
1 under the State Vocational Rehabilitation (VR) 
program in accordance with section 102(b)(3)(F) of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 
(Rehabilitation Act). RSA made its determination of SBVI’s substantial non-compliance 
during its annual review, conducted in accordance with section 107(a) of the Rehabilitation 
Act, of Case Service Report (RSA-911)
2 data submitted by SBVI during PY 2022. This letter 
constitutes RSA’s report to SBVI of the annual review of PY 2022 data in accordance with 
section 107(a)(5) of the Rehabilitation Act. 
 
Pursuant to Section 107(a)(1) of the Rehabilitation Act, RSA must conduct an annual review 
of the VR program to ensure each State is substantially complying with the requirements of 
its Unified or Combined State Plan. In conducting the annual reviews, RSA must consider, 
among other things, reports submitted by the VR agencies (section 107(a)(2)(H) of the 
Rehabilitation Act). In conducting the annual reviews, RSA must examine, among other 
areas, the provision of VR services (section 107(a)(4)(B) of the Rehabilitation Act). As 
recipients of Federal funds, VR agencies must collect and report certain individual-level

programmatic data about VR program participants pursuant to sections 101(a)(10) and 106 of

Section 102(b)(3)(F) of the Rehabilitation Act requires SBVI to develop the IPE for each 
individual determined eligible “as soon as possible, but not later than a deadline of 90 days 
after the date of the determination of eligibility,” unless SBVI and the individual agree to an 
extension of a specific period of time. According to SBVI’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data, SBVI 
developed a total of 101 IPEs for eligible individuals with disabilities. Of these, SBVI 
developed 89 IPEs within 90 days of the eligibility determination (or 88.1 percent) and 
developed 12 IPEs beyond 90 days of the eligibility determination (or 11.9 percent).
3 
Therefore, RSA’s annual review of SBVI’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data demonstrate the VR 
agency failed to substantially comply with the requirement of section 102(b)(3)(F) of the 
Rehabilitation Act because SBVI did not develop IPEs for 11.9 percent of its eligible 
individuals that year within the required 90-day period. 
 
Section 101(a)(1)(A) of the Rehabilitation Act requires States to submit a Unified or 
Combined State Plan in accordance with section 102 of the Workforce Innovation and 
Opportunity Act in order to receive VR funding. The State Plan must contain a VR services 
portion that satisfies the requirements of the VR program (34 C.F.R. § 361.10(a)(2)), 
including those pertaining to IPE development. The VR services portion of the State Plan 
must contain an assurance that IPEs, meeting the requirements of section 102(b), will be 
developed and implemented in a timely manner for eligible individuals (section 101(a)(9) of 
the Rehabilitation Act). In addition to this specific assurance, among others, each VR agency 
submitting the VR services portion of the State Plan signs an assurance that the VR agency 
will satisfy all Federal requirements governing the VR program, which would include those 
pertaining to the timeline for IPE development. Moreover, as a recipient of Federal funds, 
each VR agency, must develop internal controls to ensure the VR agency complies with all 
Federal requirements and implements corrective actions when it identifies instances of non-
compliance (2 C.F.R. § 200.303(a) and (d)). By signing the VR services portions of their 
State Plan and accepting VR grant funds, SBVI certified that it would administer and operate 
the VR program in accordance with the State Plan, Rehabilitation Act, and applicable Federal 
regulations governing the VR program. As demonstrated by SBVI’s PY 2022 RSA-911 data 
and described above, the VR agency fell short of satisfying the Federal requirement 
governing the timeline for IPE development that year.

In accordance with Section 107(b)(2) of the Rehabilitation Act, the Commissioner requires 
SBVI to develop and implement a corrective action plan (CAP) that— 
 
• Addresses the root cause of the VR agency’s noncompliance with the requirement; 
• Contains a solid, workable plan for action; 
• Contains a timeline for implementation that would be completed by end of PY 2024 
(i.e., June 30, 2025); 
• Can be supported by RSA-911 quarterly reports to show the VR agency has 
maintained compliance for at least two consecutive quarters;  
• Provides a methodology that allows the VR agency to review internally whether the 
corrective actions have resolved the root cause of the noncompliance with the 
requirement and to review whether the actions are consistent with the progress 
needed to ensure compliance; and 
• Ensures implementation of internal controls necessary for the VR agency to maintain 
compliance with the requirement on an ongoing basis. 
 
SBVI must submit the above-described CAP to their RSA State Liaison within 60 days of the 
date of issuance of this letter.
